## Evidence grades used

**VERIFIED** — confirmed by two or more independent sources. **SUPPORTED** — confirmed by one credible source, consistent with the record. **PROVISIONAL** — plausible, single-source or partially confirmed. **DISPUTED** — sources conflict; both stated. **UNRESOLVED** — could not be verified; listed in Gaps, never smoothed over.

## 1. The verified record

### Founding

- **1910:** Andrew "Rube" Foster, captain of the Chicago Leland Giants, wrestled legal control of the "Leland Giants" name from owner Frank Leland and split the club. That 1910 Leland Giants team — featuring Hall of Fame shortstop John Henry "Pop" Lloyd, outfielder Pete Hill, second baseman Grant "Home Run" Johnson, catcher Bruce Petway, and pitcher Frank Wickware — reportedly won 123 games and lost 6. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia/Chicago American Giants; BlackPast.org; AA Registry)
- **1911:** Foster renamed the club the "American Giants." **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; BlackPast.org)
- **1911 partnership:** Foster partnered with John M. Schorling — wealthy white tavern owner and son-in-law of White Sox owner Charles Comiskey — as co-owner. The team's ballpark was named Schorling Park after him. **[VERIFIED]** (BlackPast.org; Baseball-Reference BR Bullpen/John Schorling)
- **1910–1919 (independent era):** The American Giants won every recorded western Black baseball championship between 1910 and 1922 except 1916 (lost to the Indianapolis ABCs). **[SUPPORTED]** (Robert Edward Auctions archive, via SABR researcher Tom Hawthorn)

### Rube Foster — the founder

- **Born** September 17, 1879, in Calvert, Texas. **[VERIFIED]** (multiple sources)
- **Pitcher:** One of the dominant pitchers in pre-league Black baseball. His 1903 record with the Cuban X-Giants is reported as 54–1 (REA/SABR) or 51 of 55 starts (Royal Retros). **[DISPUTED on the exact figure; VERIFIED that he was the era's dominant Black pitcher]** The Chicago Defender called him "the best pitcher the race has ever known" in 1916. **[SUPPORTED]** (Medill Reports Chicago, citing Defender)
- **Nickname origin:** Earned the name "Rube" after outpitching white star Rube Waddell of the Philadelphia Athletics in an exhibition game (1902). **[SUPPORTED]** (Notable Sports Figures; Call to the Pen)
- **February 13, 1920:** Foster convened the founding meeting of the Negro National League at the Paseo YMCA in Kansas City. Eight charter franchises: the American Giants, Kansas City Monarchs, Detroit Stars, Indianapolis ABCs, St. Louis Giants, Dayton Marcos, Cuban Stars (West), and Chicago Giants. Foster became the league's first president and ran it from Chicago, personally underwriting much of its operating expense for its first six seasons. **[VERIFIED]** (Royal Retros; Medill Reports; Wikipedia)
- **The 1919 race riots** in Chicago were a catalyst: Black Chicago's demand for its own institutions — including its own league — sharpened after the violence. **[SUPPORTED]** (BlackPast.org)
- **May 26, 1925:** Foster was nearly asphyxiated by a gas leak in Indianapolis. He recovered physically, but his behavior grew erratic afterward. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia/Rube Foster)
- **1926:** Suffering serious delusions — including carrying a revolver everywhere and believing he was about to be called to pitch in the World Series — Foster was institutionalized midway through the season at the Illinois state asylum in Kankakee. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; NPR/WHRO)
- **Died** December 9, 1930, of a heart attack, age 51, never having recovered. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; Notable Sports Figures)
- **Funeral:** Thousands attended in Bronzeville, Chicago — "an overflow crowd of 3,000 people who stood in the snow and rain." His coffin was closed, attendees said, "at the usual hour a ballgame ends." He is interred at Lincoln Cemetery in Blue Island, Illinois. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia, citing contemporary accounts)
- **Hall of Fame:** Elected 1981 as a manager and pioneer. **[VERIFIED]**
- **Aftermath:** His widow, Sarah Watts Foster, was unfamiliar with his business arrangements and realized no benefits from his ventures. Schorling sold the team to white florist William E. Trimble in 1928. **[SUPPORTED]** (Notable Sports Figures)

### Championships

- **Negro National League pennants:** 1920, 1921, 1922 — three consecutive titles in the league's first three seasons. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; Royal Retros; AA Registry)
- **1920 season:** 51–19–2 (.722). Cristóbal Torriente led the team with a .393 average. **[SUPPORTED]** (Wikipedia/1920 Chicago American Giants season)
- **Colored World Series titles:** 1926 and 1927, both over the Bacharach Giants of Atlantic City (Eastern Colored League), both managed by Dave Malarcher after Foster's removal. 1926: 5–4–(2). 1927: 5–3–(1). Both played as best-of-nine series. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia/Negro World Series; AA Registry)
- **Later titles:** 1932 Negro Southern League pennant; 1933 (second) Negro National League; 1934 second-half championship in Gus Greenlee's NNL. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia)
- **1926 playoff heroics:** Willie Foster (Rube's younger half-brother) won 26 games in 1926, including 23 consecutive victories, then threw consecutive complete-game shutouts in a playoff doubleheader against the Kansas City Monarchs to send the Giants to the World Series. He followed with a 32–3 record in 1927. **[VERIFIED]** (Bhamwiki, citing Hall of Fame records; elected to Cooperstown 1996)

### Other stars

- **Ted "Double Duty" Radcliffe** (pitcher/catcher): American Giants debut 1929; also 1934, 1942–43; managed the club in 1943. Nicknamed by writer Damon Runyon in 1932 after catching one game and pitching the next against the New York Black Yankees. Moved to Chicago's South Side in 1919 as part of the Great Migration. Died in Chicago in 2005 at age 103; at 96, became the oldest man ever to appear in a professional game (one pitch for the Schaumburg Flyers, 1999). **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia/Ted Radcliffe; SABR; Encyclopedia of Alabama)
- **Dave Malarcher** ("Gentleman Dave"): Foster's protégé, took over on-field management in 1926, won the 1926 and 1927 World Series emphasizing Foster's pitching-and-defense pattern. **[VERIFIED]** (RetroSeasons; AA Registry)
- **Frank Wickware:** Threw a no-hitter on August 26, 1914, beating the Indianapolis ABCs 1–0 — walked the leadoff man, who was caught stealing, then retired 26 straight. **[SUPPORTED]** (Baseball-Reference BR Bullpen, citing John Holway's *Complete Book of Baseball's Negro Leagues*)

### Ballparks

- **Schorling's Park** (1911–1940): Formerly South Side Park (III), the White Sox's home 1900–1910. Located at 39th Street (now Pershing Road) and South Wentworth Avenue — 39th south/first base, Wentworth east/right field, Princeton west/third base, 38th north/left field. A few blocks south of the Comiskey Park sites, in the heart of the South Side. Renamed for co-owner John Schorling. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia/List of baseball parks in Chicago; deadballbaseball.com)
- **Capacity dispute:** Listed as 15,000 (Baseball-Reference BR Bullpen) and described as a "5,000-seat stadium" (AA Registry). **[DISPUTED — likely 15,000 total capacity vs. 5,000 seated; treat as unresolved pending a primary source]**
- **Destroyed** by fire on Christmas Day, 1940. **[VERIFIED]** (BR Bullpen)
- **Comiskey Park** (1941–1950): After the fire, the Giants shared the White Sox's park, playing when the Sox were on the road. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia)
- **Today:** The Schorling Park site is the Wentworth Gardens housing project. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia)
- **Earlier:** Before 1911, the predecessor Union Giants played at the first "Schorling's Park" (a.k.a. Auburn Park) at 79th and Wentworth. **[SUPPORTED]** (Wikipedia)

### Decline and end

- **Integration era:** After Jackie Robinson broke the color barrier in 1947, top Black talent was signed away to the majors. **[VERIFIED]**
- **Comiskey attendance** dwindled to the hundreds. **[SUPPORTED]** (Wikipedia)
- **Disbanded** by the 1955 season; revived in 1958, barnstorming through the South until 1961. **[SUPPORTED]** (Wikipedia; oursportscentral.com cites the Negro Leagues Baseball Museum calling them "the longest continuous franchise in the history of black baseball," founded 1910 through the 1950 season)
- **MLB recognition:** In December 2020, Major League Baseball announced it would incorporate Negro Leagues statistics (1920–1948) into its official records. Josh Gibson's .372 career average is now listed as MLB's all-time leader, ahead of Ty Cobb's .367. **[VERIFIED]** (Workers World; Dean's Team Chicago, citing CNN)
- **White Sox tributes:** The White Sox have worn American Giants replica uniforms on multiple occasions: July 1, 2007 (at Kansas City), July 26, 2008 (vs. Detroit), July 16, 2011 (Negro League weekend at Detroit). **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia)

## 2. What did it cost them

*The flagship question's second half. This is the medicine inside the candy.*

### The road

- Negro League players were barred from white hotels and restaurants across the country — North and South. They stayed in Black boardinghouses and private homes, ate where they could, and planned every stop in advance: which gas stations would serve them, which towns were safe after dark. **[VERIFIED]** (Workers World; WTTW documentary via Dean's Team Chicago: "They knew which boardinghouses they could stay in, what restaurants would accommodate them")
- Hank Aaron's account, widely cited: players lived on "a loaf of bread and some peanut butter" because restaurants wouldn't serve them; boardinghouse managers sometimes turned them away to "sleep in the field on your suitcases." **[SUPPORTED]** (NPR interview via WCLK/CFPublic, 2023)
- Travel was grueling even by the era's standards: the Homestead Grays covered 30,000 miles in a 179-game season in a cramped bus with no air-conditioning or restroom. A 15-year-old Roy Campanella rode 2,800 miles in 27 days with the Elite Giants in 1937. **[SUPPORTED]** (BoSox Injection, citing Neil Lanctot's *Campy*)
- The American Giants barnstormed the West Coast in winter and toured up through Portland, Seattle, and into Canada (Victoria, Vancouver) in the early spring — annual Vancouver stops 1913–1916. **[SUPPORTED]** (REA archive, via SABR researcher Tom Hawthorn)

### The economics

- Foster "played by major-league rules, paid major-league salaries (within the constraints of Black baseball economics), kept consistent schedules, and operated as a legitimate professional baseball business." **[SUPPORTED]** (Royal Retros) — but the parenthetical is doing heavy work: Black teams had no ballpark ownership (they rented), no radio money, no farm systems, and gate receipts were the entire business.
- Foster personally underwrote much of the NNL's operating expense for its first six seasons and took a cut of gate receipts from every club — while also reaching into his own wallet to keep struggling teams alive. **[SUPPORTED]** (Royal Retros; NPR/WHRO)
- After his death, his widow Sarah Watts Foster "was unfamiliar with his business arrangements and realized no benefits from his ventures." The man who built Black baseball's economy died without an estate to leave. **[SUPPORTED]** (Notable Sports Figures)
- The 1919 Chicago race riots — which killed dozens and displaced thousands of Black Chicagoans — were the immediate backdrop to the league's founding. Foster built the NNL in a city still smoking. **[SUPPORTED]** (BlackPast.org)

### The body and the mind

- Foster's collapse is the starkest cost in the dossier: a May 1925 gas-leak exposure, escalating paranoia (carrying a revolver everywhere), delusions of being called to pitch in the World Series, commitment to the Kankakee asylum in 1926 at the height of his powers, death in 1930 at 51 — never having seen the back-to-back championships his own rebuilt team won in 1926 and 1927. **[VERIFIED]**
- The league he founded collapsed in 1931, one year after his death and amid the Great Depression — "after several years of declining financial success and the absence of Foster's guiding hand." **[SUPPORTED]** (Notable Sports Figures)

### The erasure

- From 1920 to 1948, over 3,400 Black players were barred from the majors by a "gentlemen's agreement" — no written policy, just the owners' tacit pact dating to an 1867 vote. **[SUPPORTED]** (Workers World; Medill Reports, citing the Foster Legacy Foundation)
- Their statistics were not recognized by Major League Baseball until December 2020 — up to a century after the games were played. **[VERIFIED]**
- Josh Gibson, who never played a major-league inning, is now the career batting-average leader. The record book corrected itself 70+ years late. **[VERIFIED]**

## 3. The Chicago connection

### Neighborhoods

- **Bronzeville:** The team's community and Foster's own neighborhood. The Chicago Defender — founded in Bronzeville to fight Southern segregation, build Northern Black community, and root for the American Giants — was the team's press arm and the community's paper. NPR's Scott Simon: the Defender was founded "to root for the Chicago American Giants" alongside its civil-rights mission. **[VERIFIED]** (NPR/KWIT, 2016, via Ethan Michaeli's *The Defender*)
- The Defender's coverage was boosteristic by design: the "Mister Fan" column (almost certainly written by Fay Young) effused "unbridled and unabashed enthusiasm," and attendance figures ran in headlines — "Ten Thousand See Detroit Opener at Mack Park," "17,000 See Foster Win." **[SUPPORTED]** (academic paper via ndl.ethernet.edu.et)
- Foster lived in Bronzeville, blocks from where the Flyball apparel shop now sells American Giants gear — "the Negro League started right here in Bronzeville," in shopkeeper Bruce Gage's words. **[SUPPORTED]** (Medill Reports Chicago)
- **3,000 mourners** stood in snow and rain in Bronzeville for Foster's funeral. **[VERIFIED]**

### Ballparks (South Side)

- Schorling's Park: 39th & Wentworth (now Pershing & Wentworth/Princeton) — the old White Sox grounds, handed to Foster's club when Comiskey built his "Baseball Palace of the World" in 1910. Four blocks south of where Comiskey Park / U.S. Cellular Field would stand. Now the Wentworth Gardens housing project. **[VERIFIED]**
- Comiskey Park (1941–1950): the Giants played in the White Sox's house when the Sox were away — two teams, one park, one city, divided by race and schedule. **[VERIFIED]**
- The pre-1911 Union Giants played at Auburn Park, 79th & Wentworth. **[SUPPORTED]**

### Institutions

- **The Chicago Defender** — the team's chronicler, advocate, and community bulletin. **[VERIFIED]**
- **The Paseo YMCA (Kansas City)** — where the league was founded, but the league was *run* from Foster's Chicago headquarters. Chicago was the capital; Kansas City was the convention hall. **[SUPPORTED]** (Royal Retros)
- **Lincoln Cemetery, Blue Island, Illinois** — Foster's resting place. **[VERIFIED]**

## 4. Candy hooks — 7 Shorts-ready angles

*Per the doctrine: feed them candy, lace with medicine. The names pull; the depth keeps. Each hook is a verified fact shaped for a 60-second Short.*

1. **"He built Black baseball — then died in an asylum."** Rube Foster founded the Negro National League in 1920, ran it from Chicago, and was committed to the Kankakee asylum in 1926 at the height of his powers. He died in 1930, never seeing the back-to-back championships his own team won. 3,000 people stood in snow and rain at his Bronzeville funeral. *(The tragedy arc — the strongest hook in the dossier.)*

2. **"26 wins, 23 straight, back-to-back shutouts — in a playoff doubleheader."** Willie Foster's 1926 season: 26 wins including 23 consecutive, then consecutive complete-game shutouts against the Kansas City Monarchs to send Chicago to the World Series. Followed it with 32–3 in 1927. Rube's own half-brother. *(The stat avalanche.)*

3. **"He pitched AND caught — and lived to 103."** Ted "Double Duty" Radcliffe, named by Damon Runyon himself, played both ways for the American Giants, managed them, moved to the South Side in the Great Migration, and at 96 became the oldest man ever to appear in a pro game. *(The character piece.)*

4. **"Thirteen Hall of Famers. One team."** Cool Papa Bell, Oscar Charleston, Pop Lloyd, Pete Hill, Cristóbal Torriente, Smokey Joe Williams, Turkey Stearnes, Mule Suttles, Willie Wells, Andy Cooper, Hilton Smith, Bill Foster, Rube Foster — thirteen Cooperstown plaques, one Chicago franchise. *(The prestige flex.)*

5. **"123 and 6."** The 1910 Leland Giants — Foster's team before the rename — reportedly won 123 games and lost 6. One of the great single-season records in baseball history, in any league, and almost nobody knows it. *(The number that stops the scroll.)*

6. **"The coffin closed at the usual hour a ballgame ends."** Foster's funeral: the detail that makes 3,000 mourners in the snow feel like a ballpark, not a statistic. *(The closer — grief as civic ritual.)*

7. **"Two teams, one park, divided by the schedule."** From 1941 to 1950, the American Giants played at Comiskey Park — but only when the White Sox were out of town. Same grass, same city, never the same day. *(The Chicago gut-punch.)*

## 6. Gaps — what we don't know yet

1. **Schorling Park capacity:** 15,000 (BR Bullpen) vs. "5,000-seat stadium" (AA Registry). Unresolved — likely total capacity vs. seated, but no primary source confirmed.
2. **Player salaries:** "Major-league salaries within the constraints of Black baseball economics" (Royal Retros) is evocative but unsourced. Actual payroll figures for the 1920–22 championship teams not found in this pass. The Chicago Defender's sports pages are the lead.
3. **Game-by-game 1926/1927 World Series:** Series scores verified (5–4–(2), 5–3–(1)); individual game accounts live in the Defender archives and Black press microfilm — not yet pulled.
4. **Foster's 1903 pitching record:** 54–1 (REA/SABR) vs. 51 of 55 (Royal Retros). Both are secondary; Seamheads/Retrosheet-era box scores are the arbiter.
5. **Attendance figures:** Defender headlines give spot numbers ("17,000 See Foster Win"); no compiled season attendance for the Giants in this pass. Baseball-Reference's NNL misc tables list no attendance column for these seasons.
6. **The 1919 race riot → league founding causal link:** BlackPast.org asserts inspiration; the Defender's 1919–20 editorials would confirm or complicate. Single-source — treat as SUPPORTED, not VERIFIED.
7. **"Longest continuous franchise in Black baseball":** Attributed to the Negro Leagues Baseball Museum via oursportscentral.com (2010). Plausible (1910–1961 with the 1955–58 gap). Museum's exact wording not independently confirmed.
