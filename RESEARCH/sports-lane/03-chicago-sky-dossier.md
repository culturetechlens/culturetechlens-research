## 1. The verified record

### The franchise

- **Awarded February 2005:** NBA commissioner David Stern announced Chicago had been awarded a WNBA franchise, temporarily called *WNBA Chicago*. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia/Chicago Sky)
- **Named September 20, 2005:** The name and logo debuted at the Adler Planetarium. Team president/CEO Margaret Stender said the yellow-and-blue colors represent "a beautiful day in Chicago between the blue sky and bright sunlight." **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia/Chicago Sky)
- **Inaugural season 2006.** First WNBA franchise not associated with an NBA team (the second independently owned/operated franchise in league history, per EBSCO). **[VERIFIED]** (Britannica; EBSCO; Wikipedia)
- **First coach/GM: Dave Cowens** (former Celtics star). First draft pick: Candice Dupree, 6th overall, 2006 (Temple) — All-Rookie, led the team at 13.7 ppg. Inaugural record: **5–29**. Cowens departed after one season. **[VERIFIED]** (Britannica; Sportskeeda)
- **The climb:** First playoff appearance **2013** — Elena Delle Donne drafted that year, 24–10 record, Pokey Chatman coaching. First Finals appearance **2014** (lost to Phoenix). Four straight playoff appearances 2013–16. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; Sportskeeda)
- **Arenas:** UIC Pavilion (2006–2009) → Allstate Arena, Rosemont (2010–2017) → **Wintrust Arena (2018–present)**. Wintrust opened 2017 in the South Loop's McCormick Square, ~10,000 seats, shared with DePaul basketball. **[VERIFIED]** (phshirt; EBSCO; Wikimedia Commons category page)
- **Ownership:** Michael J. Alter, principal owner since inception (Alter Group, real estate). Nadia Rawlinson joined as co-owner and operating chairman (January 2023). John Rogers, early minority owner. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; Sportico via sky.wnba.com)

### The 2021 championship

- **June 2021:** Candace Parker — Naperville native, 13 seasons with the Los Angeles Sparks — signed with her hometown Sky in free agency. **[VERIFIED]** (NPR; Athlon Sports)
- **Regular season: 16–16.** The sixth seed. Nobody's favorite. **[VERIFIED]** (Britannica; Wikipedia/2021 WNBA Finals)
- **The run:** Beat Dallas and Minnesota in single-elimination rounds, then toppled the #1 Connecticut Sun 3–1 in the semis. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia/2021 WNBA Finals)
- **The Finals, October 10–17, 2021:** Sky vs. Phoenix Mercury (a rematch of the 2014 Finals). Sky won **3–1**. Game 4: **80–74** at a sold-out Wintrust Arena, October 17 — down nine entering the fourth quarter, won it behind Allie Quigley's 26 points and Parker's 13 rebounds. **[VERIFIED]** (NPR; Wikipedia/2021 WNBA Finals)
- **Finals MVP: Kahleah Copper.** **[VERIFIED]** (NPR; Wikipedia)
- **The historical weight:** Chicago's first professional basketball championship since the Bulls' 1998 title. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia/2021 WNBA Finals)
- **James Wade** — head coach and GM since 2019 — became the **third Black male coach to win a WNBA championship**. **[VERIFIED]** (NPR)
- **Obama's note:** "Congrats to our new WNBA champs, @ChicagoSky! I couldn't be prouder of this team — they worked hard for this moment, and have made our city proud." **[VERIFIED]** (NPR, quoting the post)

### The key figures

- **Candace Parker:** Born St. Louis, raised Naperville, Illinois — Naperville Central High School, three-time Illinois Ms. Basketball (2002–04, first ever). Tennessee national titles 2007–08. #1 overall pick, 2008 (Sparks). 2× WNBA MVP (2008, 2013 — the only player to win MVP as a rookie), 2020 Defensive Player of the Year, 7× All-Star, 3× champion (2016 Sparks, 2021 Sky, 2023 Aces). Retired **April 28, 2024** (Instagram announcement), after 16 seasons. Named Adidas president of women's basketball, May 2024. The Sky retired her #3 jersey **August 25, 2025**. **[VERIFIED]** (NBC Sports; Athlon Sports; Basketball Museum of Illinois)
- **Courtney Vandersloot:** Drafted **#3 overall, 2011** (Gonzaga). Twelve seasons in Chicago (2011–22), then two with the New York Liberty (2023–24) — winning the **2024** championship there, the first in Liberty franchise history. Returned to Chicago in free agency, **February 2025**. Tore her ACL **June 7, 2025** — seven games into the homecoming. On **May 29, 2025**, before the injury, she became the franchise's all-time leading scorer (**3,745 points**), passing Allie Quigley. Led the league in assists five times (2015, 2018–21); franchise single-game record 18 assists (August 31, 2020). Re-signed **April 11, 2026**; returned from the ACL injury **June 26, 2026**, vs. Portland. **[VERIFIED]** (sky.wnba.com; WNBA.com; USA Today; Yardbarker)
- **Allie Quigley:** Franchise scoring leader (3,723 points) until Vandersloot passed her in May 2025. Jersey **#14 retired July 9, 2025** — Vandersloot, on crutches from the ACL tear, gave the center-court speech to her wife. **[VERIFIED]** (Swish Appeal; sky.wnba.com)
- **Kahleah Copper:** 2021 Finals MVP. Traded to Phoenix before the 2024 season (for context on the post-title dismantling). **[SUPPORTED]** (multiple 2024 trade reports; graded SUPPORTED pending a second source in this pass)
- **James Wade:** Head coach/GM 2019–2023. Departed during the 2023 season (Emre Vatansever finished as interim). **[VERIFIED]** (Sportskeeda; phshirt)
- **Teresa Weatherspoon:** Hired as head coach for 2024; relieved September 2024 after a 13–27 season. **[VERIFIED]** (phshirt; Britannica season table)
- **Tyler Marsh:** Hired as head coach for the 2025 season; still in the role. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia/Chicago Sky; Sportskeeda)
- **Angel Reese:** Drafted **#7 overall, April 15, 2024** (LSU — 2023 national champion, 2023 Final Four Most Outstanding Player, 2024 SEC Player of the Year). Two seasons in Chicago, two All-Star nods. **Traded to the Atlanta Dream, April 2026.** Won **2026 WNBA Defensive Player of the Year** with Atlanta (announced October 1, 2026) — first in Dream franchise history — setting WNBA single-season records for total rebounds (521), offensive rebounds (206), and double-doubles (32). **[VERIFIED]** (USA Today, October 1, 2026; OurSports Central; WAFB)

### The post-title years (2022–2026, compressed)

- **2022:** 26–10, lost in the semifinals. The last contending season of the Parker-era core. **[VERIFIED]** (Britannica)
- **2023:** 18–22, lost in the first round. Wade departed mid-season. The $85M valuation round (see §2). **[VERIFIED]** (Britannica; Sportico)
- **2024:** 13–27, missed the playoffs. Weatherspoon's single season. Reese's rookie year. **[VERIFIED]** (Britannica)
- **2025:** Rebuild under Marsh. Vandersloot's ACL tear. Quigley's jersey retirement. **[VERIFIED]** (multiple sources above)
- **2026:** Reese traded to Atlanta (April); Vandersloot's ACL comeback (June); Sky near the bottom of the standings at midseason. **[VERIFIED]** (USA Today, June 2026; USA Today, October 2026)

## 2. The economics — the CTL spine

*This is why the Sky is Subject 03 and not a team history. The franchise is the balance sheet of women's professional basketball — every line item is a claim about what Black women's labor is worth.*

### The pay scale, dated

- **2020 CBA (in effect 2020–2025):** 2024 supermax **$241,984**; standard max **$208,219**; rookie minimum **$64,154**; veteran minimum (3+ years) **$76,535**; team salary cap **$1,463,200**. **[VERIFIED]** (Her Hoop Stats; Spotrac)
- **The comparison that ends the argument:** The NBA rookie minimum for 2024–25 was **$1,157,153** (HoopsRumors; Spotrac) — roughly **4.8 times** the WNBA's *supermax*. The NBA's average salary that season was **$11.8 million** (Basketball Reference, via Orlando Magic Daily). An undrafted NBA rookie who never played a minute earned more than the best-paid woman in the WNBA. **[VERIFIED]** (all figures dated 2024–25)
- **The new CBA (ratified March 2026; 7-year term, opt-out after six):** Salary cap **$7 million** in Year 1 (2026) — nearly 5× the 2025 cap. Supermax **$1.4 million** (20% of cap). Standard max **$1.19 million** (17%). Average salary ~**$583,000–$600,000**. Minimum **$270,000**. Revenue share ~**20%** of gross revenue (up from ~9%). The #1 draft pick's rookie deal: **$500,000** base in 2026 (vs. Paige Bueckers' $78,831 in 2025 — more than 6×). Cap expected to exceed **$10 million** by 2031. Season can extend to November 21 from 2027; up to 50 games (2027), 52 (2029). A'ja Wilson, Kelsey Mitchell, and Napheesa Collier signed $1.4M supermaxes — the first $1M+ salaries in league history. **[VERIFIED]** (ESPN via Swish Appeal; Front Office Sports via GambleRss; Sports Illustrated; Basketball Network)
- **The "Exceptional Performance" rule:** Young stars on rookie deals can now renegotiate into max/supermax money early with All-WNBA or MVP honors — the mechanism that would have paid Angel Reese like a star while she was still on a rookie contract. **[VERIFIED]** (Front Office Sports via GambleRss)

### The charter-flight fight

- **May 7, 2024:** Commissioner Cathy Engelbert announced full-time charter flights for all teams — a Delta Air Lines partnership costing **~$25 million per year for two years** ($50M total). The league had spent **$4 million** on charters in 2023 (playoffs and back-to-backs only). **[VERIFIED]** (AP via NBC Sports; Essentially Sports)
- **The trigger:** Five days earlier, rookie Caitlin Clark was mobbed by fans and a TV crew at Dallas-Fort Worth airport. But the pressure had been building since **June 2023**, when Brittney Griner was harassed by a "provocateur" at the same airport — an incident ESPN's Christine Brennan said should have been the catalyst a year earlier. **[SUPPORTED]** (Essentially Sports, citing Brennan)
- **What it replaced:** Commercial flights, middle seats, layovers, security lines — for athletes up to 6'9", in a compressed Olympic-year schedule with more back-to-backs. Liz Cambage (6'8") had publicly said she paid out of pocket to upgrade because she didn't fit in coach. **[VERIFIED]** (NPR/WUGA, February 2022; Essentially Sports)
- **The CTL read:** The league could afford $50M for planes the moment the *attention* arrived — not when the *players* asked. The money followed the cameras, not the knees. **[SUPPORTED as analysis]**

### The media rights

- **July 2024:** An 11-year deal with Disney (ABC/ESPN), Amazon (Prime Video), and NBCUniversal, valued at **~$2.2 billion (~$200M/year)** — announced as part of the NBA's broader $77B package. ESPN had aired the Finals exclusively since 2003; the new deal splits them across partners. **[VERIFIED]** (ESPN, July 25, 2024; Front Office Sports)
- **May 2026:** Expanded to **$3.1 billion** total with Paramount (CBS), Scripps (Ion), USA Sports, and NBA TV added. Average annual value **$281 million** — roughly **6.5×** the previous deal's $43M AAV. A record **216 national games** in the 2026 season. Reset provisions kick in after the 2028 season — either side can trigger renegotiation. **[VERIFIED]** (Front Office Sports, May 2026; USA Today)
- **The arc:** The league's total media revenue was ~$60M "last year" (2025, per FOS). The new money is real, and it's why the $7M cap exists. **[VERIFIED]** (Front Office Sports)
- **Engelbert's exit:** Cathy Engelbert — commissioner since July 2019, the league's first — announced **September 4** she will retire at the end of the 2026 season. Napheesa Collier publicly criticized her leadership in the 2025 end-of-season press conference. No successor named. **[VERIFIED]** (Insider Sport, September 2026)

### The expansion valuations

- **Golden State Valkyries:** $50M expansion fee (announced 2023, began play 2025). Now reported as the **first women's sports franchise worth $1 billion** (Yahoo Sports, 2026). **[VERIFIED]** (Front Office Sports; Yahoo via dejavu.org)
- **Toronto Tempo** (2026, first non-U.S. franchise): fee **DISPUTED** — $50M (CNBC/Yahoo) vs. $115M (other reports). **[DISPUTED — both figures stated]**
- **Portland Fire** (2026): **$125M** fee (Front Office Sports). **[SUPPORTED]**
- **Cleveland (2028), Detroit (2029), Philadelphia (2030):** **$250M each** — a record for U.S. women's sports, eclipsing the NWSL's $110M Denver fee. True commitment per team exceeds $300M with facilities. 18 teams by 2030 — the most in league history. All three owned by NBA ownership groups (Dan Gilbert, Tom Gores, Harris Blitzer). **[VERIFIED]** (Front Office Sports; Defector; SportsPro)
- **The Sky's own mark:** June 2023 — 10% sold to eight investors (mostly women: Laura Ricketts, Mary Dillon, Laura Desmond, Tina Tchen, Cari Sacks, Anne Sempowski Ward) at an **$85M valuation**, then the second-highest in the league behind Seattle's $151M. The $8.5M raise was earmarked for player experience, marketing, staffing, and a new practice facility. **[VERIFIED]** (Sportico, via sky.wnba.com; Just Women's Sports)
- **The CTL read:** In three years, the price of admission went from $50M to $250M. The Sky's $85M valuation in 2023 — headline news then — would barely buy a third of a 2028 expansion team now. The market decided women's basketball was undervalued; the players are still waiting for the market to pay *them*. **[SUPPORTED as analysis]**

### The numbers vs. the narratives

- **Narrative: "The league loses money."** The WNBA's historical operating losses are real and documented — but the $75M capital raise (2022), the $3.1B media deal, and the $250M expansion fees are the market pricing the *future*, not the past. Both things are true at once. **[SUPPORTED]**
- **Narrative: "Nobody watches."** The 2024 regular season was the most-watched in 24 years; the 2025 Clark-vs.-Reese matchup was the most-watched regular-season game in 25 years (SportsPro). 216 national TV games in 2026. The audience arrived; the infrastructure is catching up. **[VERIFIED]** (SportsPro)
- **Narrative: "The players should be grateful."** The 2026 CBA's 20% revenue share is the players *buying* what the market already priced — after years of protest, including the 2020 CBA opt-out fight that got them to this table. Gratitude flows upward in every labor story ever told. **[SUPPORTED as analysis]**

## 3. What did it cost them

*The flagship question's second half. The price of the economics above, paid in bodies and years.*

### The double shift

- **The structure:** Five months of WNBA season, then seven months overseas — Turkey, Spain, China, Australia, and (until 2022) Russia. The LA Times' formulation: stars travel overseas for "four or five times what they earn at home." **[VERIFIED]** (Sports Business Journal, citing LA Times)
- **The Ekaterinburg premium:** UMMC Ekaterinburg — bankrolled by mining billionaires — paid **~$1M per season** to Brittney Griner (per her wife Cherelle Griner, via ABC News: "more than triple" her WNBA salary) and **~$1.5M to Diana Taurasi in 2015** — the year Taurasi sat out the WNBA season entirely to rest and collect the Russian check. Breanna Stewart, Jonquel Jones, and Courtney Vandersloot all played there. **[VERIFIED]** (Get Up Erica/ABC News; NPR/WUGA; Sportsnet)
- **Candace Parker's verdict:** "People look at this as our off-season, but this is our season, and the WNBA is our off-season. This is where you take care of your family." **[VERIFIED]** (NYT via NPR/WUGA)
- **The human line:** Cherelle Griner — "BG would wholeheartedly love to not go overseas. She has only had one Thanksgiving in the States in nine years since she's been pro." **[VERIFIED]** (ABC News via Get Up Erica)
- **The new math:** The 2026 CBA's $1.4M supermax finally approaches what Ekaterinburg paid a decade ago. Whether it ends the double shift depends on whether the money reaches the middle of the roster, not just the supermax tier. **[SUPPORTED as analysis]**

### The Griner case — the extreme price

- **February 17, 2022:** Brittney Griner detained at Moscow's Sheremetyevo Airport — vape cartridges with cannabis oil in her luggage. She was in Russia to play for UMMC Ekaterinburg, the same offseason job she'd held for years. **[VERIFIED]** (NPR; Arizona State Law Journal)
- **Sentenced to nine years** (August 2022). Released **December 8, 2022** — 294 days later — in a prisoner swap for arms dealer Viktor Bout, exchanged at Abu Dhabi airport, announced by President Biden. **[VERIFIED]** (NPR, December 8, 2022)
- **Why she was there:** The $1M Russian salary vs. ~$228K WNBA supermax (2022). She was there because the economics demanded it. The detention made the pay gap a national-security story. **[VERIFIED as causal chain; the salary figures are VERIFIED]**
- **The aftermath:** Nearly a dozen WNBA players had competed in Russia the previous winter; after Griner's detention and the Ukraine invasion, **none went back**. Turkey became the top market — at "a few hundred thousand," much less than Russian money. **[VERIFIED]** (Sportsnet, 2022)

### The visibility gap

- For most of the league's history, the best women's basketball players on earth were more famous in Ekaterinburg than in Chicago. The 2024–26 attention surge (Clark, Reese, the $3.1B deal, 216 national games) is the first time the domestic audience has approached the scale of the talent. **[SUPPORTED as analysis]**
- The Sky's own arc mirrors it: a championship in 2021 that the city celebrated — then a rebuild conducted in relative quiet while the league's valuation quintupled around them. **[SUPPORTED as analysis]**

## 4. The Chicago connection

### The building

- **Wintrust Arena:** Opened 2017 in the South Loop's McCormick Square; the Sky's home since 2018. ~10,000 seats. Shared with DePaul basketball. The first purpose-built stretch in franchise history after the UIC Pavilion and the Rosemont exile — a South Side-adjacent home for a team that had been playing in a suburb. **[VERIFIED]** (EBSCO; phshirt; Wikimedia Commons category)

### The parade — October 19, 2021

- **The route:** Departed Wintrust Arena at 11 AM, north on Michigan Avenue from Roosevelt to Randolph, rally at the Jay Pritzker Pavilion in Millennium Park at noon. Free and open to the public. **[VERIFIED]** (City of Chicago release via OurSports Central; SI)
- **The city showed up:** Mayor Lori Lightfoot ("Let's get ready to paint the town #SkyTown!"). Governor JB Pritzker declared Chicago **"Skytown"** for the day. Chance the Rapper attended — photographed trying on Stefanie Dolson's Tokyo Olympics 3×3 gold medal. **[VERIFIED]** (SI; Just Women's Sports)
- **Parker's line:** "I remember watching the Chicago Bulls' parade and rally on TV and dreaming of being in that moment, dreaming of bringing a title to your hometown." **[VERIFIED]** (SI, October 19, 2021)
- **The CTL read:** The Bulls' parades ran down the same streets in the 90s. Parker's sentence closes a 23-year loop — the girl from Naperville who watched Jordan's city celebrate, then gave the city its own celebration. The dynasty's shadow and the Sky's sunlight, one parade route. **[SUPPORTED as analysis]**

### The South Side thread

- The Sky's stars are Black women — Parker, Vandersloot (white, the exception that proves the roster's composition), Copper, Reese, Cardoso. The 2021 title was won by a team that looked like the South and West Sides, celebrated on Michigan Avenue. The Defender's "Mister Fan" columns once sold Black baseball to Bronzeville; the Sky's championship sold itself to a city that had to be taught, in 2021, to show up for women's basketball — and did. **[SUPPORTED as analysis; the roster composition is VERIFIED]**

## 5. Candy hooks — 7 Shorts-ready angles

*Per the doctrine: feed them candy, lace with medicine. The names pull; the depth keeps. Each hook is a verified fact shaped for a 60-second Short.*

1. **"The NBA's worst rookie made more than the WNBA's best player."** 2024: NBA rookie minimum $1,157,153. WNBA supermax $241,984. An undrafted kid who never played a minute out-earned every woman in the league. *(The number that ends the argument.)*

2. **"She came home and the city painted itself Skytown."** Candace Parker — Naperville kid, 13 years in LA — signed with Chicago in 2021 and delivered its first WNBA title, then watched the governor rename the town for her team. *(The homecoming.)*

3. **"$1 million in Russia. $228,000 at home. That's why she was there."** Brittney Griner didn't go to Ekaterinburg for adventure. She went because the math demanded it — and the math nearly cost her nine years. *(The price, named.)*

4. **"The sixth seed that won it all."** 16–16. Nobody's favorite. Then they beat the #1 seed and took the Finals 3–1. The 2021 Sky are the worst regular-season team to ever win a WNBA championship. *(The underdog — verify "worst record" framing before publishing; graded PROVISIONAL as a superlative.)*

5. **"From $50 million to $250 million in three years."** Golden State paid $50M to join the WNBA. Three years later, Cleveland paid $250M. The market quintupled while the players were still flying commercial. *(The valuation avalanche.)*

6. **"She tore her ACL — then became the franchise's all-time scorer anyway."** Courtney Vandersloot: 12 years in Chicago, two in New York, back home, knee gone in seven games — and she still passed Allie Quigley for the scoring record first. Then came back from the ACL at 37. *(The lifer.)*

7. **"They traded Angel Reese. She won Defensive Player of the Year in Atlanta."** Chicago drafted the Bayou Barbie #7 in 2024, got two All-Star seasons, traded her in April 2026 — and watched her set the league's single-season rebounding records in a Dream uniform. The Sky's loss is the league's gain, and the wound is fresh. *(The one that stings.)*

## 7. Gaps — what we don't know yet

1. **Toronto Tempo's expansion fee:** $50M (CNBC/Yahoo) vs. $115M (other reports). Both figures are in circulation; neither is confirmed by the league. Stated as DISPUTED, not resolved.
2. **"Worst regular-season record by a WNBA champion":** The 2021 Sky went 16–16. Whether any champion had a worse (or equal) record needs a full champions-table check before the superlative ships in a Short.
3. **Sky local revenues and attendance:** The $85M valuation is verified; the underlying local revenue, sponsorship, and attendance figures that justified it are not in this dossier. The team's own financials are private.
4. **The Angel Reese trade package:** USA Today (June 2026) reports the April 2026 trade brought Skylar Diggins, Azurá Stevens, Rickea Jackson, and Natasha Cloud to Chicago — but the exact pick/player structure isn't fully detailed in the sources reviewed. Treat the names as SUPPORTED, the structure as a gap.
5. **Kahleah Copper's exit:** The trade to Phoenix is widely reported but was single-sourced in this pass. Marked SUPPORTED pending a second confirmation.
6. **Black Chicago's ground-level experience of the 2021 title:** Same gap as the Bulls dossier — the parade is documented, the meaning in the neighborhoods is analysis. The Defender's October 2021 coverage and South/West Side oral histories are the leads. Flag for the community-sourcing project.
7. **Practice facility status:** The 2023 raise earmarked a new practice facility ("scouting locations," aiming for post-2024). Whether it broke ground is unverified in this pass.
