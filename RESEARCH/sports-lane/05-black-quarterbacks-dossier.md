# Black Quarterbacks' Desegregation — Research Dossier

**CULTURETECHLENS · "Culture, Clearly Seen." · Black Cultural Intelligence**

**Lane:** Sports — "The Black Athletic Record"
**Flagship question:** What did they actually do — and what did it cost them?
**Subject 05 of 05** · Dossier compiled October 7, 2026

**Cite as:** CultureTechLens, "Black Quarterbacks' Desegregation — Research Dossier (Sports Lane 05)," October 7, 2026. Source: CultureTechLens.

**Status:** DRAFT RESEARCH DOSSIER. No site pages built, no Shorts made, nothing published. This is the research foundation the lane builds on.

**Scope note:** This is the desegregation arc of football's last segregated position — from Fritz Pollard's 1920s to the first Super Bowl with two Black starting quarterbacks a century later. The CTL angle is the mechanism: how the position was kept, who paid for each inch, and what Chicago's own franchise did and didn't do.

**VERIFIED** — confirmed by two or more independent sources. **SUPPORTED** — confirmed by one credible source, consistent with the record. **PROVISIONAL** — plausible, single-source or partially confirmed. **DISPUTED** — sources conflict; both stated. **UNRESOLVED** — could not be verified; listed in Gaps, never smoothed over.

## 1. The verified record

### The beginning: Fritz Pollard

- **Frederick Douglass "Fritz" Pollard** — Brown University, Rose Bowl 1916 (one of the first Black players in the game), joined the Akron Pros of the APFA in 1919, led them to the league championship in 1920. **[VERIFIED]** (Pro Football Hall of Fame; multiple sources)
- **1921:** named co-coach of the Akron Pros — the first Black head coach in pro football history. He later coached the Milwaukee Badgers (1922), Hammond Pros (1923, 1925), and Providence Steam Roller (1925). **[VERIFIED]** (Pro Football Hall of Fame; Andscape)
- **1923:** with the Hammond Pros, Pollard became the team's signal-caller — the NFL's first Black quarterback. **[SUPPORTED]** (Andscape; Pro Football History). **Note:** Pro Football Reference lists Pollard as a blocking back for Hammond, and no period roster lists a designated quarterback — the claim rests on contemporary accounts and his grandson's testimony, not box scores. **Carried as SUPPORTED, not VERIFIED.**
- **The color line:** the NFL's Black players were purged in the mid-1920s-to-early-1930s. One account dates a formal vote to **1926** (ten Black players, Pollard among them, shown the door as the league contracted). Another dates a **"gentleman's agreement"** to a **1933** owners' meeting at a Pittsburgh hotel — where, Pollard insisted to the *Los Angeles Times* in 1976, Bears coach **George Halas** was present and complicit. (Halas denied it: "At no time did the color of skin matter.") **[DISPUTED on the mechanism and date; VERIFIED that Black players were absent from the NFL from the mid-1930s until 1946.]**
- **The barnstorming years:** Pollard fielded all-Black teams — the **Chicago Black Hawks (1928)** and the **Harlem Brown Bombers (1935–38)** — challenging NFL teams to games they refused to play. The Bombers never lost. **[SUPPORTED]** (Pro Football History; Andscape)
- **The family:** the Pollards were Rogers Park, Chicago — "the suburb's only black family"; Pollard's Barber Shop was a neighborhood hangout. **[SUPPORTED]** (Humans of Africa)
- **The Hall of Fame:** inducted **2005** — two years after his son Fritz Jr.'s death. Fritz Jr.'s last words to his family: "Don't forget your quest." **[VERIFIED]** (Humans of Africa; HOF records)
- **The legacy organization:** the **Fritz Pollard Alliance** (founded 2003) was among the first to support Colin Kaepernick in 2016. **[SUPPORTED]** (Humans of Africa)

### The professional first (Canada)

- **Bernie Custis** — the first Black quarterback to start professionally, for the **Hamilton Tiger-Cats of the CFL in 1951**. **[SUPPORTED]** (Wikipedia/Racial issues faced by Black quarterbacks, citing ESPN Books' *Trials and Triumph of the Black Quarterback*)

### The modern-era first: Willie Thrower (Chicago)

- **October 18, 1953, Wrigley Field:** Chicago Bears rookie **Willie Thrower** relieved an ineffective George Blanda against the San Francisco 49ers — the first Black quarterback in the NFL's modern era. He completed 3 of 8 passes for 27 yards with an interception before Blanda was reinserted. The Bears lost 35–28. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; ChicagoBears.com; Andscape)
- **The context:** Michigan State, 1952 national champions; undrafted; nicknamed **"Mitts"** for his huge hands; could throw 70 yards. The first Black quarterback at a Big Ten school. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; ChicagoBears.com)
- **The aftermath:** one more appearance, then released. Halas's explanation, via the *Chicago Defender*: "We had a particular play we wanted to call next and Thrower has not learned all of our offenses." **[VERIFIED]** (Edinburg Post, citing the Defender)
- **The erasure:** the *Chicago Tribune*'s game story mentioned Thrower's debut but said nothing about the barrier. **[VERIFIED]** (ChicagoBears.com)
- **The memory:** Warren Moon thanked Thrower by name in his Pro Football Hall of Fame acceptance speech. **[VERIFIED]** (ChicagoBears.com; Andscape)
- Thrower died February 20, 2002, of a heart attack, age 71. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia)
- **Note:** George Taliaferro played quarterback among seven positions in the NFL from 1951–55, but Thrower was the first Black player to play the position exclusively. **[SUPPORTED]** (Andscape)

### The Magician: Marlin Briscoe

- **September 29, 1968:** Broncos rookie Marlin Briscoe entered against the Boston Patriots and directed an 80-yard touchdown drive. **One week later, October 6, 1968,** he started against the Cincinnati Bengals — a win — becoming the **first Black starting quarterback in modern pro football history**. **[VERIFIED]** (Pro Football Hall of Fame; multiple obituaries)
- **The path:** drafted in the **14th round as a defensive back**; asked coaches for a quarterback tryout in camp — "If I'm not good enough, then cut me." **[SUPPORTED]** (Gridiron Football USA; Black Enterprise)
- **The season:** 11 games, 5 starts — **1,589 passing yards, 14 touchdowns** (a Broncos rookie record), 308 rushing yards, 3 more TDs. Of 11 AFL quarterbacks with 150+ attempts, he was one of four with more TDs than INTs — Joe Namath wasn't one of them. **[VERIFIED]** (HOF; Denver Sports)
- **The door, closed:** Denver would not let him compete at quarterback in 1969. He asked for his release, joined Buffalo, and switched to **wide receiver** — Pro Bowl 1970 (57 catches, 1,036 yards, 8 TDs); Super Bowls VII and VIII with the Dolphins, including the 1972 perfect season. **[VERIFIED]** (multiple obituaries)
- College Football Hall of Fame, 2016. Died June 27, 2022, of pneumonia, age 76. **[VERIFIED]**

### The regular: James "Shack" Harris

- **1969:** drafted by Buffalo in the **8th round** (192nd overall) — most teams wanted to switch him to another position. He refused. On **September 10, 1969**, he started the season opener — the first Black quarterback to do so in either league. The *New York Times* headline: **"JETS ARE LIKELY TO FACE HARRIS, BILLS' NEGRO PASSER, ON SUNDAY."** **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; ESPN; Andscape)
- **The indignity:** while O.J. Simpson (same draft, #1 overall) stayed at the Hilton, Harris roomed at a Buffalo YMCA for $6 a night and was made to lace and clean shoes before winning the job over Jack Kemp and Tom Flores. **[SUPPORTED]** (Andscape)
- **One of his receivers was Marlin Briscoe** — the previous year's pioneer, already forced out of the position. **[VERIFIED]** (Andscape)
- **1974:** Chuck Knox named him the Rams' starter — the first Black quarterback to hold the job consistently; first Black QB to start an **NFL playoff game** (NFC Championship, December 29, 1974); **Pro Bowl and Pro Bowl MVP**. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; Andscape; Ramstalk)
- **The quote:** "They questioned our ability to lead, they questioned our character. But those are things I could control." **[SUPPORTED]** (Stark Library transcript of Harris–Williams panel)
- **After:** front-office executive — Super Bowl XXXV with the Ravens. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia)
- **The culture:** Richard Pryor built a routine around the Black quarterback's struggle; Harris told *The Undefeated* he appreciated that Pryor "was even aware of my journey." **[SUPPORTED]** (Andscape)

### The champion: Doug Williams

- **Grambling State, 1974–77,** under Eddie Robinson: 8,000+ yards, 93 touchdowns, three Black College National Championships, **4th in Heisman voting, 1977**. **[VERIFIED]** (Good Black News; HBCU Sports)
- **1978:** drafted **17th overall by Tampa Bay** — the first Black quarterback taken in the first round. Led the Bucs to three playoff berths and the **1979 NFC Championship Game**. **[VERIFIED]**
- **The pay gap:** significantly underpaid relative to white counterparts — a disparity he attributed to racism. Left for the USFL, then joined Washington in 1986. **[SUPPORTED]** (Good Black News)
- **Super Bowl XXII, January 31, 1988, San Diego:** **18 of 29, 340 yards, 4 touchdowns — all four in the second quarter** — 1 interception. Washington 42, Denver 10. **First Black quarterback to start and win a Super Bowl; Super Bowl MVP.** **[VERIFIED]** (SI; Good Black News)
- **The record:** 35 points in a single quarter of a Super Bowl. **[VERIFIED]** (SI)
- Retired 1989; coached at Morehouse and Grambling (returned as head coach 2011). **[VERIFIED]** (My Black History; HBCU Sports)

### The exile who conquered: Warren Moon

- **Undrafted, 1978.** Scouts suggested a **position switch to tight end**. He refused and signed with the **Edmonton Eskimos**. **[VERIFIED]** (Pro Football Hall of Fame; Events Edge)
- **Canada, 1978–83:** **five consecutive Grey Cups (1978–82)** — unprecedented; Grey Cup MVP 1980 and 1982; CFL Most Outstanding Player 1983. **[VERIFIED]** (HOF; Sportsnet)
- **NFL, 1984–2000:** Houston Oilers (1984–93) — 3,338 yards as a rookie, a franchise record; **1990 AP Offensive Player of the Year** (4,689 yards, 33 TDs); Minnesota, Seattle, Kansas City after. **Nine Pro Bowls.** **[VERIFIED]** (HOF)
- **The only player in both the Pro Football and Canadian Football Halls of Fame** (2006 and 2001). Combined: **70,553 yards, 435 touchdowns.** **[VERIFIED]** (HOF; PSA)
- **2022:** when Arizona put a **four-hour film-study clause** in Kyler Murray's $230.5M extension, Moon called it stereotypical — "we were lazy, that we didn't study, that we couldn't be leaders, that we weren't smart" — and the team removed it within a week. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia, citing contemporary coverage)

### The century mark: Super Bowl LVII

- **February 12, 2023:** **Patrick Mahomes** and **Jalen Hurts** — the first Super Bowl with **two Black starting quarterbacks**, 35 years after Doug Williams, 100 years after Fritz Pollard. Chiefs 38, Eagles 35. **[VERIFIED]** (ESPN; NBC Sports)
- **Mahomes:** "The guys that came before me and Jalen set the stage for this... whenever Doug Williams or Michael Vick, Donovan McNabb go out and play great football, it gives other guys like me and Jalen chances." **[VERIFIED]** (ESPN; NBC Sports)
- **The lineage to that night:** Williams (1988) → McNair (2000) → McNabb (2005) → Kaepernick (2013) → Wilson (2014, 2015) → Newton (2016) → Mahomes (2020, 2021) → Hurts (2023). Eight Black quarterbacks had started a Super Bowl; three had won one. **[VERIFIED]** (The Post Athens)
- **The youngest starting QB pair** in Super Bowl history (combined 51 years, 337 days). **[VERIFIED]** (SI)

## 2. The desegregation arc — how the position was kept

*The CTL angle: the barriers weren't one wall but a system — and each had a name.*

### The position switch

- **The mechanism:** Black college quarterbacks were routinely drafted — or recruited — **to play another position**. The choice was presented as voluntary; the scholarship calls it self-preservation: switch or risk no pro career at all. **[VERIFIED]** (Journal of African American Studies, 2008, via Squarespace PDF)
- **Sandy Stephens:** 1960 — first Black quarterback to win a national title and earn All-American honors (Minnesota). The Vikings and the AFL's Titans **drafted him and told him he wouldn't play quarterback**. He played QB for the Montreal Alouettes instead. **[VERIFIED]** (Tom Shanahan Report)
- **The study:** in the 2008–09 recruiting class, **Black quarterbacks were 28.5% more likely to be forced out of their position** than white counterparts; white running backs were 31.7% more likely to be moved somewhere their "intellectual advantages" counted. **[SUPPORTED]** (Coleman & Scott, 2018, via Critical Debates journal)
- **Lamar Jackson, 2018:** Heisman winner; Hall of Fame GM **Bill Polian** suggested a switch to receiver. Jackson **refused to run the 40 at the Combine** — forcing evaluators to watch him throw. Won **2019 MVP**. Polian recanted. After five TDs against Miami: **"not bad for a running back."** **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; Revolt)
- **The pattern, named:** Mel Kiper flagged Jackson's 57% college completion as a first-round killer — then dismissed Josh Allen's 56.2% with "stats are for losers." **[SUPPORTED]** (Revolt)

### The intellect stereotype

- **The frame:** quarterback was coded as the "thinking position" — leadership, intellect, decision-making — and Black players were coded as athletic. Athleticism in a Black QB was read as **a mask for inefficiency**, not a weapon. **[SUPPORTED]** (Revolt; Saint Mary's literature review)
- **"Stacking":** the documented practice of sorting players into positions by racial stereotype — Black players to the "athletic" positions, white players to the "cerebral" ones. **[VERIFIED]** (academic literature, multiple sources)
- **The pay data:** a 2009 study found Black quarterbacks **paid less than white counterparts at equal perceived performance**. **[SUPPORTED]** (Berri & Simmons, 2009, via Critical Debates)
- **The superintendent:** Onalaska, Texas's Lynn Redden resigned after saying **"when you need precision decision making you can't count on a black quarterback."** **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia)
- **The draft room:** Cam Newton was "slammed by draft analysts" before going #1 in 2011; **Mitchell Trubisky was taken ahead of Deshaun Watson** in 2017 despite Watson's back-to-back title games and a championship. **[SUPPORTED]** (Revolt)

### The college pipeline

- **Jimmy Raye:** Michigan State, 1965–66 — the South's first Black quarterback to win a national title at the Division I level; recruited through **Duffy Daugherty's "Underground Railroad"** of Southern Black athletes barred from major-conference schools in the South. **[VERIFIED]** (On3)
- **The incentive trap:** Black QBs thrived in option offenses that rewarded running — then were told they couldn't run a pro-style offense, the very style they'd never been given the chance to learn. **[SUPPORTED]** (Straight Dope analysis; consistent with the stacking literature)

## 3. What did it cost them

*The flagship question's second half.*

### Careers diverted or shortened

- **Briscoe:** the best rookie QB season in Broncos history — then never played the position again. His prime athletic years were spent proving himself at a second position. **[VERIFIED]**
- **Moon:** six prime years in Canada (ages 22–27) — an NFL career that should have started in 1978 started in 1984. The exile cost him his twenties. **[VERIFIED]** (HOF timeline)
- **Thrower:** two games. A barrier broken in garbage time, then a release and a lifetime of frustration at being doubted — his son said he was angered "throughout his life with those who doubted his claim." **[SUPPORTED]** (Valley News Dispatch via ChicagoBears.com)
- **Williams:** the Tampa pay gap — years of elite play at a discount, then exile to the USFL before Washington. **[SUPPORTED]** (Good Black News)

### The mental tax

- **Harris's ledger:** "They questioned our ability to lead, they questioned our character" — and he had to be unimpeachable at both while also playing quarterback. **[VERIFIED]** (Stark Library transcript)
- **Jackson's Combine:** a Heisman winner **hiding his speed** at a job interview to be taken seriously as a passer. **[VERIFIED]** (Revolt)
- **The Murray clause:** in **2022** — thirty-four years after Doug Williams's MVP — a franchise still wrote "study your playbook" into a Black quarterback's $230M contract. **[VERIFIED]**
- **Moon's verdict on it:** "something we were always accused of back in the day... that we were lazy, that we didn't study." The stereotype outlived the barriers it built. **[VERIFIED]**

### The erasure

- **Pollard:** written out of the history books for decades; the Hall of Fame came in 2005, nineteen years after his death, on his son's dying quest. **[VERIFIED]**
- **Thrower:** "Everyone knows the Jackie Robinson story; nobody knows the Willie Thrower story" — his son, Melvin. **[VERIFIED]** (ChicagoBears.com)
- **Briscoe:** died in 2022; the obituaries called him a pioneer, but his quarterback career remains a footnote to his receiving career in most accounts. **[SUPPORTED]** (obituary coverage)

## 4. The Chicago connection

### The franchise's ledger

- **October 18, 1953:** Willie Thrower breaks the modern color line **in a Bears uniform, at Wrigley Field** — then is gone within weeks. **[VERIFIED]**
- **1953–2021:** from Thrower's release until Justin Fields was named the starter, the Bears — the franchise of Halas, the charter franchise — **did not start a Black quarterback for 68 years**. **[SUPPORTED]** (franchise starter history; widely reported — full season-by-season verification is a gap, see below)
- **The Halas shadow:** Pollard blamed Halas personally for the 1933 color line; Halas denied it. The franchise Halas built then went nearly seven decades without trusting a Black quarterback with the job. The two facts sit side by side; the dossier does not assert causation beyond the documented record. **[SUPPORTED — the juxtaposition is stated, the causal claim is not made]**

### Justin Fields

- **2021:** drafted **11th overall** (Bears traded up); named starter after Andy Dalton's injury; sacked 9 times in his starting debut at Cleveland. **[VERIFIED]** (Wikipedia; 247Sports)
- **2021–23:** three seasons, two head coaches (Nagy fired, Eberflus hired), eight wins in the last two years combined. **[VERIFIED]** (AtoZ Sports)
- **March 2024:** traded to Pittsburgh — GM Ryan Poles took less than market value to, in his words, **"do right by" Fields**; the Bears drafted Caleb Williams #1 overall. **[VERIFIED]** (iHeart; AtoZ Sports)
- **After:** six starts for the Steelers (replaced by Russell Wilson); two years, $40M with the Jets ("a disaster two years"); **March 2026:** traded to Kansas City as Patrick Mahomes's backup after Mahomes's ACL/LCL tear. **[SUPPORTED]** (AtoZ Sports/Yardbarker, March 2026 reporting)

### Caleb Williams

- **April 25, 2024, Detroit:** selected **#1 overall** by the Bears — USC, Heisman 2022 (4,537 yards, 42 TDs), 9,782 college yards, 93 TDs. The franchise's first #1-overall quarterback. **[VERIFIED]** (USC Athletics; ChicagoBears.com)
- **The context the dossier carries:** seventy-one years after Thrower relieved Blanda at Wrigley, the Bears used the first pick in the draft on a Black quarterback — and the city held its breath the way it hadn't since Fields. What the pick means for the franchise's ledger is an open question, not a verdict. **[SUPPORTED — the facts verified; the framing is the dossier's, plainly marked]**

## 5. Candy hooks — 7 Shorts-ready angles

*Per the doctrine: feed them candy, lace with medicine. The names pull; the depth keeps.*

1. **"The Magician threw 14 touchdowns — and never played quarterback again."** Marlin Briscoe's 1968: a Broncos rookie record, then the door closed. He won two Super Bowls as a receiver instead. *(The triumph inside the theft.)*

2. **"He won five championships in Canada because America wouldn't let him play."** Warren Moon: undrafted, told to switch to tight end, five straight Grey Cups — then the NFL finally called. *(The exile.)*

3. **"35 points in one quarter."** Doug Williams, Super Bowl XXII, second quarter: 9 of 11, 228 yards, 4 touchdowns. The greatest quarter in Super Bowl history, by the first Black quarterback to play in one. *(The masterpiece.)*

4. **"The $6-a-night YMCA."** James Harris: 8th-round pick, rooming at the Buffalo YMCA while O.J. Simpson got the Hilton — then beat out Jack Kemp and Tom Flores and started the opener. The *Times* called him the "Negro passer." *(The indignity and the answer.)*

5. **"'Not bad for a running back.'"** Lamar Jackson, after five touchdowns: the Heisman winner they told to switch positions, refusing the 40 at the Combine, winning MVP. *(The clapback.)*

6. **"68 years."** From Willie Thrower's release to Justin Fields's first start — the Chicago Bears, charter NFL franchise, went nearly seven decades without a Black starting quarterback. *(The Chicago gut-punch.)*

7. **"100 years, two quarterbacks, one field."** Fritz Pollard, Chicago, 1923 — Patrick Mahomes and Jalen Hurts, Super Bowl LVII, 2023. A century from the first Black quarterback to the first Super Bowl with two of them. *(The arc.)*

## 7. Gaps — what we don't know yet

1. **The Bears' 68-year gap, season by season.** The claim that no Black quarterback started for Chicago between Thrower (1953) and Fields (2021) is widely reported but was not verified against a complete franchise starter list in this pass. Pull Pro Football Reference's Bears yearly starters before publishing.
2. **Pollard's 1923 quarterback snaps.** Andscape and Pro Football History say signal-caller; PFR lists blocking back; no period roster names a QB. Contemporary newspaper accounts (Akron, Hammond) are the arbiter — not pulled.
3. **The 1926 vote vs. the 1933 agreement.** Two accounts of the color line's mechanism. The NFL's 1920s minutes and the Pittsburgh hotel meeting records — if they survive — would settle it.
4. **Briscoe's first-start score.** Sources report the October 6, 1968 Bengals game as a win; the score is given as both 10–7 and 10–6. Use "a win," not a score, until the box score is pulled.
5. **Fields's Chicago years, graded.** The Nagy-to-Eberflus transition, the offensive line quality, the play-calling — the "what did it cost him" for Fields specifically requires sourced analysis, not narrative. The 2026 Chiefs trade reporting is single-sourced (AtoZ/Yardbarker).
6. **The Wonderlic paper trail.** The test's racial-discipline history at the Combine is well-documented in scholarship but no specific leaked-score episode was verified for this dossier. The academic literature (not the anecdote sites) is the lead.
7. **Bernie Custis (1951).** The CFL first is single-sourced to a Wikipedia summary of ESPN Books. Verify against the CFL record before publishing.
8. **Black Chicago's ground-level experience.** As with the Bulls dossier: what the position's desegregation felt like in Bronzeville barbershops and South Side living rooms is the community's oral record — flagged as a future assignment, not asserted here.
