## 1. Municipal / government (primary)

1. **City of Chicago, Department of Planning and Development — *Emmett Till and Mamie Till-Mobley House* landmark designation report.**
   Records: funeral arrangements with A.A. Rayner & Sons (4141 S. Cottage Grove); remains arrived Sept 2, 1955; Till-Mobley pressured A.A. Rayner Jr. to open the sealed container; body prepared for public viewing with glass cover; tens of thousands viewed at Rayner and Roberts Temple; burial at Burr Oak Sept 6, 1955.
   URL: https://www.chicago.gov/content/dam/city/depts/zlup/Historic_Preservation/Publications/Emmett_Till_and_Mamie_Till-Mobley_House_report.pdf

2. **U.S. Congressional Record, Vol. 141, Issue 203, Extensions of Remarks (Dec 18, 1995)** — Rep. Bobby Rush tribute to "Earnest Griffin."
   Records: Worsham College of Mortuary Science graduation 1934; final rites for Elijah Muhammad and Jesse Owens; first African American invited into an international thanatology association; 20+ years on Lakeside Bank board; Civil War veteran grandfather memorial wall.
   URL: https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-141/issue-203/extensions-of-remarks-section/article/E2373-2

3. **National Park Service — Emmett Till and Mamie Till-Mobley National Monument designation (news release, Aug 29, 2023).**
   Records: designation July 25, 2023; three sites incl. Roberts Temple Church of God in Christ, "the site of Till's widely attended open casket visitation and funeral."
   URL: https://www.nps.gov/till/learn/news/august-2023-graball-landing-event.htm

4. **Illinois Board of Higher Education — board records (June 2019 agenda, Item F-2).**
   Records: Worsham College founded March 17, 1911 as Worsham College of Embalming.
   URL: https://archive.ibhe.org/IBHE/board/2019/04June2019/Item_F-2_New_Units_of_Instruction_Independent_Institutions.pdf

5. **Office of U.S. Senator Tammy Duckworth — Emmett Till materials.**
   Records: 100,000 attendees across Till viewing/funeral days (conflicts with 50,000 figure — preserved as variance).
   URL: https://www.duckworth.senate.gov (Till materials page; exact path in research notes)

## 2. Institutional / collections

6. **Library of Congress, *Inside Adams* blog — Lynn Weinstein, "Honoring African Americans: Celebrating Life in Death – African American Funeral Homes" (Feb 2021).**
   Business Reference Librarian, Science Technology & Business Division. Institutional secondary framing: first family businesses after slavery; segregated trade; credit exclusion; community leadership; civil-rights financial support and gathering spaces; industry figures (~$14.2B revenue 2016; 15,818 funeral homes; ~1,200 Black-owned independents; 4 consolidators).
   URL: https://blogs.loc.gov/inside_adams/2021/02/african-american-funeral-homes/

## 3. Peer-reviewed scholarship

12. **Candi K. Cann, "Black Deaths Matter: Earning the Right to Live — Death and the African-American Funeral Home," *Religions* 11(8):390 (2020). DOI: 10.3390/rel11080390.**
    Baylor University. Peer-reviewed survey: economic-detour framing; burial-society lineage; halls as meeting/event space; informal lending; transportation; civil-rights-era support (meeting space, voter-registration equipment, bail); consolidation pressure; NFDMA history.
    URL: https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/11/8/390/xml
    *Caveat:* broad interdisciplinary synthesis; some claims rest on interviews or cited secondary work — graded SUPPORTED, not VERIFIED.

13. **Crystal R. Sanders, "Dignity in Life and Death: Undertaker Clarie Collins Harvey and Black Women's Entrepreneurial Activism," *Journal of Mississippi History* (Fall/Winter 2014).**
    Archivally grounded (Clarie Collins Harvey Papers, Amistad Research Center): funeral-home resources deployed for Freedom Riders, Womanpower Unlimited, movement information, strategy sessions.
    URL: https://www.mississippihistory.org/sites/default/files/admin/content/files/Sanders,%20Crystal%20R._Dignity%20in%20Life%20and%20Death_Undertaker%20Clarie%20Collins%20Harvey%20and%20Black%20Women's%20Entrepreneurial%20Activism_FallWinter%202014.pdf

14. **W.E.B. Du Bois, *The Philadelphia Negro* (1899).**
    Primary scholarship: 100 burial societies, 7,448 members by 1838, $18,851 paid in, $14,171 benefits paid, $10,023 on hand.

15. **For further research (not used as evidence):** Suzanne E. Smith, *To Serve the Living: Funeral Directors and the African American Way of Death* (Oxford UP, 2010); Karla FC Holloway, *Passed On: African American Mourning Stories* (Duke UP, 2002); B. Bunch-Lyons, "'Ours Is a Business of Loyalty': African American Funeral Home Owners in Southern Cities," *The Southern Quarterly* 53(1) (2015).
