- **Creator:** Unidentified photographer; assembled for the 1900 Paris Exposition exhibit of American Negro life (W.E.B. Du Bois's exhibit)
- **Date:** 1899
- **Source institution:** Library of Congress, Prints & Photographs Division
- **Collection:** African American Photographs for the Paris Exposition of 1900
- **Source URL:** https://www.loc.gov/item/93517224/
- **Reproduction number:** LC-USZ62-109101 (b&w film copy neg.)
- **Call number:** LOT 11306 [item] [P&P]
- **License/rights:** Rights Advisory — "No known restrictions on publication." (LOC does not license; researcher's obligation to assess; advisory is the governing statement.)
- **Caption:** Horses and carriages lined up before the funeral home of C.W. Franklin, undertaker, Chattanooga, Tennessee, 1899 — a Black undertaking business presented as visible, capitalized enterprise three decades after Emancipation.
- **Context:** Illustrates the brief's claim that Black funeral homes were among the first Black family businesses after slavery and operated as public-facing institutions, not back-room trades. Supports the "economic detour" framing (segregated trade, Black-owned capital on display).
- **Why selected:** One of the earliest known photographs of a Black funeral home as a business; LOC-held with an explicit no-known-restrictions advisory; ties directly to the mutual-aid/undertaking lineage section.

## V-04 — Function map: why the funeral home did more than bury

- **Type:** Build-at-publish (data-driven diagram)
- **Status:** build-at-publish
- **Build instructions:** Three-column flow diagram, CTL palette. Column 1 "Segregated exclusion" (6 nodes: white funeral homes refused Black bodies; no safe meeting halls; no Black credit; no neutral public-mourning venues; no professional training access; records of Black lives poorly kept).
