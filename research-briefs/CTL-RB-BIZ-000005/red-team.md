Adversarial review of the brief. Each item: the challenge, the finding, the disposition.

## 1. Unsupported claims
- **Challenge:** Does any claim lack a source? **Finding:** The brief's headline caution ("no documented corridor-decline evidence") is a negative finding from systematic searching — legitimate but must be labeled as absence-of-evidence, which it is (C024, UNRESOLVED). **Disposition:** pass.
- **Challenge:** "Subsidy bought buildings, not rootedness" (§09) — pithy but is it evidenced? **Finding:** Supported by the Whole Foods mechanism evidence (prices, outside shoppers, imposed replacement) but the aphorism generalizes beyond the two grocery cases. **Disposition:** keep, flagged as analytic synthesis not a discrete claim.

## 2. Circular sourcing
- **Challenge:** Do multiple citations trace to one origin? **Finding:** Yellow Banana lease details come from one Chicago Tribune story mirrored by Connect CRE and Edinburg Post — counted once, not three. The Consortium findings reach us via EdWeek, not the reports themselves; the brief is transparent about this. **Disposition:** pass with transparency; recommend direct report read at publication.

## 3. Myths repeated as facts
- **Challenge:** "Largest mass school closure" — verified? **Finding:** EdWeek's characterization ("the largest mass school closure to date"); Consortium 2015 says "the largest number of schools closed in one year by any district in the nation." Consistent; attributed, not asserted independently. **Disposition:** pass.
