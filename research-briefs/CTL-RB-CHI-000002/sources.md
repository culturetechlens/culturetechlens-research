### Primary / government & institutional records
1. **[NEW · P]** U.S. National Park Service, "From the Great Migration to Boston's Charlestown Navy Yard" — census-derived African American population table for Chicago, 1900–1950 (30,150 / 44,103 / 109,458 / 233,903 / 277,731 / 586,598). https://nps.gov/articles/great-migration-to-charlestown-navy-yard.htm — *Table reproduced secondhand; direct NHGIS verification still needed.*

3. **[NEW · S]** Chicago Urban League, *Creating a Segregated Chicago: Race and Residence* (CULtivate, 2019) — Great Migration framing, +148% Black population growth 1910–1920 ("50,000 to 75,000 new" residents), citing U.S. Census and UIC Department of Geography. https://chiul.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/CULtivate-Part-1_Residential-Segregation-and-Housing-Transportation_Full-Draft_FINAL.pdf
4. **[NEW · S]** Allissa V. Richardson, "The Platform: How Pullman porters used railways to engage in networked journalism" (peer-reviewed article) — Abbott's "Great Northern Drive" (1917 full-page spread) and the Pullman-porter distribution network. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/289684786_The_Platform_How_Pullman_porters_used_railways_to_engage_in_networked_journalism
5. **[NEW · S]** Yale Open Courses, AFAM 162 Lecture 7, "Migration and Urbanization" (transcript) — Chicago Black population 44,000 (1910) → ~110,000 (1920), +~150%. https://oyc.yale.edu/african-american-studies/afam-162/lecture-7
6. **[NEW · S]** Ethan Michaeli, *The Defender: How the Legendary Black Newspaper Changed America* (Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2016; ISBN 9780547560694) — Defender migration role; ~60,000 migrants to Chicago during WWI (single-sourced — Provisional). 9. **[NEW · P]** NPS, Wabash Avenue YMCA place page — completed 1913; Rosenwald funding; ASNLH 1915; community fundraising $20,000+. https://www.nps.gov/places/wabash-avenue-ymca.htm
