## Primary & government sources

- **[N, G]** National Park Service, "Black Metropolis Thematic Nomination" (Multiple Property Submission, 1985) — quoted via IIT Bronzeville History Booklet. Provides the verified "Wall Street of the black community" characterization of State/35th. (Shared-log line appended.)
- **[N, G]** City of Chicago, Commission on Chicago Landmarks, South Side Trust and Savings Bank landmark designation report (2008) — $350,000 construction cost, 1922 completion, June 6, 1931 closure, Classical Revival details.
- **[N, G]** City of Chicago, 79th/Vincennes TIF district page (designation 2007) — government evidence of the 79th Street corridor's policy footprint.
- **[N, G]** Chicago Metropolitan Agency for Planning (CMAP), Roseland Medical District Existing Conditions Report & Market Analysis (c. 2021) — Michigan Avenue as Roseland's main commercial corridor; east–west commercial corridors at 95th, 103rd, 111th; Red Line/Metra station inventory.
- **[N, G-data]** CTA ridership and station data via encyclopedia relay (see below).

## Journalism

- **[N, J]** ProPublica Illinois, "Disinvested: How Government and Private Industry Let the Main Street of a Black Neighborhood Crumble" (2020-11-11) — Madison Street investigation; the brief's principal West Side source.
- **[N, J]** Columbia Journalism Review, "Fifty years after Chicago 'riot,' new photos emerge—and develop a story" (2018) — Karega Kofi Moyo photos; 11 dead, two miles of rubble on Madison.
- **[N, J]** Catholic New World / legacy.chicagocatholic.com, "Remembering the tragic riots of 1968" (2008) — April 5 timeline: 36 major fires 4–10 p.m., half the city's fire units on a two-mile stretch of Madison.
- **[N, J]** Daily Northwestern, "Bronzeville: A struggle for identity" (2001-10-17) — Timuel Black oral history on 35th→47th "downtown" shift.
- **[N, J]** Next City, "How Hard Is It To Create a Commercial Corridor for Black-Owned Businesses in Chicago?" (2021) — succession sequence (Italian/Polish → Black), Land Bank no-cash-bid strategy.
- **[N, J]** Streetsblog Chicago, "How Chicago's legacy of segregation has pushed Black people into transit, job deserts" (2020-01-21) — 79th Street "miles of businesses"; Madison, 63rd/Halsted, 79th named as formerly vibrant streets.
- **[N, J]** Newcity Design, "At Home in Chatham" (2015-11-19) — Independence Bank (79th & Cottage Grove, co-founded 1964 by George Johnson, sold 1995); Chatham's Black financial institutions.
