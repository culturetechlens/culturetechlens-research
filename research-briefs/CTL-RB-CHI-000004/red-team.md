# Red Team — CTL-RB-CHI-000004: The Black Belt: Name, Geography and Change

Adversarial audit of the brief, the claim ledger, entities, relationships, and visuals. Each check: the attack, the finding, the disposition.

## 1. Sourcing: is every major claim grounded?

- **Attack:** The transfer story (Southern term → Chicago) is the brief's most interesting claim and its weakest evidence.
- **Finding:** Correct. It is graded PROVISIONAL (C-04), the weak website is explicitly rejected in sources.md, and §05/§23 keep it out of the verdict's "establishes" column. The brief would be stronger with a migrant-letters check, which is listed as research-next #2. **Disposition: HOLD — acceptable with visible uncertainty; do not upgrade without primary evidence.**

## 2. Circularity: does any claim cite itself?

- **Attack:** The brief cites the Encyclopedia of Chicago for both the usage period and the boundary polygons, then treats agreement between them as corroboration.
- **Finding:** Partially fair. EoC "Black Belt" and EoC "Bronzeville" are separate entries by different authors, but they share an institutional roof. The brief's corroboration structure is otherwise genuinely independent: the 1922 commission (primary), the Defender column (Black press via UIC), Drake & Cayton (1945 text), the CUL report (census synthesis), and the NPS nomination (federal) do not share authorship. **Disposition: NOTE — flag EoC-on-EoC as single-institution corroboration where it occurs; done in C-15/C-16 notes.**

## 3. Myths and weak superlatives

- **Attack:** "Three-quarters of Black Chicago lived in the belt" and "30 blocks, seven wide" smell like the sibling red team's warned-about superlatives.
- **Finding:** The 75% figure is graded SUPPORTED (C-21) with an explicit thin-sourcing note, and the brief never presents it as VERIFIED. The "30 blocks / seven wide" phrasing appears once, attributed to the corroborated line, and is not load-bearing. **Disposition: PASS with grade discipline intact.**

## 4. Dates

- **Attack:** Check every date: 1903, 1917, 1919, 1922, 1930, 1932, 1941, 1945, 1985.
- **Finding:** 1903 (Du Bois) verified in primary text. 1917 (Tribune) and 1919 (riot) from the commemoration project — the Tribune excerpt's date is taken on the project's authority; the riot date is uncontroversial. 1922 (commission, Defender column) verified. 1930/1932 (Bronzeville) from EoC + two corroborations. 1941 (FSA photos) from LOC item records. 1945 (Black Metropolis) uncontroversial. 1985 (NPS nomination receipt date) from the nomination itself. The Timuel Black video's upload date (~2014) is approximate — recorded as such. **Disposition: PASS; video date flagged approximate.**

## 5. Geography

- **Attack:** Does the brief ever present one polygon as "the" Black Belt?
- **Finding:** No. §12 is explicitly a table of source-specific polygons; §15 and the red-team-warned anachronism check both stress that boundaries are functions of source purpose. The brief refuses to draw a single definitive map. **Disposition: PASS.**

## 6. Anachronism

- **Attack:** "Bronzeville" projected backward onto the 1910s; modern community areas projected onto the 1920s.
- **Finding:** The brief's inclusion/exclusion rules (§02) forbid both, and no section violates them. The 1930 coinage is kept distinct from earlier geography throughout. **Disposition: PASS.**

## 7. Causation

- **Attack:** Does the brief claim the name *caused* containment, or that migration *caused* the naming?
- **Finding:** §14 says the name "naturalized" containment and "did work" — a claim about discourse, not a causal claim about housing markets. The transfer mechanism is explicitly unresolved rather than asserted. No unqualified causal claims found. **Disposition: PASS.**

## 8. Bias: whose voices are missing?

- **Attack:** The brief leans on commissions, encyclopedias, sociologists, and one famous oral history. Where are women, ordinary residents, churches, businesses?
- **Finding:** This is the brief's real weakness, and it is structural: the *question* is about a term's meaning and geography, and the surviving dated uses of the term come disproportionately from institutions. Women are thinly present (no female term-users identified in this pass). Ordinary residents appear via Timuel Black alone — one voice, flagged as such. Churches appear only via Rev. L. K. Williams. The brief acknowledges the gap in §18/§24 (oral-history layer as research-next #6). **Disposition: HOLD — acceptable for a term-history brief, but the next pass must add resident and women's voices before any public-facing page treats this as the full story.**

## 9. Rights

- **Attack:** Are all archived images rights-cleared with file-page verification?
- **Finding:** Yes. Three FSA/OWI photographs downloaded from LOC with item-level records showing "No known restrictions" (see visuals.md). No hotlinking — files are local in visuals/. No AI-generated or watermark-stripped images. Videos are embedded/linked, never downloaded or re-uploaded. **Disposition: PASS.**

## 10. Duplicates and sibling overlap

- **Attack:** Does this brief duplicate CTL-DR-BCI-001 or CTL-DR-BVM-005?
- **Finding:** No. Sibling material is quarantined in §19 with SIBLING-DERIVED tags, and the brief's original argument (term history, boundary-by-era table, naming struggle) is new work. The Great Migration, commercial corridor, and institutional detail stay in the siblings. **Disposition: PASS.**

## 11. Grade discipline

- **Attack:** Spot-check grades: is anything VERIFIED that should be SUPPORTED?
- **Finding:** C-10 (Defender column via UIC, column not directly viewed) is SUPPORTED — correct. C-12 (Park/Burgess via one secondary) is SUPPORTED — correct. C-20 (EoC usage period) is SUPPORTED — correct; an encyclopedia's synthesis claim is not primary evidence. C-21 (75%) is SUPPORTED — correct. C-23 (NPS nomination, primary document) is VERIFIED — correct. C-27 (sibling population figures) is VERIFIED on sibling authority — borderline; the note admits no independent re-verification, and the claim is used for scale only. **Disposition: PASS with the C-27 caveat noted.**

## 12. The scare-quote reading

- **Attack:** The brief leans hard on "so-called 'Black Belt'" as proof of established vernacular. Could the commission have been distancing itself from a term it was *introducing*?
- **Finding:** "So-called" can do either job, but the commission also *defines* District I with the term and uses it repeatedly without gloss — the pattern of a term being borrowed, not launched. Combined with the 1922 Defender column's casual use in the same year, the vernacular reading is the stronger one. **Disposition: PASS — reading is argued, not assumed (§06).**

## Verdict

**Ship with the noted holds.** The brief's uncertainty is load-bearing and visible; its grades survive spot-checks; its visuals are cleared. The two holds for the next pass: (1) primary-newspaper sweep 1915–1925 to date the first Chicago use and test the transfer story; (2) resident/women's voices beyond Timuel Black.
