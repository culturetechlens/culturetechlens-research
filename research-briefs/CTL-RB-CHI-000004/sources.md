# Sources — CTL-RB-CHI-000004: The Black Belt: Name, Geography and Change

Evidence hierarchy: primary sources → archives → government/data → institutions → historical newspapers → scholarship. Search snippets and weak aggregators are never evidence; they appear below only in the "rejected/consulted" list.

## Primary sources

1. **Chicago Commission on Race Relations, *The Negro in Chicago: A Study of Race Relations and a Race Riot* (University of Chicago Press, September 1922).** Public-domain transcription via Project Gutenberg (derived from Internet Archive scan). Supplies the scare-quoted "so-called 'Black Belt,'" the 1919 riot-district definitions, the ~90% residency figure, the Roosevelt–39th/Wentworth–lake population counts, and the 1910/1920 Black-population, riot-geography, bombed-home, church, housing, and institution maps. URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/57343/57343-h/57343-h.htm
2. **W. E. B. Du Bois, *The Souls of Black Folk*, Chapter VII "Of the Black Belt" (1903).** Full text via americanliterature.com. Literary codification of the Southern term as racial geography. URL: https://americanliterature.com/author/web-du-bois/book/the-souls-of-black-folk/vii
3. **National Park Service, Black Metropolis Thematic National Register Nomination (received 1985-11-07).** Uses "Black Belt" in quotes for the historic "long narrow strip" (Van Buren–39th); distinguishes the Black Metropolis commercial/institutional core. URL: https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/64000173_text
4. **St. Clair Drake & Horace R. Cayton, *Black Metropolis: A Study of Negro Life in a Northern City* (1945).** Full-text excerpts via Everand; p.77 quotation corroborated by a Harvard dissertation. "The Black Belt became the Black Metropolis"; "center of the Black Belt — at Chicago's 47th St. and South Parkway."

Mirror accessed: igmlnet.uohyd.ac.in (LOC exhibition page).
7. **University of Chicago official YouTube channel, "Growing up in Chicago's 'Black Belt' — Timuel Black."** Oral-history testimony ("a city within a city"); credits: Vivian G. Harsh Research Collection of Afro-American History and Literature. URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xqn5OmF23HY

## Government / data

8. **City of Chicago, *Chicago Black Renaissance Literary Movement* landmark report (PDF).** Pre-1900 settlement geography: 1880s (16th–24th, west of State); by 1890 (24th–35th, Federal–State); 1920s expansion into Grand Boulevard/Washington Park. URL: https://www.chicago.gov/dam/city/depts/zlup/Historic_Preservation/Publications/Chicago_Black_Renaissance_Literary_Movement_Report.pdf
9. **Chicago Urban League, *CULtivate Part 1: Residential Segregation and Housing/Transportation* (2019, PDF).** Synthesizes census data into working polygons: 1920s (State–Cottage Grove, 31st–47th); 1940s (Cermak–South Chicago Ave., LaSalle–lake); 1950s West Side growth. URL: https://chiul.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/CULtivate-Part-1_Residential-Segregation-and-Housing-Transportation_Full-Draft_FINAL.pdf

## Institutional references

10. **Encyclopedia of Chicago, "Black Belt" entry.** Usage period "from the turn of the 20th century until after World War II"; original corridor 22nd–31st along State; retrospective expansion 39th–95th (Dan Ryan–lake). Bibliography: CCRR 1922, Drake & Cayton, Philpott. URL: http://encyclopedia.chicagohistory.org:80/pages/140.html
11. **Encyclopedia of Chicago, "Bronzeville" entry (Christopher Robert Reed).** Media labels ("Black Belt," "Black Ghetto," slur) and resident resentment; Gentry 1930 coinage; Bee 1930 Mayor contest; 1932 Defender move; c.1925 Second/Third Ward bounds (22nd–51st, Cottage Grove–Rock Island RR). URL: http://encyclopedia.chicagohistory.org:80/pages/171.html
12. **Chicago Race Riot of 1919 Commemoration Project.** "A narrow strip that came to be known as the 'Black Belt'"; "In the 1930s, this neighborhood came to be called Bronzeville"; 1917 Tribune survey excerpt (22nd–63rd district; Rev. L. K. Williams overcrowding quote).
13. **UIC Mapping Care project, "A Man Steps Into Nursing Field…" page.** Reproduces/cites Dr. A. Wilberforce Williams's 1922 *Chicago Defender* column describing Michael Reese Hospital in the center of Chicago's "Black Belt"; includes a March 8, 1952 Defender citation (not independently verified as term evidence). URL: https://www.mappingcare.digital.uic.edu/page/man-steps-into-nursing-field-and-finds-his-opportunities-plentiful-the-chicago-defender

## Scholarship / journalism

16. **Logan, J. et al., "Emergent Ghettos: Black Neighborhoods in New York and Chicago, 1880–1940" (peer-reviewed; PMC4597788, 2015).** The 1922 commission's 1910 "Black Belt" aggregation (12th–55th, Wentworth–Indiana, ~10% Black); enumeration-unit effects on perceived concentration. URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4597788/
17. **Chicago Magazine (2019, centennial coverage).** 1919 Black Belt summarized as 22nd–39th (Wentworth–State). Reputable journalism; corroborates the commission's northern riot polygon.
18. **ResearchGate, "Reconsidering the Southern Black Belt" (peer-reviewed article).** Soil-first origin of the Southern term; plantation/demographic layering.
19. **ResearchGate, Chicago School sociology article (secondary).** Park and Burgess's application of "Black Belt" to Chicago's South Side territory.
20. **MDPI *Land* (2026), Black Belt counties study.** Plantation slavery giving the region its demographic signature.
21. **WTTW (Chicago PBS), "Chicago's South Side with Geoffrey Baer."** Institutional documentary covering South Side neighborhoods including Bronzeville; geographic context. URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M5s-_v8lsS8
