# Red-Team Audit — CTL-RB-LIFE-000001 *Saturday at the Record Store*

Audit date: 2026-09-28. Method: adversarial review of every claim against eight failure modes. Verdicts are recorded honestly, including where the brief holds and where it is exposed.

## 1. Chicago overreach
**Attack:** Does the brief claim Chicago practice from Southern evidence?
**Verdict: HOLDING.** Every claim carries a geography label; the six strongest behavioral claims (LIFE-004 through LIFE-007) are explicitly tagged Southern-comparative in both the ledger and the prose. The brief's own §06 says "No equivalent Chicago Saturday evidence was found." Residual risk: readers skimming §18 findings may forget the tags. Mitigation: findings reference ledger IDs that carry the tags; G1/G2 graphics label markers.

## 2. Saturday extrapolation
**Attack:** The title is "Saturday at the Record Store" but Saturday-specific Chicago evidence is zero.
**Verdict: EXPOSED, acknowledged.** The title names the ritual; the evidence for it in Chicago is absent (LIFE-023 UNRESOLVED). The brief treats this as its largest uncertainty rather than its premise. Publication risk: the title will be read as a claim. Recommendation for publication: keep the title but pair it with the evidence-gap graphic G3 so the gap is visible at first glance.

## 3. Nostalgia
**Attack:** Is the past being romanticized?
**Verdict: HOLDING.** §17 exists precisely to block this. The brief documents racial surveillance in retail (LIFE-008), supply/credit discrimination, store failure concentrated outside majority-white areas (LIFE-010), and owner-reported taping threats (LIFE-009) — none of which appear in nostalgic accounts. The brief never says "the golden age." One soft spot: the phrase "the place to be" (LIFE-005) is a respondent's own warmth; it is quoted as memory, memory-graded, not as CTL's verdict.

## 4. Race and gender assumptions
**Attack:** Does the brief assume a uniform Black experience or erase women?
**Verdict: PARTIALLY EXPOSED.** The brief explicitly rejects uniform "Black taste" (LIFE-019) and records women's presence without manufacturing their perspective. But §16 admits the gap plainly: no documented account of women's shopping experience as distinct; DJ evidence is overwhelmingly male. The brief does not fix this — it flags it. The risk is structural to the source base, not to the writing.

## 5. House-origin myths
**Attack:** Does the brief take a position on where "house music" got its name?
**Verdict: HOLDING.** LIFE-022 is graded DISPUTED and the brief explicitly refuses the naming question, asserting only the shopping→DJ→party practice chain. No etymology appears in the prose.

## 6. Selection / survivorship bias
**Attack:** The sources favor survivors — stores that lasted, DJs who became famous, owners with archives.
**Verdict: EXPOSED, partially mitigated.** George's Music Room (40+ years), Importes Etc. (via famous DJs), and Calkins's directory data (which records stores, not shoppers) all tilt toward the visible. The failed stores of 1980–1990 (LIFE-010) are statistics, not stories; the casual Saturday browser who never bought anything left no record by definition. Mitigation: LIFE-007 and LIFE-019 both document non-purchasing participation, and the brief says so. Full mitigation is impossible — the gap is in the archive, not the argument.

## 7. Celebrity bias
**Attack:** Mary J. Blige and LL Cool J at George's (LIFE-015) are celebrity anecdotes.
**Verdict: HOLDING.** LIFE-015 sits in §13 (store context, not shopping practice) and the brief assigns store history to CTL-RB-MUS-000003. The celebrity list is not used as evidence for any LIFE claim. V03's Arthur Fiedler is explicitly framed as incidental to the mechanism of interest.

## 8. Copyright and rights
**Attack:** Any unlicensed or misattributed imagery, embedding, or quoting?
**Verdict: HOLDING.** V01/V02: LOC "no known restrictions," downloaded locally, not hotlinked. V03: PD verified at file level. V04/V05: CC BY-SA (attribution noted as required at publish). VID-01/VID-02: embed via official channels, oEmbed-verified embeddable, no downloads. VID-03: link only. Excluded items (Record Row re-uploads, colorized footage, NMAAHC restricted object) are documented in visuals.md with reasons. G1/G2 are original CTL data presentations — no rights issue. Remaining duty at publication: re-check embed status (link-rot plan) and add CC BY-SA contributor attributions.

## Overall
The brief's honesty mechanisms — geography labels, visible grades, the anti-nostalgia section, the evidence-gap matrix, VERIFIED-only relationships — are load-bearing, not decorative. The single largest live exposure is the title-to-evidence gap on Chicago Saturdays. That exposure is disclosed, not hidden. Recommended next action: prioritize §20.1 (Chicago oral histories) before any public launch that presents the title without the gap graphic.
