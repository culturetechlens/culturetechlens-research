# Red Team — CTL-RB-LIFE-000002 *Sunday in Black Chicago*

An adversarial review of this brief's argument, evidence, and failure modes. Each attack is paired with the brief's defense or a required fix.

---

## Attack 1: The "integrated ecosystem" thesis is asserted more than demonstrated
The brief's core claim — that Sunday was *one synchronization system* rather than five topics — rests heavily on a single congregation (Morning Star) plus a synthesis graphic (D01) the brief itself labels an "analytical construction." One landmarked church and a model do not make a citywide system. The arc graphic risks presenting the thesis as a finding when it is partly a hypothesis.
**Defense / fix:** The brief is explicit that D01 is a model, not a documented schedule, and every segment carries its grade. Best (2005) supplies the citywide mechanism (migrant-driven worship negotiation); Morning Star supplies the institutional proof-of-concept. But the red team is right that the brief needs a second and third congregation to carry "system" weight. **Required:** the next pass must test the arc against at least two more congregations' programs (Defender church pages, anniversary books) or downgrade "ecosystem" to "pattern observed at Morning Star, hypothesized citywide."

## Attack 2: Best (2005) is doing too much work
Five of the brief's foundational claims (C01–C05) come from one monograph's introduction, and the full text was not inspected. If Best's argument is mischaracterized at the introduction level, the migration frame wobbles.
**Defense / fix:** The introduction is the author's own summary of the argument — the lowest-risk part of a monograph to cite. The music dimension is independently corroborated (C10, C15). **Required:** inspect the full text before any public-facing publication; confirm the >70% figure's underlying data.

## Attack 3: The Roosevelt Hotel episode may not survive verification
C13 is the brief's sharpest counterpoint evidence and it is PROVISIONAL: the full text was not verified, the citation is incomplete, and the snippet came via search indexing. If Ervin 2015 does not say what the snippet says — or if the hotel is not Chicago's Roosevelt — the counterpoint's best anecdote collapses.
**Defense / fix:** The brief quarantines C13 explicitly and never builds a load-bearing argument on it alone; the structural counterpoint (C14, NLRA exclusion) stands independently. **Required:** resolve the full citation and verify the text before publication. If it fails, the counterpoint section must be rebuilt around verified labor evidence (Jones 1985; Defender labor coverage).

## Attack 4: Food claims are one family deep
The brief correctly grades the Sunday menu PROVISIONAL and excludes generic menu claims — but then still lets a single family's fried chicken and greens illustrate the food dimension of a citywide argument. A hostile reader will call this nostalgia with a citation.
**Defense / fix:** The brief's actual food finding is institutional (kitchens, mass feeding), not culinary — and it says so. The family account is labeled "illustrates, never proves." **Required:** do not let future edits promote C20 into a general menu claim; find church cookbooks or keep the gap.

## Attack 5: "Sunday best" without Chicago prices
C19 is real scholarship; C19b is a recorded void. The brief discusses Sunday dress at length while admitting it has no Chicago-specific affordability evidence. The section risks implying a well-dressed city on national evidence.
**Defense / fix:** The UNRESOLVED grade is displayed, not buried, and §10/§20 explicitly refuse to universalize. **Required:** the next pass must attempt Defender advertisements, department-store records, or oral histories on garment costs — or the dress sections shrink.

## Attack 6: The evening arc is comparative, not Chicago
The BTU/evening-service segment (C21) comes from non-Chicago Baptist records. Including it in D01 — even dotted — may launder a Southern/general pattern into a Chicago fact.
**Defense / fix:** The graphic labels it PROVISIONAL and the brief states Chicago verification is missing. **Required:** verify against a Chicago church program or remove the segment from the graphic in the published version.

## Attack 7: Survivorship and landmark bias
Morning Star is a *landmarked* church — designated precisely because it is exceptional. Building the institutional case on an exceptional congregation biases the brief toward the best-documented, most civically engaged churches and away from the storefront congregations where most migrants actually worshipped.
**Defense / fix:** Acknowledged. Best's "poor and working-class congregations as proving grounds" (C05) partially offsets this, but the brief has no storefront-church case study. **Required:** the next pass must include at least one storefront-congregation source or state the bias in the brief's limitations.

## Attack 8: Women's labor is claimed but not evidenced
§19 argues Sunday depended on women's invisible household labor, but the brief admits it "cannot yet quantify the household division of Sunday cooking labor in Chicago." The argument is structurally necessary to the thesis and empirically thin.
**Defense / fix:** The membership majority (C02) and institutional roles are documented; the household-labor claim is framed as inference-from-structure, not as a graded claim. **Required:** oral histories of Sunday preparation labor, stratified by class and generation.

## Attack 9: The videos are illustrative, but are they on-point?
VID-01 (Say Amen trailer) and VID-02 (PBS clip) illustrate gospel and the Black church generally — neither shows a Chicago Sunday. A reader may feel the videos decorate rather than explain.
**Defense / fix:** The brief states video never carries a claim alone, and both videos are institutionally sourced. VID-01's Dorsey/Barrett Sisters content is Chicago-specific in personnel. **Required:** keep searching for institutional Chicago-Sunday footage (Chicago Film Archives, SSHMP) for the published version.

## Attack 10: Period creep and the 2004/2021 sources
The brief uses RNS 2004 (dress) and PBS 2021 (video) — both outside the 1920s–1990s period — and a 2025 landmark report to evidence early-century facts. Each is scoped, but together they create a presentist tint.
**Defense / fix:** Each out-of-period source is explicitly scope-limited in the ledger and brief (retrospective, comparative, or institutional-record use). **Required:** no further out-of-period sourcing without the same labeling discipline.

## Attack 11: The single-photograph visual record
One sourced photograph for a 70-year citywide brief is thin, and V01's exact date and congregation are uncertain. The brief leans hard on one image.
**Defense / fix:** The brief records two unavailable-with-reason visuals rather than padding with dubious images — the honest choice. **Required:** the image gap is the next pass's highest visual priority.

## Attack 12: "Nostalgia is the enemy" — did the brief comply?
The brief's tone occasionally warms ("elders holding court," "children underfoot") in §§15–16, the least-evidenced sections. Warmth in low-evidence zones is exactly how nostalgia smuggles itself in.
**Defense / fix:** Partially guilty. **Required:** a pre-publication line edit must flatten evocative phrasing in every PROVISIONAL/UNRESOLVED-adjacent passage, or cut the passage.

---

## Verdict
The brief's argument is coherent and its grading discipline is genuine — the ledger's PROVISIONAL and UNRESOLVED grades are doing real work, not decorative work. The largest structural risks are **single-congregation dependence (Attack 1/7)** and **the unverified Roosevelt Hotel episode (Attack 3)**. Neither is fatal at the Research status level, but both must be resolved before this brief moves to any public-facing status.
