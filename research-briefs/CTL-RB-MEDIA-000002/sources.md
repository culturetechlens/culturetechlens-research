# Sources — CTL-RB-MEDIA-000002 "WVON and Black Chicago"

Provenance tiers used in this brief: **Primary** (contemporary documents, official records, first-person testimony) · **Scholarly** (peer-reviewed/dissertation) · **Secondary** (journalism, institutional synthesis) · **Lore** (commemorative, fan-archivist, or station-self accounts — never terminal evidence). Search snippets, unsourced aggregators, Wikipedia, and station lore were not used as terminal evidence.

## Primary / near-primary

1. **Broadcasting, February 18, 1963** — contemporary trade announcement of the Chess acquisition of WHFC. (Cited via registry trail; full-page retrieval deferred to CTL-DR-MEDIA-001.)
2. **Billboard, April 13, 1963** — contemporary trade record of WVON's launch. (Via trail; retrieval deferred.)
3. **Billboard, April 20 & 27, 1968** — contemporary coverage of WVON's King-assassination response (headlights tribute; Roy Wood's Atlanta funeral coverage). Cited and quoted via Searcy 2012.
4. **Billboard, August 4 & November 3, 1979** — contemporary trade record of the WXOL launch. (Via trail; retrieval deferred.)
5. **Illinois House Resolution HR0201 (2013)** — official 50th-anniversary resolution; anchors launch date, call-letter meaning, 1986 talk switch, roster, and the "first black-talk format" resolution-level claim. https://www.ilga.gov/documents/legislation/98/HR/PDF/09800HR0201.pdf
6. **Congressional Record, Vol. 159, No. 52 (April 17, 2013), Rep. Danny K. Davis** — WVON 50th-anniversary tribute. https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-159/issue-52/house-section/article/H2072-2
7. **Congressional Record, Vol. 165, No. 46 (2019), Sen. Tammy Duckworth** — Cliff Kelley tribute; source of the Obama guest-host detail and several lore-level claims (kept quarantined). https://www.congress.gov/congressional-record/volume-165/issue-46/senate-section/article/S1891-7
8. **Salim Muwakkil, "Black Talk Radio Sends Powerful, Clear Signals," In These Times, March 15–21, 1989** — key contemporary source: GM Hoyett Owens dates the talk switch to March 1986; Lu Palmer discusses the 1983 and 1987 Washington campaigns; documents mainstream monitoring of Black talk call-ins. https://www.unz.com/PDF/PERIODICAL/InTheseTimes-1989mar15/7-8/
9. **Melody Spann Cooper, written testimony, U.S. House Energy & Commerce Subcommittee on Innovation, Data, and Commerce, "Preserving Americans' Access to AM Radio," April 30, 2024** — primary owner testimony on current operations, ownership history, public-service and emergency functions. PDF archived at `/tmp/wvon/spann-cooper-testimony.pdf`. Hearing video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0dxUl6zpzqQ
10. **wvon.com** — station's own current site and self-history (accessed 2026-09-28). Secondary for history; primary for current self-presentation.
11. **The HistoryMakers Digital Library** — "WVON Radio Station Team on Stage" photograph (1960s, creator Lucky Cordell); Pervis Spann oral-history finding aid (A2002_010); Roland Martin finding aid (A2012_063). Access-restricted; used for name-spelling and roster confirmation only. https://dl.thehistorymakers.org/
12. **Final Call, March 10, 2011** — Farrakhan live-interview notice confirming the 1000 East 87th Street studio location. https://new.finalcall.com/2011/03/10/farrakhan-speaks-live-on-national-radio-to-discuss-national-and-world-events/

## Scholarly

13. **Jennifer Searcy, "The Voice of the Negro: African American Radio, WVON, and the Struggle for Civil Rights in Chicago" (PhD diss., Loyola University Chicago, 2012)** — the key scholarly source: full text obtained via Internet Archive capture of the Loyola eCommons record. https://ecommons.luc.edu/luc_diss/688/
14. **William Barlow, Voice Over: The Making of Black Radio (Temple University Press, 1999)** — cited via Ramsay for Spann's Freedom Summer work, South's Hotline, and King's WVON platform. Direct consultation deferred to CTL-DR-MEDIA-001.
15. **Edmund Ramsay, "WVON Radio and the Black Public Sphere" (DePaul University graduate paper)** — useful synthesis/theory; not a primary authority. Archived at `/tmp/wvon/depaul-wvon.pdf`. Cites Benton Foundation (2007), Herb Kent & David Smallwood (2009), Searcy, and the Chicago Tribune timeline.
