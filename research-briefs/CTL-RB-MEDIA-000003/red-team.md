## 2. Nostalgia check

- The brief's tone toward the 1968–1979 "production decade" is admiring; the red team confirms the counterweight is present: *Bird of the Iron Feather*'s unexplained funding loss (C-BIRD-2), the *Soul Train* move to Los Angeles, and the 1985 boycott are all foregrounded as failures, not footnotes.
- *Jubilee Showcase* is not romanticized: the "host paradox" (white host, Black performers, 21 years) is the brief's own analytical complication, stated in §09.
- Risk remains that the "three-year burst" framing (§07 finding 1) reads as a golden age. Mitigation: finding 3 (contested anchor desk) and finding 5 (ownership gap) are structurally adjacent; the era labels include "Backlash" and "Ownership Gap."

## 3. Local/national conflation check

- Bob Howard (CBS, 1948): explicitly quarantined as national context; Chicago-production link marked UNRESOLVED; never appears in the Chicago timeline or findings.
- *Soul Train* post-1971 (Los Angeles era): flagged as CTL-DR-MEDIA-001 scope; the brief covers only the 1970–1971 Chicago edition plus the Ghent local run.
- Burrell Communications' national campaigns: flagged as MEDIA-001; the brief's claim is limited to the firm's Chicago founding and its Chicago intervention in advertising images.
- Kerner-era national employment figures (<1%, via CJR): explicitly labeled national, not local, in §16.
- Smithsonian *Soul Train* objects: excluded from visuals because they postdate the Chicago period.

## 4. Missing-women check

- On-air women are covered: Simpson, Merri Dee, plus the Staple Singers/Emotions as performers; the 1990s generation (Hall, Robinson, Burns) is named as further research.
- **Gap admitted:** no Black women behind the camera or in station management were identified; advertising (Burrell) is male-led in the record. §20 lists this.
- Merri Dee's 1971 kidnapping/shooting is reported factually without sensationalism; her anchor career, not the violence, is the analytical point.

## 5. Behind-camera / editorial-control check

- Creative control is one of the five defined dimensions (§05), and the brief tracks it: Ordower (white control), Cornelius (Black ownership), Durham (Black authorship), Burrell (Black creative direction), Jovon (Black ownership).
- **Gap admitted:** rank-and-file technical/managerial employment by race is UNRESOLVED (C-UNK-1) — the brief states this is the weakest dimension and names the FCC/EEO research needed.
- The AAPB warning (Black public-affairs shows sometimes remained under white editorial control) is cited for *Our People* context; Tilmon's actual producer relationships are not established — not asserted.

## 6. Public/commercial distinction check

- WTTW (public) vs. WCIU/WSNS (independent commercial) vs. network affiliates is maintained throughout; finding 2 explicitly argues the public/independent stations carried the early load.
- *Jubilee Showcase* (commercial, ABC affiliate, white host) vs. *Our People* (public, Black host) are contrasted on the public/commercial and control axes — not conflated.
- WJYS programming trajectory (infomercials/paid religion) is used to argue ownership ≠ representation, a distinction the brief keeps visible.

- No AI-generated imagery. No hotlinked random images. No broadcast stills presented as cleared.
- **Videos:** all link-only; the directive's NEVER list was applied (rejected: Shorts/tributes of unknown provenance; full-episode uploads). The archivist-channel clip (V-V3) is included only with provenance caveat and link-only status.

## 8. Presentism check

- Period terminology ("Negro") appears only in quoted source text; the brief uses "Black" as the default with a terminology note (§05).
- The 1947–1967 era is not judged by later standards; the scarcity finding is comparative within the medium's early constraints (live TV, no preservation).
- The containment-vs-progress debate (§19) is presented as genuinely contested, not resolved in favor of the brief's narrative.
- Burrell's "positive realism" is described, not endorsed as today's standard.

## 9. Structural weaknesses the red team could not fix

1. **The 1940s–50s are thin.** Star Quest rests on one specialist source; Bob Howard is quarantined; the era boundary claims carry inference (C-ERA-1B is marked SUPPORTED-with-inference — arguably PROVISIONAL).
2. **Era labels are interpretive.** "The Black Production Decade" (1968–1979) foregrounds 1968–1970; 1971–1979 is quieter in the record. The ledger ties boundaries to dated claims, but a reviewer could reasonably move the 1979/1980 boundary.
3. **C-ERA-1B may be under-graded.** The "no sustained Black-produced programming before 1968" negative claim is the hardest to defend (absence of evidence). Consider downgrading to PROVISIONAL at next revision.
4. **Porterfield demotion details** rest heavily on obituaries and Adweek; the boycott's actual effect is UNRESOLVED, which somewhat deflates the era-3 narrative.
5. **No MBC item-level search was performed.** If the MBC holds major Black Chicago TV materials, the "archive is the story's weakest link" finding may need revision.

## 10. Verdict

The brief is publishable as **Research v0.1** with the graded ledger intact: no claim above its grade is used in the findings, all firsts are qualified, visuals are rights-honest, and the largest uncertainty (quantitative evidence and archival inventory) is stated up front in the executive summary. Recommended before v1.0: MBC holdings inventory, WTTW archive inquiry, and the Defender TV-coverage survey — any of which could move C-UNK-4, C-PROV-3, or C-ERA-1B.
