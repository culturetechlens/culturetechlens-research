# red-team.md — CTL-RB-MUS-000003 "The Black Chicago Record Store"

Adversarial review of the brief's evidence, grades, dates, rights, and scope
discipline. Conducted 2026-09-28.

## 1. Sourcing discipline

- **Single-secondary ≠ VERIFIED.** Enforced throughout. The SPIN 1986
  "Brainwash" 1,000-copy figure is participant testimony in contemporary
  journalism, not audited sales — capped at SUPPORTED (C18). Chip E.'s
  pressing account is likewise participant testimony (C19). Wikipedia claims
  are never a sole basis; Gramaphone's 1969 opening reaches VERIFIED only via
  NPR + Tribune corroboration (C21).
- **Archive-as-single-source.** The HistoryMakers finding aid is nearly the
  sole source for George's Music Room's internal functions (C01–C05, C52).
  It is institutional and primary, so VERIFIED is legitimate — but the brief
  is explicit about this concentration (§21) and flags city-directory and
  *Defender*-ad corroboration as next work.
- **Search excerpts.** The public-radio Billboard methodology piece and the
  Indiana digital-library Billboard piece were reached via search-result
  excerpts, not full text. Both are capped at SUPPORTED and flagged in the
  brief; full-text verification is logged as follow-up work.

## 2. Bias checks

- **Survivorship and name bias.** The best-documented cases (Gramaphone,
  Jazz Record Mart) survived longest; the 1980s failures Calkins documents
  left fewer records. The brief compensates with the quantitative study
  rather than pretending the case studies are representative.
- **Ownership vs. ecosystem.** The brief deliberately includes non-Black-owned
  stores (Gramaphone, Jazz Record Mart) that served Black musical ecosystems,
  and says so in §4 — avoiding the implication that only Black-owned stores
  counted, without diluting the Black-ownership thread.
- **Geography.** Gary, Indiana (Vee-Jay) is explicitly labeled a regional
  comparative case in every mention; it is never called a Chicago store.
- **Gender.** Vivian Carter's A&R role is foregrounded as a finding, not a
  sidebar; the DJ-pipeline narrative is male-heavy because the sources are —
  noted as a source-bias limit.

## 3. Myth and "first" claims

- **"House music" naming.** SPIN 1986 carries two competing accounts
  (Knuckles's Warehouse; Farley's imports). Graded DISPUTED (C42); the brief
  publishes no settled story.
- **Gramaphone "first of its kind."** Kept only as attributed contemporary
  characterization, SUPPORTED (C38), never as an audited market fact.
- **"Unsung hero of house music" (Joe Dale).** Kept as the store's own
  attributed characterization, not the brief's judgment (C35).

## 4. Date conflicts and name variants

- **George's Music Room closure:** 2010 (TRiiBE) vs. 2017 (IMDb
  documentary description). DISPUTED (C40); no unqualified date published.
- **Imports Etc. owner:** Weisburg vs. Weisberg. **Store name:** Importes,
  Etc. vs. Imports Etc. DISPUTED (C41); brief standardizes on the SPIN 1986
  contemporary spellings and flags variants.
- **National United Independent Retailers founding:** 1990 per the
  HistoryMakers finding-aid search result. The brief dates it to the archive,
  not to independent verification.
- **Russell Lee photo:** April 1941; reproduction number LC-USF33-012984-M5
  recorded from a scholarly figure caption, not from the LOC item page —
  the publication checklist requires item-page confirmation.

## 5. Rights

- **Visuals:** only I-1 (LOC FSA-OWI, public domain) is marked sourced among
  images; all other graphics are build-at-publish from CTL's own data.
  Internet photos of Gramaphone, George's Music Room, and Jazz Record Mart
  were rejected for unknown press-photo rights. No store ads or price lists
  are used (A-1 records the research lead as unavailable-with-reason pending
  publisher-archive clearance).
- **Videos:** V-1/V-2 are embeds of official-channel uploads, never
  downloads; full-length documentary reuploads of unknown provenance were
  rejected (see appendix table).
- **Lyrics:** none quoted.
- **Deceased personality:** Bob Koester (d. 2021) — Illinois publicity rights
  run 50 years post-mortem. No likeness used; no visual depends on his image.
  George Daniels is living; no post-mortem issue arises.

## 6. Duplicate / sibling-brief discipline

- CTL-RB-LIFE-000001 ("Saturday at the Record Store") owns everyday shopping
  experience; this brief excludes it in §4 and the claim ledger contains no
  experience claims.
- Sibling CTL-DR-MUSIC-001 / CTL-DR-BMI-002 / TRiiBE material is used only as
  graded leads (S28), never as VERIFIED, with caps documented per claim.

## 7. Grade audit

- VERIFIED: 17 — archive, institutional, government, or multi-source
  corroborated. Clean.
- SUPPORTED: 23 — journalism or specialist reporting; sales figures and
  single-account claims correctly capped.
- PROVISIONAL: 4 — indirect quotation (C45), later oral history (C43, C44),
  limited finding-aid detail (C46). None used as findings.
- DISPUTED: 3 — date conflict, name variants, naming-origin conflict.
- UNRESOLVED: 6 — including C48 (consumer credit), the brief's declared
  largest uncertainty, and C53 (form of artist appearances at George's
  Music Room).

## 8. Remaining risks

1. The consumer-credit gap (C48) means the brief's primary question is
   answered with a declared unknown — by design, per the accuracy-over-
   completeness rule, but it narrows the brief's completeness.
2. Heavy reliance on the HistoryMakers finding aid for the lead case.
3. The Imports Etc. case rests largely on one contemporary feature; address
   and lifespan remain unresolved.
4. Full-text verification of S15/S22/S27 excerpts is still open.
