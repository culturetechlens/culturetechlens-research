# sources.md — CTL-RB-MUS-000003 "The Black Chicago Record Store"

Labels: **PRIMARY** = archival, government, institutional, or contemporary first-hand records.
**SECONDARY** = scholarship, journalism, and reference works. Search snippets,
aggregators, AI summaries, and repeated internet claims are not evidence and
are not cited.

## PRIMARY

- **S01 — HistoryMakers, George Daniels oral-history finding aid (EAD A2007_156).**
  Archival. https://www.thehistorymakers.org/sites/default/files/A2007_156_EAD.pdf
  and https://www.thehistorymakers.org/sites/default/files/A2007_156_EAC.pdf.
  Establishes Daniels's Chess → Black-owned wholesaler → 1968 management →
  1969 ownership path; the store as artist promotional stop; benefit to
  surrounding North Lawndale businesses; youth mentorship; founding (1990) of
  the National United Independent Retailers.
- **S02 — HistoryMakers biography page, George Daniels.**
  Institutional. https://www.thehistorymakers.org/biography/george-daniels-41.
  Corroborates the career arc.
- **S03 — Delmark Records, "Celebrating Delmark Records Founder Bob Koester's
  Life & Legacy" (2021).** Institutional/label primary.
  https://delmark.com/2021/05/celebrating-delmark-records-founder-bob-koesters-life-legacy/.
  Establishes Delmark's 1953 founding (as "Delmar"), 1958 Chicago relocation,
  Koester's 1959 takeover of Seymour's Jazz Mart at 439 S. Wabash and renaming,
  the store's subsidy of the label, the 2016 closure (rising rent), and the
  2018 sale to Julia A. Miller.
- **S04 — Library of Congress, Prints & Photographs Division, FSA-OWI
  Collection.** Government/primary. Russell Lee, "Corner of Maxwell and
  Halsted Streets, Chicago," April 1941; reproduction number LC-USF33-012984-M5.
  Maxwell Street distribution-ecology baseline.
- **S05 — Library of Congress, FSA-OWI rights advisory and collection pages.**
  Government. https://www.loc.gov/rr/print/res/071_fsabf.html and
  https://www.loc.gov/rr/print/coll/052_fsa.html. FSA-OWI stills carry no known
  restrictions on publication.
- **S06 — Indiana public history (Indiana Historical Bureau blog; IPM
  Nightlights radio, 2019-09-11).** Public-history secondary based on
  documentary record. https://www.ipm.org/show/nightlights/2019-09-11/vee-jay-jazz-story
  and https://blog.history.in.gov/tag/the-spaniels/. Vee-Jay's 1953 founding by
  Gary, Indiana record-store owners Vivian Carter and James Bracken; Carter's
  DJ work; the store-as-A&R account.
- **S07 — SPIN, "Burning Down the House: Chicago Club 80s" (November 1986
  issue; republished 2014).** Contemporary journalism.
  https://www.spinmagazine.com/2014/04/burning-down-the-house-chicago-club-80s/.
  Imports Etc. (Printer's Row dance specialist, DJ customer base); Farley
  "Jackmaster" Funk's testimony; the 1,000-copy Telex "Brainwash" run; Chip E.'s
  Imports Etc. employment and store-to-pressing account; Hot Mix 5 demand
  creation; competing "house music" naming accounts.
- **S08 — Sway's Universe (YouTube), George Daniels interview (~2022).**
  Primary (interviewee's own speech). Video ID d2b45kFHOcA; see visuals.md
  V-1. Illustrates the label-vs.-independent-retail infrastructure claim.
- **S09 — Vocalo (YouTube), "Solid Sound: 50 Years of Gramaphone Records"
  (~2020).** Primary (documentary). Video ID jaAm3HgVFZ8; see visuals.md V-2.
  Illustrates the DJ-supply-house and employee-pipeline claims.

- **S21 — Third Coast Review, Chicago vinyl-shop directory.** Secondary

- **S22 — Indiana University, African American Music & Culture Digital
  Library.** Institutional archive.
  https://fedora.dlib.indiana.edu/fedora/get/iudl:2446933/OVERVIEW.

  Jerry Augustus / Hitsville co-op one-stop.
- **S27 — Block Club Chicago (2021-05-14), Koester obituary.** Reputable

  Julia A. Miller; Bob's Blues & Jazz Mart.
- **S28 — Sibling CTL research leads (CTL-DR-MUSIC-001, CTL-DR-BMI-002,

  Ora Nelle Maxwell Street storefront; Alligator/Living Blues/Flying Fish

## SCHOLARLY

- **S24 — Thomas Calkins, "More Than Sound: Record Stores in Majority Black
  Neighborhoods in Chicago, Milwaukee, and Detroit, 1970–2010," *City &
  Community* 18(3), 853–873, 2019. DOI 10.1111/cico.12433.** Peer-reviewed.
  Unequal failure odds (1980–1990); declining Black-neighborhood clustering.
  The brief's strongest quantitative source. (The public DOI is the canonical
  citation; a Sci-Hub copy was found but is not cited.)
- **S25 — Joshua Clark Davis, "For the Records: How African American
  Consumers and Music Retailers Created Commercial Public Space in the 1960s
  and 1970s South," *Southern Cultures* 17(4), 2011.** Peer-reviewed.
  ~1,000 Southern Black-owned stores at heyday; supplier-credit discrimination;
  community function. Southern context, used comparatively.
- **S23 — Amy Absher, *The Black Musician and the White City* (University of
  Michigan Press, 2014). DOI 10.3998/mpub.3974910.** Academic monograph.
  Jukebox R&B demand by 1954; adjacent infrastructure context.
- **S26 — Charles L. Hughes, *Country Soul* (UNC Press, 2015); UNC Press
  blog interview.** Scholarly secondary. Country-soul-era stores as
  multi-product, cross-genre spaces; comparative Southern context.
