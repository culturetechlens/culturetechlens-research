# Red Team — CTL-RB-MUS-000004 "Black Radio as Music Infrastructure"

This document attacks the brief before publication. Each item states the attack, the evidence state, and the disposition (kept / corrected / flagged / cut).

## 1. Legislative tributes masquerading as FCC primary evidence

**Attack:** The brief's three VERIFIED anchors are legislative tributes (Davis 2013, HR0201, Rush 2019) — retrospective, ceremonial, and potentially fed by station PR. They are not FCC licensing records.
**Disposition:** Honest about it. VERIFIED means "multiple independent authoritative sources in agreement," and the brief says so explicitly in the claim ledger and brief §22 (Limitations). The brief recommends FCC History Card retrieval as next research. The WVON launch date is nevertheless the best-anchored claim in the brief.

## 2. Circular station-history sourcing

**Attack:** Most "Broadcasting (1975)" etc. citations arrive via one anonymous station-history compiler. If that compiler misread or fabricated, the whole chain is corrupt.
**Disposition:** Mitigated, not eliminated. Claims that rest ONLY on the station histories are capped at SUPPORTED/PROVISIONAL; where two station histories cite the same trade item (e.g., WVON and WGRB both citing FCC Call Sign History for the 1983/1984 call changes), confidence rises. Direct retrieval of the trade pages is a scheduled next step.

## 3. The Motown "every song first" story

**Attack:** Berry Gordy supposedly sent every Motown song to WVON first. Three sources repeat it, but they may all descend from one station-lore source. It flatters WVON and is unverifiable in the stated form.
**Disposition:** Graded PROVISIONAL with an explicit "treat as attributed lore, not established fact" note. Kept in the brief as an illustration of the label→station link, with the grade visible.

## 4. Ratings and audience-percentage claims

**Attack:** 44–48% of Black listeners daily, 40% of households weekly (Searcy); Hot Mix 5's "2 million listeners"; WXOL's "50% audience increase"; the "1960 census 25%/1963 40% population" figures. None has an Arbitron book behind it in this brief.
**Disposition:** All capped at PROVISIONAL; the brief explicitly forbids publishing the Hot Mix 5 number and flags the Arbitron books as scheduled research. The "2 million listeners" figure is present in the ledger only to mark it as unverified.

## 5. "First" claims

**Attack:** "First Black talk station in Chicago" (HR0201); "first all-news station in the U.S." (WNUS); "first Black soap opera" (Durham). Every "first" is a hostage to someone else's archive.
**Disposition:** All kept as ATTRIBUTED claims ("described by X as..."), never as the brief's own assertion. The 1986 WVON "first Black talk" claim is attributed to the legislature.

## 6. The 1983 anachronism (BMI-002 red-team item 23)

**Attack:** Earlier drafts implied WVON's all-talk format powered Harold Washington's 1983 campaign. But the talk flip was 1986 — three years later.
**Disposition:** CORRECTED. The brief states the timeline plainly: Washington won in 1983 during WVON's music era; Lu Palmer's 10 p.m.–midnight commentary, not an all-talk format, was the electoral instrument. This is one of the brief's load-bearing distinctions.

## 7. The 1989 WGCI-AM talk debut vs. the 1986 WVON talk flip (BMI-002 red-team item 24)

**Attack:** The red team flagged a "1989 all-talk debut" vs. the "1986 WVON talk shift." Two different stations, two different owners — easily conflated.
**Disposition:** RESOLVED, and the red team was partly right to worry. 1986: WVON on 1450 AM (Midway) → Black talk. 1989–90: WGCI on 1390 AM (Gannett) → African-American talk, then dropped for "Dusty Radio." Both stay in the brief, disambiguated by frequency, owner, and call sign.

## 8. The 1969 decision vs. 1970 consummation

**Attack:** When did the Chess family actually sell WVON? Leonard Chess died October 1969; sources say the family "decided" to sell, and the sale to Globetrotter was "consummated in 1970."
**Disposition:** The brief reports both facts and marks the 1970 consummation SUPPORTED (not VERIFIED) pending trade-press confirmation.

## 9. The WXOL 1979 month discrepancy

**Attack:** Davis 2013 says WXOL premiered August 1979; the station history says both shared-time stations were on air "by October 1979." These conflict at the month level.
**Disposition:** FLAGGED in the ledger and the brief's timeline. Month-level precision is UNRESOLVED.

## 10. Marv Dyson / WJPC 1968 anachronism (BMI-002 red-team item 3)

**Attack:** BMI-002's entity text implied Dyson was president/GM of a station called WJPC from 1968 — but 950 AM was WGRT until November 1, 1973.
**Disposition:** CORRECTED. Dyson's HistoryMakers record is used only for what it verifies (hiring Doug Banks, giving Steve Harvey his radio break at WGCI). The brief never places Dyson at WJPC in 1968.

## 11. The Pervis Spann 1963 purchase claim (NEW)

**Attack:** The 2022 Rush tribute says Spann and South "purchased the station" on April 1, 1963 — contradicting the Chess purchase documented by three other legislative sources. The tribute is internally inconsistent.
**Disposition:** Graded DISPUTED and quarantined. The brief states the correct sequence (Chess 1963 → Globetrotter 1970 → Midway/WXOL 1979) and names the error explicitly. This is a new catch beyond BMI-002's red team.

## 12. The Oscar Brown Jr. anachronism (NEW)

**Attack:** Ellett quotes Brown saying he "took Negro Newsfront to WVON then WHFC" — but WVON didn't exist until 1963, and WHFC became WVON. The sequence only works as "WHFC then WVON."
**Disposition:** Graded DISPUTED. The brief notes the likely transposition/memory error and does not publish the quote as fact. New catch.

## 13. Celebrity/DJ bias — the missing people

**Attack:** The brief is about famous DJs and labels. Where are the women (secretaries, receptionists, traffic managers), the salespeople, the engineers, the advertising buyers, the church sponsors, the record-store workers, the ordinary listeners whose requests and purchases were the other half of the circuit?
**Disposition:** Acknowledged as a structural limitation (§21–22). Yvonne Daniels and LaDonna Tittle are named but thinly sourced; the brief's "What We Do Not Know" section lists the sales/labor side as the biggest evidential hole. Bernadine C. Washington ("Ambassador of Good Will") is named as an entry point for future research.

## 14. Survivorship bias toward famous stations

**Attack:** WVON dominates; WGES's 1940s block programmers, WBEE, WOPA, and the Gary/Hammond fringe get less. Stations that failed or left no fanbase leave no history.
**Disposition:** Partially mitigated — the brief deliberately opens with the 1929–1955 pre-history and gives WGES/WBEE real space. The dominance of WVON in the evidence is itself evidence of survivorship bias; the brief says so.

## 15. Causation inferred from airplay and sales

**Attack:** "Airplay → record sales" is the brief's core causal claim, but the evidence is largely correlational: the Motown story (lore), WXOL's blues-sales bump (single source), the Cooper record-economics mechanism (structural, not causal).
**Disposition:** The brief treats the CIRCUIT as structural (documented at each link) and flags causal magnitude as PROVISIONAL throughout. It does not claim airplay caused specific sales figures.

## 16. Publicity, copyright, and aircheck rights

**Attack:** Airchecks, station ads, and DJ photos are copyrighted or carry Illinois deceased-personality publicity rights (50 years post-death). Publishing them wrong is a legal and ethical risk.
**Disposition:** The brief's visuals policy (visuals.md) deliberately omitted all personality photography — documented as unavailable-with-reason — and treats airchecks as reference listening only, never embedded.

## 17. Duplicate station identities

**Attack:** WVON exists on 1450 (1963–75), then 1390 (1975–83), then 1450 again (1984–). WGRB/WGCI/WVON on 1390 are one facility. A reader can easily attribute 1983 events to the wrong call sign.
**Disposition:** The brief's frequency-tracked timeline (V7) and every section's "1450 AM" / "1390 AM" discipline are the countermeasure. entities.json and relationships.json use facility-scoped IDs.

## 18. Wikipedia-only claims inflating grades (BMI-002 red-team item 1)

**Attack:** BMI-002 warned that earlier work graded Wikipedia-only claims too high.
**Disposition:** Honored. In this brief, nothing is VERIFIED from a Wikipedia-family page alone; such pages are class-T source maps. Claims that would be Wikipedia-only (Hot Mix 5 details, Jackmasters) are PROVISIONAL.

## 19. What the red team could not break

The WVON 1963 launch (three legislative sources), the Lu Palmer 1983 correction (primary oral history + obituary + tributes), the 950 AM ownership chain (trade + Black press), and the 1390 AM lineage (trade + newspaper) all survived. The brief's central analytical claim — that Black radio in Chicago functioned as two-way infrastructure linking performers, labels, advertisers, and audiences — is structurally documented at every link even where individual causal magnitudes remain provisional.
