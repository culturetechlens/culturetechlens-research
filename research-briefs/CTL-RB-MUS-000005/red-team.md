# CTL-RB-MUS-000005 — Red Team Audit

**Brief:** "The Chicago Steppers Ecosystem" · **ID:** CTL-RB-MUS-000005 · **Date:** 2026-09-28

The red team attacked the brief for: unsupported claims, circular sourcing, myths as
facts, date conflicts, anachronisms, causation without evidence, selection/celebrity/
survivorship bias, missing women, missing ordinary people, copyright problems, and
duplicate/overlapping deliverable content.

## RT-1 — The Chatman origin story: naming vs. creating (celebrity/DJ bias) [BINDING]

**Attack:** The brief's narrative risks crediting Sam Chatman with the dance itself
because he is the only named, camera-ready origin figure. All sources say he *named*
it — the dance was created by unnamed teenagers in Chicago high schools and on
South/West Side streets (1971–75 New Bop). A "Godfather of Steppin'" frame can
silently convert a naming act into a creation act.

**Disposition:** The brief keeps the distinction explicit: Chatman named the dance;
teenagers made it. The People section names the anonymous creators as a collective
entity. No finding attributes choreography to Chatman. **Applied in brief.md.**

## RT-2 — Coining-year conflict: 1974 vs. 1960s [BINDING]

**Attack:** Wikipedia's 1974 date (single citation: WLS-TV 2022) collides with TRiiBE's
2026 "in the 1960s" and the community page's "mid-1970s." Presenting any one year as
fact manufactures precision.

**Disposition:** Recorded as DISPUTED (claim C004) with both positions and their
sources. The timeline visual (V3) renders the coining as a range bar, not a point.
**Applied.**

## RT-3 — "World's Largest Steppers Contest" — founding and hosting muddle [BINDING]

**Attack:** Three incompatible single sources: (a) YouTube interview title says
founder Rodney Burke; (b) TRiiBE says Pete Frazier/Majestic Gents hosted "for
decades"; (c) community thread says Herb Kent hosted an event "known as the World's
Largest Steppers Contest." Conflating these produces a false institutional history.

**Disposition:** Kept separate and graded: Frazier/Majestic Gents hosting = SUPPORTED;
Burke founding = PROVISIONAL (title-level); Kent hosting = PROVISIONAL (possibly
conflated with his steppers-set hosting). The brief's Events section narrates only
what the sources jointly establish. **Applied.**

## RT-4 — "Jeffree's 'Love's Gonna Last' was a national anthem" [BINDING]

**Attack:** "National anthem" reads as nationwide popularity. The record did not
chart nationally ("Love's Gonna Last" didn't chart; "Mr. Fix-It" reached #53 R&B).
The AllMusic/RadioInsight claim is precisely the opposite: a *local* record made
into an anthem by a *dance community*. Repeating "national anthem" without
qualification inverts the evidence.

**Disposition:** The brief states the mechanism explicitly — a dance community making
a record a (local) hit — and grades the honorific as community usage. **Applied.**

## RT-5 — R. Kelly causation: did 2003 "revive" or "expose" the dance? [BINDING]

**Attack:** Practitioner sources say R. Kelly "truly revived the form" and brought it
"into national mainstream culture." The dance never died in Chicago — it was
continuous. "Revive" erases thirty years of living practice; "mainstream exposure"
is the accurate frame. Additionally, celebrating Kelly's cultural role without
acknowledging his convictions would be a moral and editorial failure.

**Disposition:** The brief uses "national mainstream exposure/breakthrough," never
"revival," and handles Kelly as a factual music-history figure with the conviction
context named once and no celebratory framing. **Applied.**

## RT-6 — Love Jones / Herb Kent mangled claim [BINDING]

**Attack:** The community claim that Love Jones "depicts an event formerly hosted by
local Chicago DJ Herb Kent, known as 'The World's Largest Steppers Contest'" stacks
three assertions (film scene; Kent hosting; contest identity) on one forum thread.
Repeating it would launder a possibly conflated memory into fact.

**Disposition:** Film featuring stepping = PROVISIONAL; Kent hosting WLSC =
PROVISIONAL with explicit non-confirmation note; the three assertions are unbundled
in the ledger (C009, C035). **Applied.**

## RT-7 — Radio claims: WKKC-1975 and WVAZ-1989 (single-source tertiary) [BINDING]

**Attack:** Both claims appear only in uncited Wikipedia text repeated by community
copies — a classic circular-sourcing loop (Wikipedia ⇄ community pages quoting
Wikipedia). The frequency/call history is also unreconciled: community says
WBMX (102.7/1390AM) played Jeffree cuts in the late 1970s; Wikipedia says WVAZ
(102.7) began steppers programming in 1989 — same frequency, different calls.

**Disposition:** Both graded PROVISIONAL (C015, C016); the loop is named in the
ledger; the brief's Further Research calls for station-history sources (FCC files,
Kennedy-King archives). **Applied.**

## RT-8 — Circular sourcing check: pancocojams ⇄ Wikipedia [BINDING]

**Attack:** pancocojams reprints the full Wikipedia excerpt, and Wikipedia's 1974
date cites a WLS-TV segment that no one in this pass viewed directly. Risk: the
same sentence counted as two sources.

**Disposition:** Claims were graded on *provenance-diverse* corroboration only.
Where pancocojams added no independent source (e.g., the Wikipedia reprint), it was
not counted as a second source; the independent corroborant is the Society of
Boppers page or local journalism. **Applied throughout the ledger.**

## RT-9 — Missing women: the archive problem, named [BINDING]

**Attack:** The People section is male-DJ heavy because the *sources* are. Women who
do the load-bearing work (promoters like Black Mary; journalists like Samantha
Chatman; scholars like Anjulet Tucker; followers like Sherry Gordon and April
"Cincinnati Red" Burns; the Chicago Female Promoters collective) appear only in
quotes, listings, and one scholarly study. A brief that reproduces the sources'
gender skew without naming it commits survivorship bias.

**Disposition:** The brief makes this a finding (C036, VERIFIED as a finding about
the archive): named women are documented only in practitioner quotes, listings, and
scholarship — not in institutional archives or published histories. Mary Harris is
the People section's anchor precisely to counter DJ-centrism. **Applied.**

**Disposition:** The brief states this outright in Limitations and Analysis: the

## FINAL VERDICT

### WHAT THE EVIDENCE ESTABLISHES

- Chicago steppin' is a swing-family partner dance that evolved from the Chicago
  Bop (1940s–60s) through the 1970s New Bop; no published syllabus exists —
  transmission is oral, visual, and familial.
- Sam Chatman is widely and independently credited with naming the dance; the
  naming story (mic + flyers) is community-held oral history.
- The steppers' set is a weekly, formal, intergenerational social institution;
  the DJ is its curator and historian; the canon is chosen by dancers, not labels.
- The World's Largest Steppers Competition is a documented, recurring national
  institution: 35th annual, 2026-09-19, Tinley Park; longtime host/producer Pete
  Frazier (Majestic Gents); torch passed 2026 to Stephen "Snoopy" King.
- The practice has diffused from Chicago to documented outposts (Atlanta,
  Detroit, Houston, Los Angeles, New York City, Milwaukee, Cleveland, Dallas,
  Baton Rouge, others); Atlanta is the only outpost with scholarly documentation.

### WHAT THE EVIDENCE SUGGESTS

- Chatman named the dance around the mid-1970s; Mary Harris's 62-year promotion
  history is substantially true; the steppers circuit sustained outsize local
  life for specific records (Jeffree, "Hey Love," "Everybody Needs Love,"
  "My Baby Loves Me"); the dance reached national mainstream visibility via
  Love Jones (1997) and "Step in the Name of Love" (2003) without ever dying
  locally.

### WHAT REMAINS DISPUTED OR UNKNOWN

- The coining year (1974 vs. 1960s vs. mid-1970s); the WLSC's founder and
  founding year; the WKKC-1975 and WVAZ-1989 radio claims; whether Love Jones
  depicts a Herb Kent–hosted "World's Largest Steppers Contest"; the economic
  scale of the circuit; the names of any club workers; the status of Tucker's
  planned Southern Spaces article; the full lineage of steppers DJs.

### WHAT CTL SHOULD RESEARCH NEXT

1. Oral-history capture on community terms — Mary Harris's circle, women
   promoters/instructors, club workers, 1970s steppers DJs — before the memory
   is lost.
2. Chicago Defender archive retrieval for 1960s–80s set documentation (highest-
   value primary-source target).
3. Station-record verification (WKKC/WVAZ) and a Lixenberg licensing inquiry.
4. Regional comparative histories (Detroit ballroom, D.C. hand dance, Texas
   swing-out) to test the Chicago-diffusion frame.
