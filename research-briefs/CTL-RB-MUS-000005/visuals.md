## V1 — Neighborhood context: South Side, October 1973

- **Type:** Sourced photograph.
- **Status:** SOURCED.
- **Image:** John H. White, "Black youngsters performing on an empty lot at 5440 South
  Princeton Avenue," Chicago, Illinois, October 1973 (DOCUMERICA series).
- **Creator:** John H. White (Pulitzer-winning photographer; DOCUMERICA commission,
  Environmental Protection Agency, 1973–74).
- **Date:** October 1973.
- **Source institution:** U.S. National Archives and Records Administration (NARA),
  DOCUMERICA set, via the official NARA Flickr set:
  https://www.flickr.com/photos/usnationalarchives/sets/72157633309290525
- **Rights:** Public domain — U.S. federal government work; NARA's own statement for
  this material: no known copyright restrictions. Attribution: "John H. White,
  U.S. National Archives (DOCUMERICA)."
- **Caption:** "Kids performing on an empty lot at 5440 South Princeton Avenue,
  Chicago's South Side, October 1973 — the neighborhood stage where the bop-to-steppin'
  generation grew up."
- **Context / why this visual:** The brief's Scope anchors stepping's formation on the
  South and West Sides in the 1940s–70s (claim C029). White's South Side DOCUMERICA
  work is the closest rights-cleared photographic record of the actual streets and
  vacant-lot social space that raised the stepping generation. Selected over
  performer portraits to respect Illinois' 50-year post-mortem publicity-rights rule
  and to center ordinary people.
- **Note:** Select the exact file from the NARA set at publish time; the caption above
  is taken verbatim from the NARA set listing.
