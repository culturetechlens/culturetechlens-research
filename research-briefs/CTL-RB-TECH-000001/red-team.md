# Red Team — CTL-RB-TECH-000001

**Culture, Clearly Seen. · Black Cultural Intelligence**

Brief: *The Technology Behind Chicago House* · v0.1 (2026-09-28)

Purpose: attack this brief's claims, sourcing, framing, and gaps. Every item below is either resolved (with the fix recorded) or carried as an open risk.

## A. Attacks on claims

**RT-1 — "Detroit trio Phuture."** A MusicRadar TB-303 guide used in research calls Phuture a "Detroit trio." Phuture is Chicago. *Resolution:* the article (S-35) is cited ONLY for the TB-303 production figure; it is not trusted for any Chicago fact, and the error is documented in sources.md. Lesson recorded: specialist music copy must be verified against practitioner sources.

**RT-2 — The "$40 303" price.** The ~$40 figure is DJ Pierre's own quote — strong as a quote, single-source as a market fact. *Resolution:* claim C-016 is graded VERIFIED as a quote, SUPPORTED as a price fact; the brief never generalizes from it to "303s cost $40."

**RT-3 — "Yamaha 284."** No well-known Yamaha product matches this name; it appears only in Wikipedia-derived sources. Could be a misremembered model, a store nickname, or a transcription error. *Resolution:* graded PROVISIONAL (C-033); the brief flags it and never repeats it unflagged. The reconciling 2023 masterclass account (JX-8P/TR-707/Prophet 2000) is carried as SUPPORTED with the conflict recorded (C-D3).

**RT-4 — 40→122 BPM.** Exact tempo figures from one secondary source. *Resolution:* technique VERIFIED (C-015); figures PROVISIONAL (C-034). The brief separates them.

**RT-5 — Sale vs. gift of the 909.** Fowlkes says sold (rent distress); Knuckles' telling sounds like a gift. *Resolution:* DISPUTED on terms (C-D2), VERIFIED on transfer. Both framings recorded; the brief does not pick one.

**RT-6 — TB-303 production total.** 10,000 vs 20,000. *Resolution:* DISPUTED (C-D1); the brief uses the range and states "small run, commercial failure" (VERIFIED) as the load-bearing fact.

**RT-7 — "On & On" first-club-test date.** Bidder 2001 says "late 1984" for the Playground debut; the record was released early 1984. A club test *after* release is not a club test. *Resolution:* the date is UNRESOLVED (C-U5); the brief claims only first-pressed (qualified) and shows the gap in the disambiguation graphic.

**RT-8 — Did Knuckles really run the 909 "under every record"?** This is Knuckles' own 2012 recollection of a mid-80s night, ~25 years later. Memory compresses. *Resolution:* the quote is VERIFIED as his account; the brief frames it as his described practice, not as an independently witnessed fact. The engineering plausibility (909 → mixer aux/line in) is uncontested.

**RT-9 — Roland's promotional interest.** Roland's own articles are a key source for the "Acid Tracks" narrative; Roland benefits from the acid mythos. *Resolution:* Roland-sourced Chicago-event claims are graded SUPPORTED (C-021), never VERIFIED alone; chronology is corroborated with MusicRadar and Wikipedia.

**RT-10 — "Acid Tracks" slowed 130→120.** Single-thread account (Jefferson via Wikipedia). *Resolution:* SUPPORTED (C-020), not VERIFIED.

## B. Attacks on sourcing

**RT-11 — Wikipedia dependence.** ~12 Wikipedia citations. *Resolution:* Wikipedia is T3 in this brief — used for chronology and as a pointer; no VERIFIED claim rests on Wikipedia alone. Each consequential fact is corroborated with T1/T2 or downgraded. This is documented in §23 and §21.

**RT-12 — No contemporaneous documents.** No studio logs, receipts, equipment inventories, or 1980s-dated interviews were found. The entire economics section rests on retrospective practitioner accounts. *Resolution:* stated as a limitation (§21); the "Further Research" section scopes document recovery for CTL-DR-TECH-001. Grades reflect this: economics claims are SUPPORTED, not VERIFIED.

**RT-13 — Japanese-language sources not consulted.** The inventor-side evidence is English-language only. *Resolution:* stated in §21; device-history deep research scoped for TECH-001 (patents, service manuals, Japanese engineering sources).

**RT-14 — Video as illustration, not evidence.** The two embedded videos come from official channels (RBMA, Roland) and were assessed via title/description/oEmbed metadata — not watched end-to-end, and upload dates are estimates. *Resolution:* videos illustrate practitioner accounts already in the claim ledger; neither is the sole evidence for any claim (visuals.md states this explicitly). Transcript-level review and embed re-check are required at publication.

## C. Attacks on framing

**RT-15 — Technological determinism risk.** "The machines made house" is the lazy reading of this brief. *Resolution:* §19 explicitly rejects it; the thesis is affordance + operator ingenuity; the mandatory sentence ("Roland Corporation invented the TB-303; Chicago producers culturally transformed it") appears verbatim in the Key Findings and the Final Verdict.

**RT-16 — Invention/adoption slippage.** Any phrasing like "Chicago's 303" could imply invention. *Resolution:* role tags on every person/organization entity; "cultural transformer" used consistently; relationships.json uses "culturally-transformed," never "invented," for Chicago actors.

**RT-17 — "Accident" narrative.** The $40-machine-found-by-chance story can read as "acid house was an accident," which erases skill. *Resolution:* §19 rejects the accident framing; the brief treats the first encounter as serendipitous and everything after as skilled iterative production (C-036 PROVISIONAL on the exact boundary).

**RT-18 — Gender gap.** The sources document no women as machine operators, engineers, or technicians in this story. This reflects the source set's bias, not necessarily the historical record. *Resolution:* carried as UNRESOLVED (C-U4) and flagged in §20.7 and §22.4 for targeted oral-history research. The brief does not manufacture inclusion.

**RT-19 — Survivorship bias.** Only pressed/surviving records leave machine evidence; tape-only and unpressed work is invisible. *Resolution:* stated in §21; the tape-economy analysis (§09) explicitly argues the vinyl record undercounts production.

**RT-20 — The sampler marginality claim.** "Samplers were marginal" (§07.7) is an absence-of-evidence claim about 1984–87 Chicago. *Resolution:* the brief names the one documented sampler (Prophet 2000, SUPPORTED) and scopes the SP-12/SP-1200/MPC-60 chapter for TECH-001 (§22.5). The claim is bounded to the documented record set, not to all Chicago production.

## D. Attacks on completeness

**RT-21 — Mixer models unknown.** A technology brief that cannot name the mixers is incomplete. *Resolution:* carried as the #1 UNRESOLVED item (C-U1); §22.6 scopes booth archaeology. The brief documents mixer *function* (909 wired in; Hardy's rig) without inventing models.

**RT-22 — No SL-1200-in-Chicago documentation.** The turntable's Chicago club presence is asserted from general DJ history, not a Chicago-specific source. *Resolution:* the device facts are VERIFIED; Chicago-specific installation claims are SUPPORTED at most (C-031), and the brief does not claim model-level booth inventories.

**RT-23 — Trax economics one-sided.** The bankruptcy and criticism are documented; Trax's defense (if any) is not in the sources. *Resolution:* SUPPORTED grading (C-029); the brief reports the criticism as reported, not as adjudicated.
