# Sources — CTL-RB-TECH-000002 "Black Chicago Goes Online"
**Version:** v0.1 (2026-09-28). Primary vs secondary labeled. Every URL retrieved or searched 2026-09-28.

## PRIMARY

1. U.S. National Telecommunications and Information Administration, *Falling Through the Net II: New Data on the Digital Divide* (1997 data). https://www.ntia.gov/report/1998/falling-through-net-ii-new-data-digital-divide — national household computer/online-access figures by race; basis for CL-009.
2. U.S. NTIA *Falling Through the Net* series, 1995–1999 (summary via Clinton Presidential Library holdings). https://clinton.presidentiallibraries.us/files/original/87ebaf75eda070c0d42bf4e383835832.pdf — Black/Hispanic households ~two-fifths as likely as White households to have home Internet; central-city, low-income, less-educated groups lagged.
3. *Editor & Publisher*, "Chicago Defender Long a Web Holdout, to Launch a Podcast" (2005). https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/chicago-defender-long-a-web-holdout-to-launch-a-podcast,111946 — contemporary trade press; dates Defender site launch to December 2004 (CL-001), podcast to ~May 2005 centennial (CL-002).
4. *Editor & Publisher*, "Chicago Defender… It's Unapologetically Black, No More" (retrospective). https://www.editorandpublisher.com/stories/chicago-defender-its-unapologetically-black-no-more,81889 — corroborates the Defender had no website until Roland S. Martin's 2004 arrival.
5. The Final Call, institutional history pages (finalcall.com). https://finalcall.com/artman/publish/aboutus/aboutus.shtml ; https://www.finalcall.com/artman/publish/National_News_2/article_101176.shtml ; https://new.finalcall.com/2010/02/09/the-final-call-newspaper-continuing-a-tradition/ — dates Online Edition start to the 1995 Million Man March (CL-003). Institutional retrospective, not contemporary pages.
6. Jason Scott, *BBS: The Documentary* (2005), 8 episodes, released under Creative Commons BY-SA. Director's YouTube channel: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dddbe9OuJLU (Ep. 1, BAUD) ; official playlist https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL7nj3G6Jpv2G6Gp6NvN1kUtQuW8QshBWE — archival interviews including Christensen and Suess on the Chicago 1978 first BBS (CL-007).

7. Charlton D. McIlwain, *Black Software: The Internet & Racial Justice, from the AfroNet to Black Lives Matter* (Oxford University Press, 2019) — secondary scholarship documenting AfroLink (1990) and AfroNet (1993); the 5.2-million-Black-people-online-in-1995 figure.
8. Lori Emerson, "Excavating Future Histories of the Internet: AfroNet Newsletter Telegraph" (2021). http://loriemerson.net/2021/04/12/excavating-future-histories-of-the-internet-afronet-newsletter-telegraph/
9. *Canadian Journal of Communication*, review of *Black Software* (2022). https://cjc.utppublishing.com/doi/full/10.3138/cjc.2022-0032?cookieSet=1
10. Marketplace, "How the history of Blackness on the internet was erased" (2021). https://www.marketplace.org/episode/2021/02/10/how-the-history-of-blackness-on-the-internet-was-erased
11. J. Nathan Matias, "What's the genealogy of black technology in the US? Charlton McIlwain at Strand" (2019). https://natematias.medium.com/whats-the-genealogy-of-black-technology-in-the-us-charlton-mcilwain-at-strand-2064fe487120?source=rss-61f90df70e11------2
12. Nicholas Rougeux, "Obama's online evolution" (c82.net blog). https://www.c82.net/blog/?id=53 — Wayback-based enthusiast retrospective; dates Obama for Illinois v1 to early 2003 (CL-004, CONJECTURAL).
13. Brookings, "The Digital Divide: Bridging the Divide Naturally." https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-digital-divide-bridging-the-divide-naturally/ — CPS/NTIA-derived 1994/1998 national figures (CL-008, CL-010).
14. Data & Society, "Black Software" (Databites event page). https://datasociety.net/library/black-software/ — official page embedding McIlwain's talk; used for video selection V3.
