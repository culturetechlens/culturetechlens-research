## 1. Research Question (file level)

What can CTL responsibly establish about Johnson Publishing Company
(1942–2019) — its founding, operations, publications, products, people,
places, decline, and archival afterlife — and which widely repeated claims
about it cannot be substantiated?

## Per-claim records

### EF-C-001 — Founding: November 1942, Chicago, by John H. Johnson

2. **Claim.** Johnson Publishing Company was founded in Chicago,
Illinois, in November 1942 by John H. Johnson, originally as the Negro
Digest Publishing Company.
3. **Evidence For ↔ Evidence Against.** FOR: African American Registry
(founded November 1942); Congressional Record vol. 147 (2001 tribute);
Congressional Record vol. 149 (2003 tribute); AP obituary (2005); Johnson,
*Succeeding Against the Odds* (1992); Grolier Club exhibition (Vol. 1,
Nos. 1–12, November 1942–October 1943, masthead "Negro Digest Publishing
Co."). AGAINST: none found — no source disputes the month, city, or
founder. Searched: company histories, obituaries, the audit's duplicates.
4. **Primary + Secondary Sources.** PRIMARY: Johnson's autobiography;
Congressional Record tributes; *Negro Digest* Vol. 1 masthead (via
Grolier). SECONDARY: AA Registry; AP obituary; CTL-DR-BPM-003 (co-jpc,
Verified); Encyclopedia.com.
5. **Source Evaluation.** The autobiography is first-person primary but
self-interested on business details; here it is corroborated by
contemporary government records (Congressional Record) and the physical
masthead evidence. The Grolier masthead is independent physical
corroboration of the company name and the November 1942 start.
6. **Corroboration.** Autobiography, two Congressional tributes (2001,
2003, different speakers), AA Registry, and the Grolier masthead
corroborate each other independently; the masthead is the only
source-independent physical evidence and it anchors the month/year.
7. **Conflicts/Contradictions.** None.
8. **Confidence Classification.** VERIFIED — multiple independent
sources, primary and strong secondary; no counterevidence.
9. **CTL Finding.** JPC was founded in Chicago in November 1942 by John
H. Johnson, originally as the Negro Digest Publishing Company.
10. **What would change it.** Articles of incorporation or Illinois
Secretary of State records showing a different legal founding date would
narrow or revise the claim.

### EF-C-003 — The $500-loan origin narrative

2. **Claim.** The founding was financed by a $500 loan secured by
Johnson's mother's furniture, because banks would not lend to Black
borrowers.
3. **Evidence For ↔ Evidence Against.** FOR: Johnson's autobiography;
Congressional Record vol. 149 ("$500 seed money… mother pawned their
furniture"); AA Registry ("mama used as collateral"); BPM-003 (verified
as the origin narrative). AGAINST: no source disputes the $500 or the
collateral; no source corroborates the lender's identity (see EF-C-051).
Searched: audit records; loan documents not located.
4. **Primary + Secondary Sources.** PRIMARY: Johnson's autobiography;
Congressional tributes. SECONDARY: AA Registry; BPM-003.
5. **Source Evaluation.** This is the founder's own account — primary but
self-reported, and self-serving founding myths are common in business
history. It gains weight from repetition in Congressional tributes and
from internal consistency (the bank-refusal explanation matches
documented 1940s lending discrimination; the 820 S. Michigan financing
difficulties, EF-C-031, corroborate the pattern).
6. **Corroboration.** The Congressional Record and AA Registry repeat
the narrative but derive from Johnson's telling — they corroborate its
stability as an account, not its independent truth. No fully independent
corroboration (bank records, loan documents) has been found.
7. **Conflicts/Contradictions.** None on the $500 or the furniture
collateral; the lender's name is uncorroborated (EF-C-051).
8. **Confidence Classification.** VERIFIED — graded as the documented
origin narrative: multiple independent publications, one primary
(founder) source, no counterevidence. The uncertainty is marked on the
lender identity, not the loan itself.
9. **CTL Finding.** JPC's founding rests on the $500 loan secured by
Johnson's mother's furniture, in a credit market closed to Black
borrowers — Johnson's account, repeated in Congressional tributes, with
no contradictory evidence.
10. **What would change it.** Located loan documents or bank records
from 1942 would upgrade the account to independently verified fact or
revise its details.
