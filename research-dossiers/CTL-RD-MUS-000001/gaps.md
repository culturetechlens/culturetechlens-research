# CTL-RD-MUS-000001 — Gaps

Appendix D gap register. Ordered by impact on the dossier's core claims.

## 1. MCA purchase agreement (LARGEST GAP)

The 1985/All Platinum vs 1986/Sugar Hill dispute (EF-031/CLM-031) cannot be resolved without the purchase agreement or a contemporaneous corporate filing. This is the dossier's largest gap because it sits on the catalog chain — the thing CTL's knowledge graph must get right. Next step: MCA/UMG corporate archives; SEC filings for MCA Inc. 1985–86; Sugar Hill Records Ltd. corporate records.

## 2. GRT purchase agreement

The $6.5M + 20,000-share terms are VERIFIED on the Landmark report plus press-release reprint, but the agreement itself would clarify what "Chess" comprised (masters, publishing, pressing plant, real estate) and the Chess brothers' exact retained roles. Next step: GRT corporate records; 1969 trade-press coverage.

## 3. GRT→All Platinum contract and 1975 asset inventory

What All Platinum actually bought; what was destroyed vs. sold vs. lost in the 1975 wind-down. Bears directly on the provisional mass-destruction finding (EF-042). Next step: All Platinum business records; contemporary trade press 1975–76.

## 4. Arc Music founding and corporate records

Founder split (EF-003), the Goodman role (EF-016), and the royalty-accounting practices behind the 1977 suits. Next step: BMI repertoire records; New York corporate filings; the 1977 suit dockets.

## 5. AFM session logs and session contracts

Would surface women workers, ordinary staff, session economics, and the true scale of the 1960s session band — the corrective to the missing-women and celebrity-bias findings. Next step: AFM Local 10-208 (Chicago) archives.

## 6. 1975 dismantling / master-tape survival accounting

What masters survived the GRT wind-down, where they went, and what the 2008 fire actually destroyed (EF-043). Next step: UMG vault inventory (if ever released); GRT accounting records.

## 7. Macomba Lounge contemporary address evidence

Resolves the Cottage Grove vs Drexel conflict (EF-005). Next step: 1946–1950 Chicago city directories; liquor-license records; Sanborn maps.

## 8. Aristocrat founder-split documentation

The 40/40/20 vs 50/50 question (EF-003). Next step: Aristocrat corporate filings; Evelyn Aron's distributing-company records.

## 9. Stan Lewis deal documentation

The 1954 production deal rests on one contemporary Cash Box citation via Campbell (EF-011). Next step: Cash Box archives; Lewis family/business records (Stan's Record Shop, Shreveport).

## 10. Etta James and roster contract detail

Contract terms, tenure dates, and royalty arrangements for core artists beyond the litigated cases. Next step: artist estate papers; UMG contract archives.

## 11. Ralph Bass hire year

Landmark (1958) vs Rock Hall (1960). Next step: Chess payroll/employment records; Bass's own papers.

## 12. 320 E. 21st building history

Purchase terms, factory layout, pressing-plant capacity, the claimed 24-hour performance-to-shop cycle. Next step: Chicago building permits; GRT real-estate records.

## Research posture

None of these gaps block the dossier's core claims — founding, lineage, business model, GRT sale, litigation outcomes, and the three refuted myths are all on solid ground. The gaps cluster exactly where the dossier says they do: the money (contracts, royalties, inventories) and the workers (session logs, women, ordinary staff). That clustering is itself a finding about what the archive preserves and what it discards.
