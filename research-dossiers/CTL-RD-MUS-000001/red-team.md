## Celebrity bias

The people section deliberately names Malcolm Chisholm, Ron Malo, Buster Williams, Andrew Tibbs, Gerald Sims, and Tom Archia alongside the marquee artists — the ordinary workers the marquee narrative erases. Session band members (Satterfield, Cosey, Upchurch, Caston, Thompson) are named where sources allow.

## Survivorship bias

We see the hits — Maybellene, the trunk-sale success stories — not the records that never sold, the distributors who never paid, the artists Chess passed on. The dossier does not infer business health from chart success; the 1966 corporate move and the 1969 sale are narrated as strategy, not triumph.

## Catalog-chain precision

Organizations (labels) and buildings (2120, 320) are separate entities throughout. "Chess Records" as a post-1975 label credit is identified as a reissue credit, not the operating company.

## Adversarial questions we asked ourselves

1. *Is the "independent distribution" story overstated — did Chess depend on majors?* The trunk-sale model is well documented (Landmark), but the majors' distribution role in Chess's later years is under-researched. Flagged as a gap, not a claim.
2. *Does the dossier center British rock too much?* §12 carries MUSIC-001's standing rule: narrate the 1964 Stones sessions with care, centering the Chicago creators.
3. *Could the MCA dispute be an artifact of label-credit confusion?* Possible — 45-sleeves scans show "Sugar Hill" credits; tsimon/Springer say All Platinum sold to MCA. The purchase agreement would resolve it. Both sides stay live.
4. *Are we over-weighting the Landmark report?* It is a municipal document with primary-material backing, but it is still one synthesis. Date disputes were adjudicated with contemporary trade press where available (Ter-Mar), left open where not (Bass).
