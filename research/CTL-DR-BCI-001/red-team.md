# CTL-DR-BCI-001 — Red-Team Review

_Independent adversarial review of the research corpus (tracks A–D), 2026-09-28._

## Unsupported claims (claims graded Verified that lack adequate evidence) — severity: HIGH

- b-stmark (St. Mark UMC): both founding claims and the 'largest African-American church in the Northern Illinois Conference' superlative are graded Verified with source_type 'primary,' but the evidence is only the church's own website plus an Arcadia book synopsis. A self-reported superlative should not be graded Verified/primary; it is at best Provisional.
- b-quinn (Quinn Chapel): the Underground Railroad station claim is graded Verified, but the claim's own uncertainty note admits documentation 'rests on congressional tribute and LOC survey rather than primary 1850s documents.' That is a Provisional claim mis-graded as Verified.
- b-defender: 'Abbott used Pullman porters... the paper is credited as a major catalyst of the Great Migration' is graded Verified while the uncertainty note acknowledges 'historians debate the relative weight of the Defender versus labor demand.' The Defender's role is consensus but the causal weight is contested; Verified overstates certainty.
- b-olivet / b-jackson: the 'membership rose to exceed 20,000' claim is graded Verified with source_type 'primary,' but the actual source is a Chicago Historical Society photo caption citing church publications — a tertiary relay of an institutional self-report, not a primary source.
- b-delisa: 'With the Regal Theater and Rhumboogie Cafe, the ~1,000-seat Club DeLisa played a [central role]' is graded Verified citing only Alchetron — a Wikipedia mirror. One circular source cannot sustain a Verified grade.
- b-wgci: 'WGCI slowly took listenership from WVON' is graded Verified citing Alchetron plus Wikipedia. Market-share claims need ratings data (Nielsen/Arbitron archives), not encyclopedia text.
- d-austin: 'Austin is Chicago's third-largest community area by total population' graded Verified citing only Wikipedia ('Austin, Chicago').
- d-eighth-regiment-armory: 'first [Black] armory in the United States' superlative graded Verified citing only Wikipedia.
- a/bronzeville: the 'Black Metropolis... largely self-sufficient district' claim graded Verified rests on MasterClass (a commercial content site), nar.realtor (2026), and NPR. The framing is reasonable but 'self-sufficient' is an interpretive claim resting on popular sources.
- ibpoew (track c): founding details graded Verified rest on EsperStamps.org (a philatelic first-day-cover text) and a SlideShare presentation — both weak single sources for a Verified grade.
- c/d Chicago (IL) Chapter of The Links 1950 founding is correctly graded Provisional (single-source, chapter's own site) — credit for honest grading.

Affected: b-stmark, b-quinn, b-defender, b-olivet, b-jackson, b-delisa, b-wgci, d-austin, d-eighth-regiment-armory, bronzeville, ibpoew

**Recommendation:** Downgrade the mis-graded claims to Provisional, add the missing counterweight sources (porters' own accounts for the Defender distribution claim; HABS/congregational records for Quinn), and replace Wikipedia-mirror citations (Alchetron) with independent sources or drop the claims.

## Missing communities (Black Chicago communities/populations absent or thin) — severity: HIGH

- Black Muslims are entirely absent: the Nation of Islam was headquartered in Chicago for decades under Elijah Muhammad (Mosque Maryam in Chatham, Muhammad Speaks newspaper), and the Moorish Science Temple of America was founded in Chicago in 1925 by Noble Drew Ali. Two Chicago-born religious movements with national impact have zero entities.
- No Black Catholic presence: Holy Angels, St. Elizabeth, and St. Anselm parishes; Fr. Augustus Tolton (first Black Catholic priest in the US, with Chicago ministry) — absent.
- No African immigrant communities: Nigerian, Ghanaian, and Ethiopian Chicagoans (concentrated in Rogers Park, Uptown, and the suburbs) — absent, making the project read as if Black Chicago ends in the 1980s.
- No Afro-Caribbean presence (Haitian, Jamaican communities) — absent.
- No Black LGBTQ+ history: house music was born in Black gay clubs (the Warehouse, Frankie Knuckles, Jesse Saunders); the entire house-music origin story is missing even though music venues are otherwise well covered.
- No coverage of the Black suburban migration (Robbins, Harvey, Markham, Matteson) — the post-1970 outflow is documented demographically in track-d but no community entities exist there.
- No disabled Black Chicagoans; no Black deaf community (e.g., Chicago's Black deaf clubs).

**Recommendation:** Add entities for the Nation of Islam/Mosque Maryam, Moorish Science Temple, Holy Angels/St. Elizabeth parishes, the Warehouse/house-music origins, and at least one African-immigrant community profile before public launch; these are conspicuous absences in a project subtitled 'The Infrastructure of Black Chicago.'

## Geographic bias (South Side vs West Side vs other areas) — severity: HIGH

- Of 100 entities, 68 are coded South Side, 14 Loop/downtown, 11 West Side, 2 far South (Chicago State, Roseland), 1 Southwest (Marquette Park), 1 outside the city (Vee-Jay Records, Gary Indiana), 3 citywide.
- Track-b (cultural/knowledge institutions) has ZERO West Side entities: all 25 entities are South Side or downtown. No West Side churches, no West Side music venues, no West Side schools — despite the West Side holding the city's second Black population center.
- West Side coverage is confined to: North Lawndale (3 mentions across tracks), East/West Garfield Park (track-d neighborhood entries only), Austin (track-d neighborhood entry only), and the MLK Hamlin Avenue residence (track-d). No West Side cultural, economic, or religious institutions at all.
- Entirely missing geographies: Rogers Park, South Shore, Beverly/Morgan Park, Pullman, Bridgeport-adjacent Black communities, Northwest Side, Near West Side, and the south suburbs receiving Black out-migration.
- Track-c (economic/civic) is 23 of 25 South Side — every bank, insurer, and civic org is South Side; no West Side Black business corridor (e.g., Madison Street, Madison-Pulaski) is represented.

Affected: b-olivet, b-quinn, b-stmark, b-dusable, b-phillips, b-wvon, b-wgci, b-soultrain, b-regal, b-delisa, b-checkerboard, b-chess-records, jesse-binga, anthony-overton, supreme-life, provident-hospital

**Recommendation:** Commission a West Side institutions pass (churches, Madison Street corridor businesses, West Side arts: e.g., the West Side's jazz/blues clubs, churches like Greater Harvest or First Baptist Congregational's West Side history) and add South Shore, Beverly, and Rogers Park neighborhood entities to track-d.

## Institutional bias (big institutions crowding out small/informal ones) — severity: MEDIUM

- Flagship institutions dominate: DuSable Museum (2 entities), Chicago Defender (2 entities + building), Johnson Publishing, Quinn Chapel, Olivet, CHA, Chicago State, the major banks. Small/informal institutions are limited to Club DeLisa, Checkerboard Lounge, eta Creative Arts Foundation, and the Bronzeville Children's Museum.
- Entire categories of everyday Black institutions are missing: funeral homes (A.A. Rayner, Leak & Sons — historically central Black businesses), beauty schools (Poro College), barbershops, block clubs, social and athletic clubs, Black-owned taverns/lounges beyond the three famous music clubs, and storefront/independent churches.
- Churches: only 3 large historic congregations (Olivet, Quinn, St. Mark). Dozens of significant others (Liberty Baptist, Providence Baptist, First Church of Deliverance, Pilgrim Baptist — birthplace of gospel music under Thomas Dorsey) are absent; Pilgrim Baptist's gospel-music significance is a major omission in a track covering music venues.
- Only one small press (Chicago Bee, mentioned in passing inside a/bronzeville); the Chicago Whip (founded 1919 by Joseph Bibb) is missing entirely.

Affected: dusable-museum, d-dusable, defender, b-defender, b-jpc, b-quinn, b-olivet, b-stmark

**Recommendation:** Add entities for Poro College, one funeral home, one block-club network, Pilgrim Baptist Church (gospel music), and the Chicago Whip before launch; demote nothing but weight the flagship entries against the everyday ones in the synthesis.

## Survivorship bias (surviving institutions vs lost ones) — severity: LOW

- Credit: tracks handle several lost institutions honestly — Binga Bank (failed 1930), Douglass National Bank (failed 1932), Seaway National Bank (failed 2017), the Robert Taylor Homes/Stateway/Wells demolitions, the ANP (defunct), and the Regal Theater (demolished) all have entities.
- But lost institutions get thinner treatment: Club DeLisa (closed) has 1 thin claim; Vee-Jay Records (defunct) has 2 claims vs. Chess Records' 3; the Regal's demolition and the 47th Street corridor's decline are noted but not developed.
- Missing lost institutions with major significance: the Savoy Ballroom, the Sunset Cafe, the Rhumboogie Cafe (named only in passing), the Chicago Bee (defunct paper), the Wabash YMCA (mentioned in passing only), and Liberty Life (Supreme Life's predecessor — named but not an entity).
- Active institutions (WVON, WGCI, DuSable Museum, Olivet, Quinn, SSCAC, eta) consistently get 2-4 claims each; defunct ones average fewer, reinforcing a present-day-skewed picture.

Affected: b-delisa, b-veejay, b-regal, binga-bank, douglass-national-bank, seaway-national-bank

**Recommendation:** Add entities for the Savoy Ballroom, Sunset Cafe, Chicago Bee, and Wabash YMCA; expand the DeLisa and Vee-Jay entries to parity with their surviving peers.

## Missing women (specific gaps in women's contributions) — severity: HIGH

- Only 3 of 24 person entities are women (12.5%): Dorothy Gautreaux, Gwendolyn Brooks, Margaret Taylor-Burroughs. By track: track-a 1 of 9, track-b 1 of 7, track-c 0 of 6, track-d 1 of 2.

- Lorraine Hansberry appears only inside her father's entity (hansberry-carl); the playwright of A Raisin in the Sun — the most famous cultural product of Black Chicago's housing struggle — has no entity of her own.
- Mamie Till-Mobley is absent even though the Emmett Till funeral photographs (b-jpc claim) are cited; her decision to hold an open casket is the reason those photos exist.
- Annie Turnbo Malone's Poro College (Black beauty empire headquartered in Chicago, training thousands of Black women agents) is absent; Madame C.J. Walker's Chicago operations are absent.
- Vivian G. Montes only inside d-bronzeville-childrens-museum.
- Carol Moseley Braun (first Black woman U.S. Senator, Chicagoan) is absent; no Black women elected officials appear at all.

Affected: wells-homes, hansberry-carl, b-jpc, harsh-collection, b-phillips, b-barnett, d-mlk, d-eta, d-bronzeville-childrens-museum

**Recommendation:** Add person entities for Ida B. Wells, Lorraine Hansberry, Mamie Till-Mobley, Annie Turnbo Malone, and Carol Moseley Braun; promote Vivian Harsh and Maudelle Bousfield from claim-mentions to entities.

## Missing working-class history (labor, domestic workers, Pullman porters) — severity: HIGH

- No labor entities exist: the Brotherhood of Sleeping Car Porters (Chicago was the porter hub; the Chicago local was pivotal) is absent; the United Packinghouse Workers of America (Stockyards, major Black labor organizing in the 1940s-50s) is absent; no domestic-workers' organizing is covered.
- Pullman porters appear only twice, both in passing: as Defender distributors (b-defender) and as IBPOEW co-founder Arthur Riggs's occupation (ibpoew). No entity treats porters as a community or labor force.
- Domestic workers — one of the largest occupations for Black Chicago women through the mid-20th century — have zero mentions across 202 claims.
- WPA labor history is covered only through the arts (sscac); no WPA work-relief, packinghouse, steel (South Chicago), or hotel-workers' union history.
- Track-c covers Black capital (banks, insurance) thoroughly but Black labor not at all — the economic track is one-sided.

Affected: b-defender, ibpoew, sscac

**Recommendation:** Add entities for the Brotherhood of Sleeping Car Porters (Chicago), the United Packinghouse Workers, and a domestic-workers/beauticians labor entry (fold in Poro); rebalance track-c's title scope to include labor.

## Missing everyday-life institutions (barbershops, beauty shops, churches as social centers, block clubs) — severity: MEDIUM

- Zero entities for barbershops, beauty salons, or beauty schools, despite their documented role as Black economic and social infrastructure.
- Zero entities for block clubs — the signature institution of mid-century Black Chicago neighborhood life.
- Zero entities for funeral homes (A.A. Rayner & Sons, Leak & Sons), historically among the largest Black-owned businesses and civic anchors.
- Churches are covered as historic institutions (Olivet, Quinn, St. Mark) but not as social centers: no description of repasts, voter-registration drives, club meetings, or daycares operating out of churches.
- No social/athletic clubs, bowling leagues, skating rinks, or park fieldhouses; no corner stores or Black-owned taverns beyond the three famous music clubs.
- The three churches + two fraternal orders (prince-hall-masons, ibpoew) + one women's service org (links-chicago) cover associational life only at the elite/formal end.

Affected: b-olivet, b-quinn, b-stmark, prince-hall-masons, ibpoew, links-chicago

**Recommendation:** Add at least one entity each for: a beauty-school/beautician network (Poro), a funeral home, a block-club network, and a church-as-social-center case study (e.g., voter registration at a West or South Side church).

Affected: cbl, b-defender, uchicago, woodlawn, bronzeville

- No rights problems exist in the JSON as written; all 4 flagged items are forward-looking publication risks.
