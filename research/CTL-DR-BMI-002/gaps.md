# CTL-DR-BMI-002 — Gaps and unknowns

Consolidated from research tracks A–D and the red-team audit, 2026-09-28.

## Track A: Venues and clubs

1. Regal Theater architect: Edward Eichenbaum (Wikipedia) vs. Lubliner & Trinz (Encyclopedia of Chicago) — Disputed. Need 1928 building permits / landmark report.
2. Regal opening day: February 1928 vs. February 4, 1928 — Provisional (day-level).
3. Ken Blewett (Regal manager 1939–1959, first Black B&K manager) — single government-report source; seek Chicago Defender coverage.
4. Club DeLisa: exact opening date (Feb 28, 1933), rebuilding year (1941), closing date (Feb 16, 1958), "The Club" 1966 — all from one reference site (African American Registry) plus a photo-caption researcher. Need Defender/Tribune corroboration.
5. Rhumboogie: opening month (Apr 1942), fire (Dec 31, 1945), reopening (Jun 1946), final close (May 1947) — chain from Wikipedia citations; need period press. Charlie Parker "Dream Band" claim needs verification.
6. Theresa's: Needham origin story, Carl Jones 1954, Buddy Guy tenure specifics, "Godmother of the Chicago Blues" nickname, Needham death 1992 — well-repeated but no primary sources located this round; the Chicago Ethnic Arts Project (Library of Congress) holds Needham photos — a strong primary lead.
7. Roberts Show Club: closing date and bowling-alley/skating-rink conversion timeline vague; setlist.fm dates are crowdsourced and unverifiable.
8. Sutherland Lounge: Coltrane recording date conflict (March 1 vs. Oct 10–15, 1961); gray-market release; Miles Davis/St. Louis Jimmy Oden setlist.fm dates conflict with the lounge's documented Feb 1964 closing.
9. Gerri's Palm Tavern: founding date and original founders not found; performer claims vague; outcome of the 2002 "Nostalgia Jazz and Wine Club" plan unknown; Oliver's death date not captured.
10. Lee's Unleaded Blues: owners' surname "Grey" vs. "Rey" — Disputed; need business license records. Davis purchase date (~2001), 2015 closure, and current operating status need confirmation.
11. The Warehouse: build year (1906 vs. 1910) and Williams purchase year (1975 vs. 1976) — Disputed; county property records would resolve.
12. The Power Plant: building fate after Knuckles left (1986) — Unresolved.
13. Muzic Box: address timeline (Lower Wacker vs. 326 N. Michigan Ave.) — Provisional. Ron Hardy's death year (1991 vs. March 2, 1992) — Disputed.
14. Kingston Mines: founding year (1968 claimed vs. 1969 theatre-company founding) — Provisional; "oldest continuously operating" billing contested by B.L.U.E.S. (which claims "live blues since 1979" and Chicago's oldest blues bar per Lyft listing) — flag as Disputed billing.
15. B.L.U.E.S.: post-2020 status — for sale during pandemic; current operating status Unresolved.
16. Green Mill: original name spelling (Pop Morse's vs. Pop Morris's Roadhouse/Garden); rename date; Capone/McGurn lore largely legendary; 1986 purchase by Dave Jemilo unverified.
17. Savoy Ballroom: end date (1948 vs. 1954) and demolition date (early 1970s) — Provisional; flagged for deeper research.
18. Primary-source leads for next pass: Chicago Defender archives (all venues), NPS nomination forms, UChicago Chicago Jazz Archive, HistoryMakers oral histories (Theresa Needham? Checkerboard?), LOC Chicago Ethnic Arts Project, Cook County property records (Warehouse, Palm Tavern, Lee's), and Illinois liquor-license records for founding dates.

## Track B: Labels and recording

1. Vee-Jay bankruptcy month: August 1966 (Wikipedia, IPM) vs filed May 1966 / wound up August 1966 (Arts Desk). Year 1966 is solid; month disputed.
2. Ewart Abner joined Vee-Jay in 1954 (Smithsonian) vs 1955 (Wikipedia). Needs archival resolution — Abner is a pivotal Black executive figure; recommend deeper research (he later became Motown president).
3. Chess move to 2120 S. Michigan: May 1957 (city landmark publication) vs 1956 in some accounts. Minor but affects timeline precision.
4. Brunswick catalog ownership today: Donald Clarke says Tarnopol sold back to MCA in 1984; family revived the label in 1995 — the current catalog holder is Unresolved.
5. Curtom's specific Chicago street address and the exact Defunct-1980 date: single-source (Wikipedia infobox); catalog ownership by Warner Music Group likewise single-source.
6. Twinight founders' racial background / whether Twinight was Black-owned: unresolved. Also no verified street address.
7. Street addresses not found for: Chance, Parrot/Blue Lake, Chi-Sound, Dakar (beyond the 1449 S. Michigan succession note), Curtom, Twinight. City directories / Sanborn maps / label discographies would be the next step.
8. Vee-Jay blame narratives (Abner's gambling, Capitol lawsuits as primary cause) rest on few sources; a primary-source business history (court records from the 1966 bankruptcy, Billboard archives) is missing.
9. Constellation Records (Ewart Abner's post-Vee-Jay Chicago label, early 1960s) and Cobra Records (1956–1959) deserve full entries but were not verified beyond single secondary mentions — recommend dedicated passes.
10. Delmark's "oldest independent American jazz and blues label still active" claim (The Local Voice) is marketing-adjacent; no comparative verification done.
11. The African American Registry dates Curtom's launch to 1970 vs the consensus 1968 — single-source outlier, noted but not adopted.
12. No primary sources (incorporation records, Billboard chart archives, bankruptcy filings, oral histories beyond quoted snippets) were consulted; nearly all evidence is secondary. A future pass should target the Chicago Defender archives, Billboard, the Blues Heaven Foundation collection, and the Calumet Regional Archives (Vivian Carter materials).

## Track D: Lineages and record stores

1. **Early-1980s Chicago hip-hop infrastructure is the thinnest area.** This pass documents the 1991–1997 radio/independent-label breakthrough well, but park jams, house parties, breaking/graffiti scenes, school-based crews, community radio, and the 1980s battle circuit lack citable sources. The WGCI "Rap Down"/"Bad Boyz" material rests on user-uploaded recordings; seek station archives, *Chicago Defender* coverage, or oral-history projects.
2. **Rose Records founding, scale, and closure are unresolved.** The 1931/Aaron Rosenbloom/35-store/1995-closure account comes from merchandise copy. Next steps: *Chicago Tribune* archives, city business directories, period advertisements, and the James Rosenbloom papers if any exist.
3. **Importes, Etc. closing date and full business chronology are unresolved.** No citable closure reporting was located.
4. **NCGCC founding year (1932 vs. 1933) is disputed.** Resolve via the organization's own records or incorporation documents; do not publish a year until resolved.
5. **First Church radio: 1934 launch (landmark report) vs. 1935 "Midnight Broadcast" (Wikipedia).** Consistent if read as ministry-then-program, but the WSBC dating needs a second source. The Ebony "most popular Negro radio minister" quotation's year was truncated in digitization.
6. **"Father of Gospel Music" / "Mother of Gospel" honorifics** are attested but their first usage and original rationale need scholarly sourcing (the Bernice Johnson Reagon framing appeared only in search excerpts).
7. **Roberta Martin Studio of Music (1939)** and the 1998 postage stamp rest on a stamp-dealer site; seek USPS records and music-publishing scholarship. Women gospel publishers beyond Sallie and Roberta Martin (e.g., Lillian Bowles, Elma Hendrix/Parham in Detroit) deserve a dedicated pass.
8. **The Impressions' Chicago formation and Mayfield's early context** were not re-verified against citable sources; the Chi-Lites chart claims need Billboard confirmation; Brunswick's later Carl Davis–led Chicago operation, Barbara Acklin's songwriting, and Record Row studio infrastructure are leads, not findings.
9. **The Alligator Records / *Living Blues* / Flying Fish incubation claim** (Bruce Iglauer on the Jazz Record Mart) is the most important unverified "store as institution-generator" claim; locate the original Sun-Times/Tribune text.
10. **Maxwell Street's women and working-class figures** (e.g., Memphis Minnie and other women blues performers; vendors; Red Abrams) are under-documented in the sources located; oral-history collections should be the next stop.
11. **The Koester/Delmark racial dynamic** (white owner embedded in Black music retail and recording) needs explicit, careful treatment in flagship copy; this track records the facts but does not analyze it.
12. **Hip-house priority claims** (who made the "first" hip-house record) are disputed and label jacket copy is self-promotion; keep them out of verified copy.
13. **General method note:** roughly 46 sources were consulted; ~15 claims reach Verified (corroborated or primary-institutional), the majority are Provisional (single solid secondary or oral testimony), 4 are Disputed, and 3 are Unresolved. Public-facing copy should draw only from Verified claims, use Provisional claims with attribution, and keep Disputed/Unresolved items in research notes.

## Track C: Fact vs lore (quarantined claims)

1. **"On and On" is the first house record.** LORE-ADJACENT / DISPUTED. Jesse Saunders claims it; RA's review notes the A-side is still post-disco hi-NRG and the claim rests on the B-side's utilitarian DJ tools. Competing claimants: Jamie Principle's "Your Love" (tapes circulating 1982–84; some call it the first house track), Z-Factor "Fantasy" (recorded before "On and On," released after — Rachael Cain calls it "the first recorded house record"), Chip E.'s "Jack Trax." Discogs community notes explicitly reject a single "mythical one true first." Grade: Disputed — never present as settled.
2. **The name "house music" comes from the Warehouse.** WIDELY ACCEPTED, MECHANISM DISPUTED. The City landmark report states the term "is believed to be derived from the genre's birthplace." Competing version: owner Robert Williams labeled his promotional posters "house parties"/"house music" (Warehouse history, Wikipedia). A third telling credits dancers shortening "the music they play at the Warehouse." The namesake link is Verified; the exact derivation path is Provisional.
3. **Frankie Knuckles "invented" house music single-handedly.** LORE. The documented record (landmark report, SPIN, LoC) shows Knuckles as a pivotal synthesizer of disco/soul/funk/electronic sources for a specific dancefloor, but the genre's recorded form came from many producers (Saunders, Principle, Heard, Jefferson, Pierre, Chip E., Adonis) and a second pole (Ron Hardy/Music Box). "Godfather of House" is an honorific, not a sole-inventor finding.
4. **Ron Hardy played "Acid Tracks" four times in one night to win the crowd over.** ORAL HISTORY / PROVISIONAL. Single-sourced via Wikipedia's citation; a classic scene set-piece with no corroborating contemporary account found.
5. **The Larry Sherman pressing-plant legend** (only plant in Chicago; Saunders and Lawrence asked for 500 copies at $5 each, returned in 20 minutes for 1,000 more; white-label/black-text batches). LORE. Discogs community text, no primary corroboration.
6. **The Hot Mix 5 "broke" nearly every Chicago house record on air and were taped and mailed worldwide.** LORE-ADJACENT / PROVISIONAL. Directionally consistent with the landmark report (WBMX among the first to air live house mixes) but the scale claims (2M listeners, worst-to-first) are uncited.
7. **Trax's exploitation narrative** (no royalties, no contracts, cheap vinyl). DOCUMENTED GRIEVANCES, NOT ADJUDICATED. Sourced to named artist interviews (Jamie Principle, DJ Pierre via 5 Mag). Present as reported allegations, not findings.
8. **"Disco died at Disco Demolition Night (1979), and house rose from its ashes."** SIMPLIFICATION. The KTTZ obituary notes both the Warehouse and Paradise Garage got *more* popular after disco's supposed death; the scene continuity (same DJs, same dancers, evolving edits) is better documented than a rupture narrative.
9. **WGCI's "Jackmasters" as a Hot Mix 5 copycat (July 1986–Feb 1987).** PROVISIONAL. Single aircheck-archivist source; plausible given the documented WBMX/WGCI rivalry but unverified.
10. **Ron Hardy died in 1991.** DISPUTED. See Gaps #5; do not use 1991 without qualification.

## Structural gaps (red-team audit, Track E)

Coverage holes to address before any flagship public release:

1. **Geography beyond the South Side/North Side club corridors.** West Side venues and scenes are thin; Black suburban scenes (south suburbs, Robbins/Inkster corridor) are absent; Latinx/Puerto Rican/Caribbean/African community music spaces in Chicago are not covered.
2. **Black classical music.** Concert artists, church-trained classical musicians, and institutions (e.g., the legacy around R. Nathaniel Dett, Margaret Bonds) are outside this pass's scope — flag as a deliberate boundary, not an oversight, in flagship copy.
3. **Informal and working-class infrastructure.** House parties, park jams, taverns, skating rinks, jukebox operators, sound-system builders, recording engineers, and record-pressing/distribution workers are named as important but insufficiently researched.
4. **Early-1980s Chicago hip-hop's informal infrastructure.** Weakly documented; needs oral history before it ages out.
5. **Women's roles beyond the marquee names.** Track D centers women where evidence supports it; venue ownership/management, label back-office labor, and DJ/engineering work by women need dedicated research passes.
6. **Record stores as A&R infrastructure.** Imports Etc. closing date unresolved; Rose Records dates/scale need Tribune/city-directory verification; Jazz Record Mart's incubation role needs original newspaper sourcing.

## Archival priorities (red-team audit, Track E)

- **Chicago Defender and Chicago Tribune archives:** venue advertisements/listings to pin opening/closing dates (Club DeLisa, Rhumboogie, Theresa's, Music Box, Warehouse build year).
- **Liquor-license and city-directory records:** B.L.U.E.S. (April 1979), Kingston Mines (1982 Halsted move), Lee's Unleaded Blues (2024 reopening status).
- **FCC records:** WGRB/WYNR/WVON/WGCI/WBMX/WKKC broadcast-history facts currently rest on secondary histories.
- **NPS and City landmark files:** already strong for Sutherland and the Warehouse — extend the method to the Regal/Avalon and other surviving buildings.
- **Billboard trade-press and RIAA data:** Vee-Jay sales figures, Chess/Delmark catalog claims, DJ International/Trax "first" claims.
- **Corporate records:** Vee-Jay bankruptcy filing month (May vs. August 1966); Brunswick catalog ownership today; Chess/UMG/Geffen chain of title.
- **NCGCC organizational records:** resolve the 1932 vs. 1933 founding-year conflict; do not state a year in public copy until resolved.
- **Oral-history targets:** surviving participants from the Warehouse/Music Box/Power Plant era, Hot Mix 5 members, Imports Etc. staff/customers, early Chicago hip-hop DJs and promoters, gospel choir veterans (Dorsey/Martin lineages), Maxwell Street musicians and vendors.
