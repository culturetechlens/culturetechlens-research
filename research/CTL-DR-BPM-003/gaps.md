## A. Disputes that may be unresolvable (current lean noted)

1. **Robert S. Abbott's birth date — Nov 24, 1868 vs Dec 24, 1870.** Institutional weight (Georgia Encyclopedia, Britannica, Chicago Literary HoF, Coastal Georgia guide) favors 1868; Wikipedia/BlackPast say 1870. A Georgia birth record or the Hampton Institute enrollment record could settle it, but none was located. **Recommendation:** present both dates in public outputs ("1868 or 1870") — the tracks already do this; do not let a single date harden.
2. **Bee founding 1925 vs 1926.** Possibly reconcilable: organized/first issue Oct 2, 1925 (African American Registry) vs weekly operation or Bee Building association in 1926. Only 12 Bee issues survive (1938–46, UIUC) — no 1925/26 masthead is known to exist. **May be unresolvable from surviving evidence.**
3. **Bee end 1946 vs 1947.** Depends on whether the sons' short-lived tabloid continuation counts as the same paper. Definitional as much as factual — CTL should state its convention explicitly.
4. **ANP closure 1964 vs post-1967.** The Barnett Papers at Chicago History Museum (collection 1980/213) should contain the answer (final mailings, correspondence). Until consulted: "wound down 1964–67."
5. **Half-Century Magazine end 1925 vs 1947.** Rooks (Rutgers UP, 2004, peer-reviewed) says 1925; a Wikipedia Bee article says 1947. Needs a citation check against Rooks and WorldCat holdings — likely resolvable, not yet resolved.
6. **Jet's Till issue Sept 15 vs Sept 22, 1955.** Lean Sept 15 (LOC, Metress/NYT). A scan of the issue masthead would settle it — likely resolvable.
7. **3435 S. Indiana tenure 1956 vs 1960.** E&P/AP (contemporary) say the move to 2400 S. Michigan happened in 1956; the Landmark record says the Indiana building housed the paper until 1960. Both can be true (retained building). Chicago city directories or Defender masthead imprints 1956–60 would settle it.
8. **ANP launch March 2 vs March 21, 1919.** Needs the charter/incorporation record — likely in the Barnett Papers.
9. **Bee political orientation** ("conservative/Republican" vs "support of liberal causes," adjacent sentences in one Wikipedia article). Only primary-issue reading can settle it, and the 1925–35 run is lost. **May be partially unresolvable** — surviving 1938–46 issues can at least fix the later period.
10. **Lu Palmer's doctorate** (PhD claimed vs coursework-only per Congressional Record). University of Iowa records could settle it; low priority — the tracks correctly refuse to assert it.

## B. Unknowns needing primary research (prioritized)

**P0 — blocking for any public output:**
1. **Rights matrix** (see red-team §11): JPC archive licensing path (NMAAHC/Getty); Real Times Media permission for Defender content; CPL rights status of Abbott-Sengstacke Papers; HistoryMakers permissions. The JPC archive's public-access target (~2029) is the critical path for any visual use of Ebony/Jet photography.
2. **Barnett Papers at CHM** (finding aid: http://chsmedia.org/media/fa/fa/1980/213.htm): ANP charter date, closure date, subscriber lists, and — explicitly unresolved in Track B — **whether the Defender subscribed to, boycotted, or ignored ANP copy**. The Defender–ANP relationship is a flagship-level unknown.

**P1 — major content gaps:**
3. **WJPC radio.** Entirely missing from all tracks: JPC bought WGRT (May 1973, $1.8M), renamed it WJPC — Chicago's first Black-owned radio station — sold Dec 1994. For a "media infrastructure" project this is a significant omission. Needs: FCC records, Chicago broadcast histories, JPC annual reports.
4. **Women photographers in the Chicago Black press.** Track D's biggest flagged gap: zero verifiable names. Search the Defender photo morgue (Abbott-Sengstacke Papers, CPL) and JPC archive staff lists; check the Panorama article's bibliography (journalpanorama.org, cited in Track D) for leads.
5. **Lost runs survey:** Bee 1925–35 (none known), Whip 1932–39 (no digitized holdings located), Broad Ax 1927–31 (last surviving issue Sept 10, 1927). A WorldCat/archive-grid survey for any surviving physical holdings — even a negative result is a citable finding ("no known surviving holdings").
6. **Labor/workforce history:** Defender and JPC headcounts over time, job categories, wages, unionization (the "first to unionize" claim needs verification or retraction), the Abbott anti-union vs "open trade unions" goal tension, R.R. Donnelley's role as Ebony/Jet printer and its 2009 ~$12M mortgage against JPC HQ over unpaid bills.
7. **Chain papers' non-Chicago histories:** Michigan Chronicle (Detroit) and Tri-State Defender (Memphis) — founding editors, lifespans, editorial lines, circulation. Currently names only.

**P2 — important depth:**
8. **"Don't Spend Your Money Where You Can't Work" employment outcomes.** The 15,000+ jobs figure (WTTW via Wikipedia) is thinly sourced; Richard Durham's WPA essay (Harsh Collection, Box 41 folder 7) is the primary lead.
9. **Whip financing (Overton/Binga/DePriest).** Single-source (Wikipedia infobox, no visible citation). If verified from a second source, Track B notes it becomes a flagship CTL insight — Overton bankrolling both the Defender's militant rival (Whip) and its conservative rival (Bee).
10. **Defender's Southern apparatus:** subscription-agent economics, stringer/correspondent network, mail-subscription volumes, church/barbershop distribution economics. The porter mechanics rest on one popular source (goodblacknews.org) — needs corroboration or downgrade-proofing before it hardens into CTL canon.
11. **Bee primary-issue reading** (12 surviving issues, 1938–46, UIUC): political orientation, Olive Diggs's housing beat, women's pages, literary coverage. Also: verify/falsify Ida B. Wells's Bee editorship (1926–31 window only).
12. **Post-1970s community press depth:** Crusader, Citizen chain, N'DIGO, South Street Journal / Chicago Street Journal — oral histories while founders/principals are living (Dorothy Leavell, Hermene Hartman, Ron Carter). Track B's entries are thin and Wikipedia-dependent.
13. **Defender's daily-era printers (1956–2003)** and who printed the final 2019 issues — Track E leaves unresolved.
14. **JPC HQ art collection** (150+ works, donated to DuSable) and the Ebony Test Kitchen (→ Museum of Food and Drink) — cultural-infrastructure assets adjacent to the publishing story; Charlotte L. Lyons (food editor) belongs here too.

## C. Standing cautions for CTL public outputs

1. **Carry the archival-survival caveat everywhere** (Track B's formulation): "the record is thinnest where the archives are gone, not where the history was small." Every comparative or "first" claim needs it.
2. **Never present a single date where tracks document a dispute.** Abbott (1868/1870), Bee (1925/26; 1946/47), ANP (1919 launch day; 1964/67 closure), Thompson (1905/06), Till issue (Sept 15/22, 1955) — show the dispute or pick a lean with a footnote.
3. **Attribute causal claims.** Migration (accelerant, not sole cause), "integration killed the Black press" (Horne's thesis, not consensus), 2019 digital move (Real Times' stated reason), Truman/JFK electoral claims (publisher marketing — do not repeat as fact).
4. **No JPC-archive images in public outputs without a license.** Assume the same for Abbott-Sengstacke Papers items until CPL/family rights are clarified.
5. **Do not assert:** Lu Palmer's PhD; Ida B. Wells's Bee editorship; the Defender's pre-1960 press ownership; JPC owning a printing plant; Gordon Parks as JPC staff; Roy DeCarava or Marvel Cooke as Chicago Black-press figures (Track D correctly excluded both — keep them out).
6. **Corrupted-web-memory watch:** Track B caught oldnews.com fabricating the Whip's founding ("1933 by John H. Johnson," born 1918). Treat unsourced aggregator claims about Black press history as hostile until verified — at least one more sweep for similar fabrications is warranted before publication.
