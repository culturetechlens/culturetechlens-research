## What Survived

Leak & Sons (four generations, still operating), the Johnson Products brands (Black-owned again since 2009), WVON (still Black-owned), the Jordan and Overton Hygienic buildings, the Regal Theater, 3501 S. King Drive, and the paper trail — the Defender archive, Half-Century's digitized issues, the Business History Review study.

## What Disappeared

Binga State Bank, Douglass National Bank, Seaway National Bank; ~27 Black-owned insurers between 1962 and 1992; Madison Street's commercial fabric; Maxwell Street; the Poro and Binga Blocks; the conglomerates' intergenerational control; the policy economy's informal capital base.

## What Changed

Capital moved from Black-owned banks and insurance reserves to mainstream lenders; ownership moved from family conglomerates to corporate acquisition and consortium buybacks; the customer relationship moved from captive market to one-way desegregation; memory moved from company archives to academic and community preservation work.

## What Remains Unknown

The items numbered 1–27 above — most importantly the Defender archive's contents, the susu question, Supreme Life's post-1962 fate, the Madison Street Black-owned layer, women-owned ordinary firms, and succession-failure causes.

## What Requires Preservation

The surviving business records (Fuller, Poro photographs, Supreme Life's archive), the Defender's business archive, oral histories of ordinary-firm owners (closing window), NARA/DOCUMERICA street-vendor images (confirm rights), and the landmark-quality buildings that are the ecosystem's surviving hardware.
