# CTL-DR-GLOBAL-001 — Gaps, Unknowns, and Future Research

**Date:** 2026-09-28

## What we know

- Chicago functioned as a Black institutional capital: movements, presses, and denominations relocated their headquarters *to* Chicago (Moorish Science Temple 1925, Nation of Islam 1934, Muhammad Speaks 1961, The Broad Ax 1899).
- The Black press (Defender + ANP wire service) is the best-documented multi-city Black information system in the corpus, with a real foreign-correspondent network.
- Chicago→Europe music circuits are documentable in unusual detail: tours, sessions, chart positions, label deals (blues circuit from 1962; house breakthrough 1986–89).
- Chicago–Africa ties have a concrete institutional record: anti-apartheid ordinances and resolutions, the ANC Midwest chapter, the Kemetic Institute's study-tour origins, Mandela 1993.

## What we think we know

- The scale of Defender/ANP circulation and reach (tribute-sourced figures sit at Provisional).
- Founding and "first" narratives for Garvey's Chicago arrival, Muhammad Speaks, Pilgrim's gospel role, and Dorsey's appointment year — all recorded as Disputed with contradictory evidence preserved rather than smoothed over.
- Whether Muhammad Speaks's celebrated Africa coverage was original reporting or wire reprint (flagged, uninvestigated).

## What remains unknown

- **Chain migration at fine grain:** no archivally demonstrated Southern county→named-Chicago-neighborhood mapping exists; only state-level patterns documented family-by-family. This is the corpus's largest demographic hole.
- **Return and circular migration:** only two named Chicago-to-South returnees documented (Margaret Walker 1949, Sun Ra 1990–93) — an oral-history gap.
- **Afro-Latinx Chicago:** no race-disaggregated demographic data located; non-Puerto Rican Afro-descendant flows undocumented.
- **Chicago house/footwork in Africa:** track E covered Europe only; African engagements uninvestigated.
- **Multi-node circuits:** no Lagos→São Paulo→Chicago or Kingston→London→Chicago path documented — the evidence that would upgrade "corridors" toward "system."
- **Third World Press:** African authors on the roster, but no documented Chicago contact beyond publication.
- **South African Consulate-General in Chicago:** closure date and any Black-community engagement unconfirmed.

## Structural gaps (red-team)

- **LGBTQ+ contributors absent** across all tracks — most distorting in track E, where Chicago house's queer Black and Latino origins go unacknowledged (Knuckles appears without the Warehouse's queer context).
- **Working-class and non-famous actors thin:** porters as an unnamed network, migrants as family units, no club-goers, church members, or festival attendees as entities. A famous-people graph with a labor network drawn around it.
- **Women** appear mainly as performers; women institution-builders sparse (Vivian Carter/Vee-Jay, Elsie Hernandez, Etta Moten Barnett are exceptions).
- **Indigenous presence** only via Du Sable's "first non-Indigenous settler" framing; no Potawatomi or other Indigenous Chicago entities.
- **African immigrant voices** thin beyond a single figure; no African immigrant is a full person-entity.
- **Caribbean track** documents presence in Chicago more than Chicago↔Caribbean exchange; return flows uninvestigated.
