# CTL-DR-LIFE-001: How We Lived — An Encyclopedia of Black Everyday Life

**Research brief v1.0 — 2026-09-28**

## Executive summary

This package documents the parts of Black Chicago history that traditional histories compress or overlook: ordinary life. Not celebrities — practices. How people shopped, worshipped on Sundays, got their hair done, played cards at gatherings, listened to the radio, posed for portraits, cruised, danced, mourned, and celebrated — with era, place, and generation attached to every claim, and every claim evidence-graded.

The corpus: **81 entities** (46 practices, 16 institutions, 13 objects, 5 source collections, 1 domain), **283 graded claims** (145 Verified / 119 Provisional / 16 Unresolved / 3 Disputed), **91 timeline events** (52 Verified / 39 Provisional), **46 verified-only relationships**, and **9 object biographies** (99 documented links). Four workstreams (home & family; commerce & leisure; media, culture & ritual; object biographies) were merged mechanically, then red-teamed in full census — all 293 original claims individually assessed. The red team found **zero blocking issues and zero fabricated claims**; 44 should-fix items (grade inflation at the margin, rule drift between files, two unmerged duplicates, one folk-etymology clause) were applied before this brief was written.

## Methodology

- **Faceting:** every entity and claim is organized ERA × PLACE × GENERATION × ACTIVITY × OBJECT × INSTITUTION.
- **Evidence standard:** each claim carries claim, evidence, source, primary/secondary flag, publication/date, uncertainty, contradictory evidence, and a Verified / Provisional / Disputed / Unresolved grade. Nothing is manufactured; unresolvable material went to gaps.md, not into claims.
- **Object Biography system** (see `object-biography-methodology.md`): a fixed 11-step chain — object → manufacturer → design → period → advertising → symbolism → consumer use → photographs → museum holdings → related people → related movements. Symbolism requires contemporary documentation; researcher inference is forbidden. Every link is independently graded.
- **Relationships are verified-only.** Twelve relationships that failed the rule were removed to a "held for corroboration" appendix in gaps.md — restorable only with stronger evidence.

## Key findings

1. **The advertising record is a primary source for colorism and consumption.** In the February 1930 *Chicago Defender*, 44 of 103 ads sold products promising to permanently change Black women's features — a quantified primary source for how consumption and colorism intersected in everyday life (Verified).
2. **A 1938 radio program reunited migration-separated families.** Jack L. Cooper's WSBC "Search for Missing Persons" was a family-reunification institution hiding inside broadcast history (Verified).
3. **A dated 1964 census of storefront churches exists.** James B. Stronks enumerated ~35 named congregations along West Roosevelt Road in June 1964, published in the journal *Names* — an extraordinary primary snapshot of the storefront-church world (Verified).
4. ***Jubilee Showcase* ran 1963–1984** — 21 years of Sunday-morning Black gospel TV on WLS, "church before church," tapes donated to the Center for Black Music Research, whose 2019 closure leaves stewardship unconfirmed (Verified; rights unclear).
5. **Block clubs began as Urban League Great Migration acclimation**, with documented coaching signs ("Don't Lean Off the Porch") — via Seligman's *Chicago's Block Clubs* (Verified).
6. **Lake Ivanhoe, Wisconsin (1926)** — a Black-founded resort community by three Black Chicago businessmen, the less-known Chicago alternative to Idlewild (Provisional, single source).
7. **CTA skip-stop service was experienced and narrated as racially coded** across 70+ years — ordinary transit practice documented as Black experience, from the Emmett Till play script to 2024–25 IDOT rider interviews (Verified/Provisional mix).
8. **NMAAHC explicitly debunks the Madam C.J. Walker hot-comb invention myth** ("Sizzle" essay) while documenting U.S. catalog sales by 1886 — the package's warning example against brand mythology (Verified).
9. **Ebony's 1949 "Why Negroes Buy Cadillacs"** gives contemporary Black-press symbolism: the Cadillac as aspiration to be treated as a "first class American" (Verified).
10. **The Defender's 1922 bridal portraits** staged marriage and motherhood as racial-uplift performance with class-coded respectability (Provisional — sourced via thesis; primary issues need direct reading).

## What we know

Everyday Black Chicago life is richly documentable from non-traditional sources: Defender advertisements and society pages, church anniversary books, yearbooks, catalogs, oral histories (Terkel Archive, Chicago History Museum), museum holdings (NMAAHC, Chicago History Museum), and scholarly monographs. The Object Biography system works: 9 of 10 seed biographies produced majority-Verified link chains.

## What we think we know

The Provisional tier (119 claims) is where the richest follow-ups live: Defender TV-page content analysis, Terkel Archive mining, the Karamu feast's 1971 Chicago origin (single-sourced), Lake Ivanhoe's full history, Jiffy Cab (currently TikTok-only — a real lead, zero archival corroboration).

## What remains unknown

- **Birthdays: near-total blank.** No Chicago-specific Black birthday documentation found anywhere — the single largest hole.
- **Dating and marriage beyond announcements:** courtship norms and wedding customs are undocumented.
- **Home decoration:** zero Chicago-specific sources; the "front room" material is general Black-American or UK-diaspora.
- **Home technology** (appliances, telephone, TV-set adoption): only content-farm material exists online; refused as sources.
- **Everyday grocery shopping 1950s–80s** and the corner store as social institution.
- **Rent parties/house-party economics in Chicago** (Harlem is documented; Chicago is not).
- **Neighborhood sports below high-school level** (16-inch softball, boxing gyms, park leagues).
- **Work life beyond porters and police:** packinghouses, domestic day-work, postal work, teaching are thin.
- **The street's own platting** — n/a (cross-project note: PLACE-001 found 63rd Street's platting date Unresolved).

## What CTL should investigate next

1. Defender archives (1915–1975) — the highest-value untapped source across nearly every domain.
2. Terkel Radio Archive keyword mining for Chicago worker tapes (packinghouse, domestic, postal).
3. Church anniversary books and school yearbooks via Chicago History Museum / DuSable archival work (best source for generational layering of church Sunday).
4. Russell Lee's 1941 FSA photos + Defender furniture ads for home decoration.
5. Sears/Montgomery Ward catalog use by Black Chicagoans — catalogs exist; the Chicago-specific use evidence doesn't yet.
6. Oral-history collection on birthdays, dating, and corner stores before the generation that remembers is gone — this is time-sensitive.
7. Rights clearance pass: 60+ logged items need clearance before any public rendering; nothing in this package is cleared.

## Limitations

- Sources skew middle-class (Defender society pages, catalogs, yearbooks); working-class and poor Black Chicagoans are under-documented — the package says whose experience each source captures.
- One worker did public-web research only; several secondary sources were consulted via reviews/abstracts (Best 2005, Stange 2003, Jordano 2009 flagged for direct reading).
- Chicago sources document **porch**-sitting, not "stoop" in the New York sense — recorded explicitly, not assumed.
- The console-stereo biography (ob-07) was removed entirely: 0 Verified links, Wikipedia plus a hobbyist site — plausible filler has no place here.
- The Kwanzaa Karamu-feast 1971 Chicago origin claim is single-sourced and needs corroboration before any public use.
