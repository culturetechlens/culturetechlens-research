## Secondary scholarship

- **St. Clair Drake & Horace R. Cayton, Black Metropolis: A Study of Negro Life in a Northern City (University of Chicago Press, 1945)** — secondary (sociological study built on WPA field research). The foundational documentation of kitchenettes, the Black Belt, and Bronzeville everyday life. Quoted via eScholarship dissertation.
- **Tracy N. Poe, "The Origins of Soul Food in Black Urban Identity: Chicago, 1915–1947," American Studies International (Feb 1999)** — secondary (peer-reviewed). Documents the class conflict over migrant foodways (sidewalk barbecue pits, chicken shacks, the Defender's "Pig Ankle Joints" headline) and the later embrace of soul food as racial identity. Read via summary; full article not examined.
- **C. Semmes, The Regal Theater and Black Culture** — secondary (scholarly book). Documents the Regal (1928–1968) as the largest movie/stage-show venue built for a Black community. Read via chapter excerpt.
- **P. Daniel, "A History of Discrimination against Black Students in Chicago Secondary Schools," History of Education Quarterly (1980)** — secondary (peer-reviewed). Documents 1920 failure-rate gaps and 1925 Wendell Phillips test-score data. Read via scan.
- **Patryk Mamczur, "Rent parties, old settlers and jitterbugs..." (Jagiellonian University, 2019)** — secondary (peer-reviewed article from oral histories, 1917–1945). Documents rent parties as a migrant coping institution. https://ruj.uj.edu.pl/server/api/core/bitstreams/1ed7b968-de97-44d4-8ce2-cd961678cd94/content
- **"Read All About It: African Americans, Marriage, and the Politics of Wedding Announcements in Black and White Newspapers" (Taylor & Francis, 2026)** — secondary (peer-reviewed). Cites Chicago Defender wedding announcements (e.g., June 19, 1943). Citation only; article not read in full.
- **Zachary Zimmermann, "Representations of Black Women in the Chicago Defender" (UW–Eau Claire thesis)** — secondary (undergraduate thesis). Documents 1922 Defender bridal portraits ("Betrothed," "A Spring Bride") and "NOT A FLAPPER MOTHER." Primary issues not directly examined.
- **Michael McMillan, "The West Indian Front Room in the African Diaspora" (2003, abstract)** — secondary (scholarship). Comparative only: UK/West Indian context, not Chicago. Used to bound the "front room" claim.

## Source bias note

Surviving sources skew middle-class and institutional: the Defender's society pages featured respectable brides; landmark reports document showcase institutions (DuSable); memoirs like Three Girls from Bronzeville document working-class life but are published exceptions. The poorest Bronzeville residents — kitchenette dwellers facing eviction, policy players, sex workers documented in Black Metropolis's "lower depths" — rarely left first-person records. Oral-history collections (Chicago History Museum, DuSable Museum, Terkel archive) exist but were not directly searched in this pass.
