# CTL-DR-PLACE-001 — Red Team Report
**63rd Street: A Cultural Biography of a Chicago Corridor · 2026-09-28**

Checklist review against the Phase 1 research package (111 entities, 189 events). Findings are graded; nothing below is presented as resolved unless the evidence supports it.

## 1. Unsupported claims (graded Unresolved — must not be published as fact)

- When 63rd Street was laid out/platted — no source found.
- Halsted station (Englewood branch) opening date.
- 63rd & Halsted pedestrian-mall end date.
- Sears Englewood exact street address/corner.
- St. Rita of Cascia parish address (weakest evidence in the set — TikTok caption).
- Exact dates for several theater demolitions (Tivoli demolition date; Tower; Southtown).

## 2. Disputed claims (preserved as disputes, not resolved)

- Sears construction: 1929 ($1.5M) vs. 1934 (fully air-conditioned).
- Englewood Mall chronology: 1969 vs. ~1973.
- Englewood 1920 population: LISC's 139,000 vs. community-area 86,619.
- Tivoli address: 6323 vs. 6325 S. Cottage Grove.
- Maryland Theatre opening: 1928 vs. 1918.
- Daley's current address: 6257 S. Cottage Grove vs. 809 E. 63rd.
- Englewood Union Station demolition: late 1970s vs. 1982.
- Wieboldt's location: 63rd & Green (better supported) vs. Halsted (common error) — near-resolved in favor of Green.

## 3. Missing communities

- **No mosque on 63rd proper verified** — recorded as a research gap, not evidence of absence. Muslim institutional life along the corridor is unmapped.
- Latino communities in the Chicago Lawn segment are thinly covered.
- Small-congregation founding dates (Greater St. John AME, St. John EMBC, St. Mary's M.B.) unrecovered.

## 4. Geographic bias

- Woodlawn/Englewood (eastern corridor) dominate; **West Englewood and Chicago Lawn are thin** on everyday commerce and institutions.
- Corrective applied: Chicago Lawn's documented commercial vitality is explicitly fenced off from the decline narrative — the corridor is not one story.

## 5. Famous-person bias

- Partially mitigated: the package centers venues, institutions, and infrastructure. But performer names (Jamal, Davis, Parker, Coltrane) still anchor club notability — unavoidable given how the sources record these rooms, and flagged here.
- Black ownership documented only for **McKie's (McKie Fitzhugh)** and **Basin Street (part-owner Eddie "Cleanhead" Vinson)** — deliberately not inferred for other clubs.

## 6. Institutional bias

- The *Defender* dominates the press narrative (via the UChicago guide); other Black papers (Bee, Whip) are thin.
- TWO dominates the organizing narrative; smaller block clubs and civic associations are under-documented.

## 7. Survivorship and nostalgia bias

- The "golden age" commercial narrative (second-busiest district, bustling strip) leans on booster and nostalgia sources; the package grades these Provisional and preserves the hardship record alongside (kitchenettes, arson, disinvestment).
- 1970s arson claims rest on a single secondary source with **no legal findings** — flagged, not asserted.
- The "white flight after 1968" causal account is single-sourced; decline is presented multi-causal.

## 8. Missing women

- Church mothers, missionary societies, deaconesses: absent.
- Women business owners on the corridor: largely absent.
- Women in the club/entertainment scene beyond performers: absent.
- **This is the package's largest demographic blind spot.** Phase 2 must include a dedicated track (Defender social pages, church anniversary books, beauty-culture sources).

## 9. Missing working-class history

- Everyday shops west of Halsted: thin; several addresses still need directory work (Elman's Shoes, Regal Men's Shop, Rexall, Lauer Sisters).
- Beauty/barbershop oral histories: **not found** — one of two experience-layer voids (the other: congregational life).

## 10. Weak sources (contained, not laundered)

- Station dates: overwhelmingly Wikipedia-only → graded Provisional; chicago-l.org verification is the top corroboration priority.
- Sears dates: YouTube single source → Disputed.
- St. Rita address: TikTok → weakest link, flagged in three files.
- "Second-busiest" 1934 dating: TikTok → weak; LISC 2005 is the credible repeater.
- Social posts, MapCarta, commercial listings: confined to Provisional claims, never sole support for Verified facts.

## 12. Misleading causal claims (checked)

- **Dan Ryan Expressway → 63rd business decline:** no specific causality found in sources; documented as a 14-lane barrier (MPC 2023) without attributing corridor decline to it. Not claimed.
- **1969 Dan Ryan L → ridership loss on old branches:** Provisional, attributed, not asserted as sole cause.
- **1997 el demolition → redevelopment:** community leaders' stated rationale documented; retrospective "planning mistake" judgment graded Provisional.
- No single-cause explanations of decline appear in the package.

## 13. Copyright and rights

- No images reproduced in the research package. Photo archive leads noted per property (Chicago History Museum, Theatre Historical Society of America, CARLI, Cinema Treasures uploads, Graham Garfield/Racine collection) with **rights status unresolved**.
- Defender/Tribune/Daily News clippings will need rights clearance before any publication use.
- Archival photograph comparisons and then/now features (per the master prompt's visual layer) cannot proceed until rights are cleared — flagged as a Phase 2 workstream.

## 14. Structural limitations of this package

- **No live-browser verification was performed by one worker (W5);** several newspaper citations across workers are secondhand (cited via tertiary sources, not viewed). A browser-capable agent should verify Defender/Tribune/Daily News pages directly before publication.
- Census figures 1930–2020 are quoted via Wikipedia's community-area tables (secondary); direct Census/API verification is a Phase 2 task.
- The experience layer's interpretive notes are fenced off from documented evidence per protocol — but the documented base is thinner than the prompt's ambition (see §8–9).
