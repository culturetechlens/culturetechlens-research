# Gaps and Open Questions — CTL-DR-PRESERVE-001

> **CULTURETECHLENS** · *Culture, Clearly Seen.* · **Black Cultural Intelligence**

**Project:** CTL-DR-PRESERVE-001 · **Date:** 2026-09-28

What this research could not resolve — recorded as gaps, not assumptions. Each gap names what is unknown, why it matters, and what would resolve it. **Privacy constraint is absolute throughout:** no private inquiry is proposed that would intrude on families, firms, or congregations; several leads below can only be resolved by the holders themselves choosing to act.

---

## Tier A — highest-value unresolved leads

### 1. A.A. Rayner & Sons Funeral Home historical records — UNRESOLVED
No archive, finding aid, or records-transfer announcement was found. The firm (Chicago, founded 1947) handled Emmett Till's funeral arrangements — its business records would be first-order civil-rights evidence. Searches located only business listings and current operations. Records may exist privately, may have been lost, or may be held by the family; none of these can be verified without private inquiry, which the privacy constraint forbids us from initiating intrusively. **This is the single highest-value unresolved lead in the corpus.** Resolution path: a respectful, holder-initiated conversation — e.g., via the Bronzeville Historical Society's existing funeral-records relationships — not a records demand.

### 2. Obsidian Collection Archives — current status UNVERIFIED
The 2018 reporting documented ~250,000 Defender images counted and only 136 images plus 8 photo stories publicly live; the Lu Palmer Mansion restoration (zoning approved ca. 2023) has no confirmed completion. **Current (2026) digitization totals, preservation-grade storage, and backup posture are undocumented.** Given the framework's Tier 2 assessment of the Defender/Obsidian backlog, this is the most time-sensitive verification gap: the assessment may be understated or outdated in either direction. Resolution path: direct, public-source check of Obsidian's current releases and infrastructure disclosures.

## Tier B — custodianship unknowns

### 3. Muhammad Speaks newspaper morgue — UNRESOLVED
No archive or morgue location for the Nation of Islam's paper (1961–1975), the largest-circulating Black newspaper of its day. Holdings may rest with the Nation of Islam (a private religious organization — privacy constrains inquiry) or be dispersed. Distinct from the documented 18-box run at the Black Archives of Mid-America (finding aid AC58), which is a separate holding, not the paper's own archive. Resolution path: holder-initiated disclosure only.

### 4. Vee-Jay Records masters and master book — UNRESOLVED
After the 1966 bankruptcy, assets passed through Vee-Jay International and licensees (Buddah, Springboard). No current repository for the 5,000+ master tapes or the chronological master book was found. Masters may survive in licensee vaults, private hands, or be lost; commercial availability of the music does **not** imply preservation of masters. Resolution path: discography and vault research; label-successor inquiry.

### 5. Chicago Whip morgue and business records — UNRESOLVED
No archive, morgue, or finding aid for the Chicago Whip (1919–1939) surfaced in targeted searches. Surviving runs may exist in library microfilm (not confirmed in this pass). Resolution path: newspaper-microfilm union catalog search.

### 6. Binga State Bank / Supreme Life Insurance corporate records — UNRESOLVED
Supreme Life board minutes (1953–1964) are cited in Cambridge Business History Review scholarship — proving records existed and were accessible to at least one researcher — but the footnotes point to company-held minutes, not a public repository. Records may survive in family hands, corporate-successor files, or state insurance-regulator files. Resolution path: corporate-successor and regulatory-archive inquiry.

### 7. Knoxville College records — PROVISIONAL
Campus closed 2017; historic building lost to fire November 2024; records custody undocumented. The framework's Tier 2 assessment is provisional: campus decay and the 2024 fire are documented, but the records-specific threat is inferential and uniqueness unverified. Resolution path: contact with the college's board and preservation planners.

## Tier C — classes of the unknown

### 8. Private and church archives as a class
The corpus repeatedly finds that the most significant holdings are the least visible: Thomas A. Dorsey family and congregational records, private family collections of Black Chicago history, church basements like Shorefront's. **There is no survey instrument for this universe.** The BMRC survey reached institutions; nothing equivalent exists for church and family holdings, and privacy correctly limits what can be asked. Resolution path: trust-based, community-led survey work (the Shorefront and Bronzeville Historical Society models), not extraction.

### 9. Black-owned business records as a class
Binga/Supreme Life, the Chicago Bee/Overton Hygienic, Vee-Jay, Chess (masters destroyed 2008) — Black business records are systematically absent from public custodianship. Corporate records become bankruptcy assets (JPC, Vee-Jay) or simply vanish with the firm. Resolution path: business-history research agenda; state insurance and corporate-registry archives.

### 10. Smaller BMRC repositories
The BMRC survey and portal cover member institutions; smaller Chicago-area repositories — church archives, neighborhood societies, single-person operations — were beyond the survey's reach. The portal's 1,000+ records describe the visible tip. Resolution path: extended survey funding.

### 11. HBCU records beyond the radio project
The $5M HBCU Radio Preservation Project (2023) covers 29 stations' broadcast archives. Institutional records of closed and struggling HBCUs (Knoxville as the documented case) have no equivalent program. Resolution path: HBCU records survey modeled on the radio project.

## Tier D — structural and methodological gaps

### 12. Privacy-limited work
Burial and funeral records, family collections, and living collectors' holdings were deliberately treated at aggregate level. This is correct — and it means the synthesis systematically under-describes the most humanly significant materials. The gap is structural: **privacy protection and archival visibility are in tension, and privacy wins.** Future work must be holder-led.

### 13. Negative findings (not safety)
- **Building demolition destroying records:** no documented case found. Uncataloged materials destroyed in demolitions leave no paper trail — the absence is expected, not reassuring.
- **Domain expiration:** no documented case found. The exposure surface (small archives, personal sites, no IT staff) is real and unmeasured.
Both are recorded as explicit negative findings. A future pass should test them directly: demolition-permit cross-referencing for the first, domain-registration monitoring for the second.

### 14. Digital decay measurement
Pew's 18% tweet-decay figure (2023) is the only quantified decay rate in the corpus. There is no equivalent measurement for Black institutional websites, digitized collections' link rot, or platform-hosted archives (Black Archives Co, aircheck museums). Resolution path: longitudinal link-rot and availability auditing of the corpus's own source list.

### 15. What the tracks could not see
The corpus is Chicago-anchored by design and web-visible by method. The community track notes, honestly, that the most important collections are the ones no search can find. Every quantitative claim in this project describes the documented fraction. **The undocumented fraction is the actual emergency.**

### 16. Black women's organizational and community history is nearly absent
Of the 84 mapped collections, roughly 2 explicitly center Black women's organizational or community history — a representation gap, not a memory gap. Church mother boards, women's clubs, sorority chapters, domestic-worker networks, and mutual-aid societies generated records that the web-visible corpus barely captures. Resolution path: a dedicated track on Black women's organizational archives, holder-led where privacy demands.

### 17. Working-class history is thin
The corpus over-represents institutions, professionals, and the celebrated; working-class Black life appears mostly through burial records and employment-adjacent materials. Resolution path: oral-history and union/labor records survey.

### 18. Rights uncertainty as a synthesis-level investigation
Thirty-three of 84 records carry undocumented rights status (noted in the research brief as finding 7); rights blockers recur across tracks (Eyes on the Prize license expiry; Duke's 2013 orphan-works filing; platform takedowns). The tracks document rights at record level; a dedicated investigation should quantify rights status across the corpus and map clearing pathways.
