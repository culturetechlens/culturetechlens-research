# Red-Team Audit — CTL-DR-SCI-004 "Sacred Ground: How Black Churches Built Chicago's Civic Infrastructure"

**Auditor role:** adversarial review of tracks A–E (evidence-graded JSON). No new research except spot-checks.
**Date:** 2026-09-28 · **Verdict tally: 0 fail · 13 concern · 1 pass**

**Bottom line:** The tracks are diligent and unusually honest about evidence grades — but they are not yet publishable as settled public history. The same facts carry different verdicts in different tracks, several "Verified" claims rest on thin or single-source evidence, and the corpus reproduces the archive's famous-pulpit bias. A harmonization pass is required before synthesis.

---

## 1. Unsupported claims — verdict: CONCERN

- **Quinn Chapel as "birthplace" of Provident Hospital** — graded *Verified* in track-b on a single Congressional-tribute source, but *Provisional* in track-a. Spot-check: HABS IL-1204 says Quinn members were "instrumental in founding" Provident — weaker than "birthplace." Track-b's gloss ("fair characterization") is an interpretive upgrade, not evidence. **Downgrade to Provisional.**
- **Olivet's 1918 $80,000 building purchase** — *Verified* in track-b, but both citations come from the same organization (Preservation Chicago) and the track admits deed records weren't consulted. **Downgrade to Provisional.**
- **NCGCC co-founded "in 1933"** — *Verified* in track-d, though the track's own summary says sources date it 1932 *or* 1933. **Downgrade to Disputed.**
- **Mahalia Jackson's Chicago history** — *Verified* in track-d on sources including a fan wiki (folksongindex.fandom.com). A fandom wiki cannot carry a Verified grade. **Downgrade.**
- **"The first gospel choir appeared in the 1930s at Pilgrim"** — *Verified* in track-d (Lectionary/Kernodle), directly contradicting the project's own unresolved Pilgrim-vs.-Ebenezer 1931 primacy dispute. **Downgrade to Disputed.**
- **Pilgrim "became the home of Pilgrim Baptist in 1920"** — *Provisional* in track-d; spot-check (Congressional Record, 1981 city landmarks summary, Preservation Chicago) confirms **1922**. The 1920 date is wrong — correct it.
- **Fellowship founded 1950** — *Verified* in tracks c and d, *Disputed* (1950 vs 1958) in a and b. **Walk back to Disputed everywhere.**
- **Willie Barrow "first woman to lead a major civil rights organization"** — *Verified* in track-c, but its own uncertainty field cites Coretta Scott King. The superlative is press framing; the verifiable fact is "first woman executive director of Operation PUSH."
- Correctly handled (keep as-is): Olivet "largest Protestant congregation in the world" (Provisional), Lucy Smith 60,000 funeral (Unresolved).

**Recommendation:** project-wide rule — no *Verified* on a single source for any superlative ("first/largest/only"); require two independent sources. Run a harmonization pass so every duplicated fact carries one verdict.

## 4. Famous-pulpit bias — verdict: CONCERN

~20 unique congregations get dedicated entries; ~17–18 are prominent flagships. The storefront tradition — **72% of Black Chicago churches** in the 1930 Mays & Nicholson survey — is covered almost entirely as aggregate statistics, with only 2–3 named storefronts (from name-lists) and zero biographical entries for small-congregation pastors. Coverage is roughly inverted relative to the historical landscape: **~85–90% of named-congregation attention goes to institutions that were a small minority of Black churches.** Mitigation: track-e names the thinness honestly. **Recommendation:** state the inversion quantitatively as a headline finding; add 2–3 named small-congregation case studies; don't let track-a's 12 "landmarks" become the de facto canon.

## 5. Missing women — verdict: CONCERN

Track-c is genuinely strong (3 of 11 leaders are women: Wyatt, Barrow, York). Elsewhere: track-a's 12 congregations profile **zero women pastors**; founding women appear as wives/club members. **No Chicago church mother is named anywhere** — the office is covered only as an abstract "practice." No deaconess, missionary-society president, or usher-board leader is named. High-risk items: Mother Consuella York's "first Black woman ordained" rests on the ordaining church's site + one press account with a single-sourced year (1954); Lucy Smith's 60,000 funeral is Unresolved on Wikipedia alone (ministry blogs repeating it look Wikipedia-derived — circular). **Recommendation:** name "no named church mothers" as a stated finding with a research plan (Defender obituaries, anniversary books, oral histories); keep York Provisional; consider elevating Mary Evans (AME healing pastor, currently a passing mention) as a second woman-founder case.

## 6. Missing working-class / everyday-life history — verdict: CONCERN

Named leaders are overwhelmingly professional-class (elected officials, appointees, denominational presidents, megachurch pastors, recording artists). The working-class congregant appears only as an aggregate. Lay auxiliaries are described functionally but no lay leader is named; **no oral-history or memoir source is used in any track.** Class tension (Boyd's "dicty old-line churches" vs. storefronts) stays analytic, never grounded in a congregation-level story. **Recommendation:** mine Defender church-news columns and anniversary-book ads (occupations), use Harsh Collection holdings (e.g., the Wyatt papers), and develop track-d's "choir anniversary economy" from cultural observation into a quantified economic case.

## 7. Missing communities — verdict: CONCERN

Entire traditions absent: Presbyterian, Lutheran, Episcopal, CME, AME Zion, Seventh-day Adventist, Jehovah's Witnesses. COGIC — the largest Black Pentecostal body, 8% of 1965 storefront names — gets only an aggregate entity, no named congregation. Catholic coverage is one priest + one parish. Beyond Christianity: only the Nation of Islam. AME is thin (2 congregations) relative to its historic weight. **Recommendation:** add at minimum one named COGIC congregation, one more Black Catholic parish, one mainline congregation; explicitly scope what the flagship does *not* cover.

## 8. Denominational balance — verdict: CONCERN

Of 20 uniquely named congregations: **Baptist 10**, AME 2, UMC 1, UCC 1, Apostolic 2, Pentecostal 1, Spiritualist/MSCC 1, Church of God (Anderson) 1, Congregational 1 (+ NOI 1). Baptists are 50% — and all are large flagships, while small Baptist storefronts (the majority Baptist experience per Noreen's 69%) get nothing. Methodists trail Baptists 10-to-3. Track-c's leader set is more balanced, partially offsetting the skew. **Recommendation:** weight additions toward AME, COGIC/Pentecostal, and Catholic; in synthesis, don't let Baptist-flagship history stand in for "the Black church."

## 9. Geographic bias — verdict: CONCERN

~18 of 20 named congregations are South Side, most in the Bronzeville/Grand Boulevard/Douglas corridor. **West Side: exactly one** (Warren Avenue Congregational, E. Garfield Park — and its building now houses a different church). **North Side: zero. Suburbs: zero.** The geography reproduces the Black Metropolis map and freezes it; the post-1960s westward/southward movement of Black Chicago is institutionally invisible. **Recommendation:** add a West Side module (Austin/North Lawndale/Garfield Park), publish a map of all named congregations showing the clustering, and cover at least one south-suburban congregation.

## 10. Survivorship bias — verdict: CONCERN

**Zero defunct Black congregations are profiled.** The only "lost" institutions are buildings (Pilgrim's burned 1891 edifice; two synagogue predecessors). No track investigates a closure, though track-a admits post-peak histories are thin. Churches lost to Dan Ryan construction, urban renewal, arson, and migration are part of the civic-infrastructure story. **Recommendation:** investigate as named cases — (a) congregations displaced by the Dan Ryan/urban renewal, (b) Pilgrim's status since the 2006 fire, (c) congregations that followed members south/west vs. those that didn't survive, (d) Metropolitan Community Church after the 2003 sale, (e) Deliverance's post-Cobbs era. Add a "lost churches" map layer.

## 11. Weak sources — verdict: CONCERN

- **Piracy:** track-a's Metropolitan Apostolic landmark report via a **zlibrary mirror** — replace with the official chicago.gov PDF.
- **Fan-mediated:** Greater Harvest 1962 liner notes via fan-transcribed YouTube description; Clay Evans founding-1950 via a gospel blog obituary (graded Verified in track-d).
- **Low-authority tertiary carrying Verified grades:** fandom wiki + soulwalking.co.uk (Mahalia Jackson); Alchetron (Wright 1972); answers.com (Obama wedding).
- **Single-source superlatives:** Olivet "city's first kindergarten" (one 2026 pastor interview); Lucy Smith funeral (Wikipedia only); Mother York "first" (church site + one article).
- **Conflict of interest:** Woodlawn Central figures rest on the developer's own site + a Black Enterprise op-ed by the project leader's family member; figures vary across sources ($895M vs $580M; 27 vs 32 acres).
- **Excerpt-only access:** Haggler article (citation unconfirmed), Sernett/Higginbotham via summaries, WBEZ via an Everand listing.
- **Circular-risk chains** (church site → Congressional tribute → church site; Wikipedia ↔ church site) are generally labeled Provisional — correct handling, but map the chains at synthesis.

## 12. Misleading causal claims — verdict: CONCERN

- "Birthplace of Provident Hospital" overstates planning meetings; HABS's "instrumental in founding" is the supportable wording.
- "Bronzeville's churches saved and renovated landmark buildings" — sweeping causal claim, no per-building evidence cited.
- "MLK's only major address in fall 1965" at Fellowship (Provisional, single source); spot-check found documented King Chicago-area addresses in summer 1965, so "only" is load-bearing and unverified — keep Provisional, don't promote.
- Barrow "first woman to lead a major civil rights organization" — qualify or drop; "first woman executive director of Operation PUSH" is the verifiable fact.
- "Largest Protestant congregation in the world" (Olivet) is unverifiable without global comparative data — render as "described at the time as…" or drop.
- Good practice noted: track-e attributes the "ethnic niche" explanation to Boyd as framework, not fact.
