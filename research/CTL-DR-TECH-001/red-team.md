## 1. The 14-point challenge

**1. Straw-man check.** Does the package argue against real claims or easy targets? *Real claims.* The 12 myth cases were taken from live, circulating versions (school materials, BHM lists, viral videos, marketing copy) — including "Henry Sampson invented the cell phone," which Sampson himself denied while alive.

**2. Missing the point.** Could a critic say the package answers the wrong question? *Risk accepted and contained.* The central question is deliberately wide ("invented, adopted, modified, distributed, culturally transformed"). The role taxonomy is the containment: every claim is tagged to a specific role so breadth doesn't become blur.

**3. Evidence quality.** Is evidence primary, secondary, or tertiary? 42 primary sources, 96 secondary. The weakest zones (early web history, AI projections) are graded Provisional/Unresolved rather than padded with tertiary blog claims.

**4. Counter-examples.** What breaks the "Black invention" frame? *Track G.* The package's strongest move is that its myth-busting track reports that beloved claims are false — the frame is "what actually happened," not "Black invention maximalism."

**5. Source laundering.** Do secondary sources just cite each other? *Flagged.* The Sampson cell-phone myth traces to a circular citation web; the red-team traced it to Sampson's own denial. Patent records are the circuit-breaker: a patent number can be checked independently of any journalist's retelling.

**6. Survivorship bias.** Are we only seeing the famous? *Yes — partially.* The inventor track skews famous; the labor track is the corrective (porters, cranemen, keypunch workers, CTA operators). The largest gap in the package is precisely the unfamous: Black engineers inside industrial Chicago firms.

**7. Presentism.** Are modern categories projected backward? *Guarded.* "Cultural transformer" is a modern analytic label; the adaptation case studies anchor it to period mechanisms (time-brokerage, sound-system wiring) rather than vibes.

**8. Correlation vs causation.** Do bias audits prove corporate pullbacks? *No — graded Provisional.* The AI track documents audits and documents pullbacks but does not claim the first caused the second without evidence.

**9. Cherry-picking.** Were inconvenient cases dropped? *No — the method forbids it.* Roger Linn (not Black) is in the graph as an inventor precisely so the 808/909 cultural-transformation story doesn't imply Black invention. Sampson's denial is included, not buried.

**10. Terminology drift.** "Inventor" vs "innovator" vs "adopter" — *audited (see §3).* The package's most repeated error class was exactly this; it was corrected case by case.

**11. Firsts and superlatives.** Any "first Black X" left standing? *Audited.* "First Black website / first person online" claims were dropped for lack of primary evidence. Remaining firsts are sourced to specific records.

**12. Chicago claims.** Is Chicago centrality assumed or evidenced? *Evidenced at the gear level:* the Knuckles TR-909 interview, Marley Marl's workflow, the WVON/WJPC histories, Chess/Universal Recording firsts. The claim is "Chicago was an adoption laboratory," not "everything happened in Chicago."

**13. Rights and media.** Photographs, recordings, advertisements, screenshots are NOT cleared in this package. No media is claimed as cleared. See gaps.md.

**14. Version fragility.** AI claims will age fastest. Gender Shades is dated 2018; Latimer-LLM status is Unresolved as of 2026-09-28. The research-brief timestamps every AI judgment.

## 3. Role-discipline audit

Role definitions (with observable tests) from the adversarial track:

- **inventor** — originates a novel device/process, documented by patent or dated first reduction to practice. TEST: name the patent number/date.
- **engineer** — trained/practicing applier of scientific principles. TEST: credentials, titles, or documented engineering practice.
- **innovator** — improves/recombines existing tech without originating the concept. TEST: identify prior art; specify exactly what changed.
- **operator** — runs/maintains existing tech as skilled labor. TEST: operated rather than designed or owned it?
- **entrepreneur** — organizes capital, labor, distribution. TEST: founded companies, raised capital, built distribution.
- **adopter** — early/significant user whose adoption changes the diffusion path. TEST: can you document use as a turning point?
- **cultural-transformer** — changes how a technology is understood, valued, or used within a culture. TEST: did meaning or social life change, not just mechanics?

Collective-invention cases documented (light bulb, telephone, traffic signal, Internet, gas mask) to block single-inventor attribution in both directions.

**Common miscategorizations corrected:** Madam C.J. Walker (entrepreneur + innovator + cultural-transformer, NOT inventor); Katherine Johnson-style "NASA inventor" framing (adopter/engineer, not inventor); Marley Marl (operator + innovator, not inventor of the sampler); DJ Kool Herc/Grandmaster Flash/Grand Wizard Theodore (operators + innovators + cultural-transformers, not inventors of the turntable); Roger Linn kept as inventor precisely to hold the line.
