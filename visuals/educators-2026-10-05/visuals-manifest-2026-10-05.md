## 5. hook-black-press.png
**Lesson 4 · Who Controls the Story?**
**What it shows:** Ownership is not targeting — two panels: Black-targeted but NOT Black-owned (BET/Paramount Skydance, iHeartMedia, Audacy urban radio) vs. the Black-owned national core (Urban One, Allen Media Group, Essence Ventures). The headline stat: fewer than 180 Black-owned stations of 11,000+ (~1.6%). The policy hinge: built by policy (1978), dismantled by policy (1995–96).
**Classroom use:** Project after reading the paper passage aloud. Point at each column and ask: *"Which column is longer? Does that mean the content in the left column is inauthentic?"* Keep it up during "The Deed Audit" so students can mirror the two-column structure for their own brand picks.
**Source data:** Maxey, *Who Controls the Story?*, October 2026 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23173917), §§3.1–3.2, 3.5; NABOB 2020 station counts.

## 6. hook-black-technology.png
**Lesson 5 · Black Technology**
**What it shows:** The Pullman porter as missing protagonist — 38 air-conditioning systems and 43 berth types committed to memory, by rule book — above the myth-audit verdicts (12 claims: 6 exaggerated, 4 false, 1 unverifiable, 1 confirmed; the Super Soaker as control case). Closes with: *"22 verified patents · 165 years · truth beats myth."*
**Classroom use:** Project during the hook after reading the Dubin passage. Ask: *"Why is the porter, not the inventor, on this card?"* Keep it visible during "Audit a Claim" as the verdict reference.
**Source data:** Maxey, *Black Technology*, October 2026 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23173962), §§3.1–3.3; Dubin, *Trains*, November 1969.

## 7. hook-bronzeville.png
**Lesson 6 · Bronzeville**
**What it shows:** A corridor map of the Black Belt (22nd to 55th): the Stroll on State Street (Binga Bank 1908, the *Defender* 1905, Regal Theater 1928), 47th Street (Club DeLisa, Rhumboogie Cafe, Grand Terrace, Gerri's Palm Tavern), the churches (Quinn Chapel AME, Olivet Baptist, Pilgrim Baptist — birthplace of gospel), and the scholars & poets (Drake & Cayton's *Black Metropolis* 1945, Gwendolyn Brooks). Closes with the migration arc: 44,000 in 1910 → 1,102,620 in 1970.
**Classroom use:** Project during the hook while drawing the Black Belt on the board. During "Build the Block," each group points to its institution's corridor on the card before presenting. The migration stat is the exit-ticket anchor: institutions built at scale, under siege.
**Source data:** CTL-DR-BCI-001 research brief; Black Cultural Knowledge Graph (77 Bronzeville entity records, grades as recorded).

## 1. hook-memory-emergency.png
**Lesson 1 · The Black Memory Emergency**
**What it shows:** The five destroyers of Black memory from CTL-DR-PRESERVE-001 — cataloging backlogs, ownership change, collector mortality, rights uncertainty, platform deletion — each with its paper-verified descriptor.
**Classroom use:** Project during the 10-minute hook, after reading the BET Digital deletion passage aloud. Walk down the five panels; ask students which destroyer surprises them most. Revisit at the end: *"Which of the five threatens your own family's records?"*
**Source data:** Maxey, "The Black Memory Emergency," October 2026 (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23173585), findings 3.1–3.6.

## 2. hook-index-paradox.png
**Lesson 2 · How Do You Rank Culture?**
**What it shows:** The attention paradox from CTL Index Edition 2026-10 (Artifacts): Afro pick — evidence depth 100.0, 106 lookups, score 46.2 (rank 9); Cadillac — 33,922 lookups, evidence 75.0, score 78.8 (rank 1). "Most looked up ≠ most important."
**Classroom use:** Project as the hook. Give students 60 seconds to argue which artifact "deserves" to win — then reveal the methodology's boundary line printed on the card: *"Rankings describe the data. They do not crown winners."* The card doubles as the activity reference sheet during "Build Your Own Index."
**Source data:** CTL Index Edition 2026-10 scored table, `programs/culturetechlens-index/editions/2026-10/index-scores.csv` (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23081630).

## 3. hook-lanes.png
**Lesson 3 · What Makes an Album Great?**
**What it shows:** The Clear Ear Award's lanes system — the eight lanes (R&B, hip-hop, soul, jazz, gospel, house/Afro-house, Afrobeats, Black diaspora) and the 40/30/30 scoring model (critical consensus / cultural impact / CTL evidence depth).
**Classroom use:** Project during the hook after reading the lanes quote aloud. Keep it up during "Build Your Lanes" so groups can mirror the structure: define lanes, set criteria per lane, score, disclose one judgment call.
**Source data:** CultureTechLens, "The Clear Ear Award," 2026-09-30 (culturetechlens.com/music/sound-award/).

## 4. evidence-grades-poster.png
**All lessons · The Honesty System**
**What it shows:** The five CTL evidence grades, one line each — VERIFIED (publication-grade) through UNRESOLVED (recorded as a gap, never an assumption). Closes with: *"Unknowns are never reported as zero."*
**Classroom use:** Print for the wall or project at the start of any lesson that asks students to evaluate sources. Suggested opening question: *"Which of your own sources would earn which grade? Now the harder one — which of your own claims?"* Reusable across the whole kit and future lessons.
**Source data:** CTL-IDX-001 methodology (2026-09-28); CTL-DR-PRESERVE-001 §2.2 (evidence grades).
